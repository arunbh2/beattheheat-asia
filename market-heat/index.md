# Heat in markets and street vending

Where heat comes from in an open market, who it reaches between dawn and dusk, and what helps vendors and porters drink, rest and keep earning, with the evidence for each measure and its limits.

## Why the same market hits six people differently between dawn and dusk

An open market holds many working days at once. A wholesale buyer, a porter, a woman selling vegetables alone, a fish seller, a vendor with a child at the stall and a shopper waiting for a bus all share the same street, and meet its heat at different hours, in different places, with different room to stop.

An illustrative market day

- 06:00 Wholesale buying and loading

The day starts before sunrise with heavy lifting. Porters carry loads paid by the trip, and fish and produce sellers buy ice to keep stock fresh for the day ahead.

- 11:30 The exposed produce row

The sun is overhead. Heat comes from above and bounces up from paving, and shop air conditioners blow hot air into the aisle. A vendor alone has nobody to mind the stall.

- 14:00 The locked or distant toilet block

The toilet is far, paid, locked or unsafe, so some vendors drink less to avoid leaving their goods. The body gets less water in the hottest hours.

- 17:30 The empty aisle and unsold stock

Footfall drops, produce has wilted, and the day's loan repayment or cart rent is still due tomorrow morning.

The same heat lands differently because of where people stand, what they carry, whether they can leave the stall, and what an hour off costs them.

Understand market heat

## Seven things set a vendor's or porter's heat load

A vendor's or porter's heat load is not one number. It builds from the air, the sun, surfaces and machines around them, the work their body does, how long they go without recovering, and how much water they can drink.

- Air temperature

The shade temperature a forecast gives. It is the starting point, and in a market often not the main part of the load.

**Ask:** How hot is it in the shade where people stand?

- Overhead sun and radiant bounce

Direct sun, and heat given back by paving, concrete and metal, can add more to the body than the air does. Light-coloured or reflective paving can make this worse for people standing on it unless there is shade overhead.

**Ask:** What surfaces around the stall feel hot to the hand in the afternoon?

- Air-conditioner exhaust and vehicle heat

Shops that cool their rooms push the heat into the street, and engines idling at the kerb add more.

**Ask:** Where does hot air blow onto stalls?

- Humidity and still, narrow aisles

Sweat cools the body only when it can evaporate. Humid air and aisles closed off by stalls and awnings slow it down.

**Ask:** Does air move along the aisle, or is it trapped?

- Heavy lifting and carrying

Porters and loaders make much of their own heat through effort, often at the hottest pace when trucks arrive.

**Ask:** How heavy are loads, how far are they carried, and how fast?

- Long hours without recovery

Many vendors trade from dawn to dusk, standing or squatting, with nowhere cool to sit and no one to take over.

**Ask:** When in the day does anyone get a real break?

- Drinking less on purpose

Where toilets are far, paid, locked or unsafe, some vendors drink less to avoid needing them. The body then has less water to sweat with in the hottest hours.

**Ask:** Do people drink less because of the toilets?

This page gives no safe temperature or safe working time. Heat stress depends on all seven factors together; measurement, vendors' own reports and local health advice are the guides.

### Exposed produce and fish stall row

**Around 13:30:** The sun is nearly overhead and nets or umbrellas give the least cover; stock wilts and spoils fastest.

**At the edges of the day:** Low sun reaches under canopies from the side; the row is busiest with buyers, so stepping away costs the most sales.

#### Heat sources

- Overhead sun on stalls and sellers
- Heat bouncing up from paving and concrete
- Hot air from neighbouring shop air conditioners
- Still air in narrow aisles

#### Who works or waits here

Vegetable, fruit, flower and fish sellers, many of them women trading alone, some with children.

#### Ask the vendors and porters

- Which stalls sit in full sun, and at what hours?
- Who can mind your stall if you need water or a toilet?
- What spoils first, and when in the day?

#### Measurements that help

- Globe or mean radiant temperature under stalls around 13:00
- Share of stock thrown away at closing
- How often sellers leave the stall to drink

**Depends on:** Who holds the pitch, what the street authority allows overhead, and whether shade keeps aisles and fire access clear.

### Loading bay and porter and cart queue

**Around 13:30:** There are fewer loads but no shade; porters waiting for a turn stand in the open.

**At the edges of the day:** At 06:00 and in the evening the heaviest loads move fast; a porter who stops to drink may lose a paid turn.

#### Heat sources

- Heavy lifting and carrying
- Sun on open loading ground
- Vehicle exhaust and engine heat

#### Who works or waits here

Porters and loaders paid by the load, cart pullers, drivers' helpers; mostly men, many of them migrants.

#### Ask the vendors and porters

- Do you lose your place in the queue if you rest?
- Where is the nearest water and shade from the queue?
- How many loads make a day's pay?

#### Measurements that help

- Loads per hour at different times of day
- Breaks taken without losing a turn
- Reported heat symptoms over a hot week

**Measures to consider:** [Stall-minding buddy rosters and protected porter queue turns](#rec-stall-buddy) · [A shaded place to sit and recover](#rec-shaded-rest) · [Free drinking-water point within a short walk of every row](#rec-water-point)

**Depends on:** Whether porters' associations or the market committee control the queue, and how loads are paid.

### Public toilet and menstrual-hygiene block

**Around 13:30:** The cost of a walk to a far or paid toilet is highest, and so is the pull to drink less.

**At the edges of the day:** At 06:00 and after 18:00 the block may still be locked, though trading has begun or not yet ended.

#### Heat sources

- A hot, airless block
- Distance from stalls in the sun

#### Who works or waits here

Every vendor and porter; women vendors, people who menstruate, pregnant women, older and disabled vendors and transgender vendors most affected by fees, distance, lighting and safety.

#### Ask the vendors and porters

- Is the toilet open, free, clean and safe for the whole trading day?
- Is there water, soap, a lock and a bin for menstrual waste?
- Do you drink less so you need it less?

#### Measurements that help

- Days and hours open during trading
- Water, soap and lighting working on a spot check
- How much vendors drink on a hot day

**Measures to consider:** [Free, clean, women-safe market toilets with water and menstrual hygiene](#rec-market-toilets) · [Stall-minding buddy rosters and protected porter queue turns](#rec-stall-buddy)

**Depends on:** Who owns and pays for cleaning, and whether opening hours match trading hours.

### Drinking-water and ice refill point

**Around 13:30:** Demand peaks; a dry or warm tap is felt most now.

**At the edges of the day:** At 06:00 ice is bought for the day's stock; its price sets how much sellers can keep cool.

#### Heat sources

- Sun on taps and containers that warms the water
- Queues in the open

#### Who works or waits here

Vendors, porters, shoppers and passers-by; fish and produce sellers buying ice.

#### Ask the vendors and porters

- Is free, safe drinking water within a short walk of every row?
- Who refills and cleans it, and is that paid?
- Has the price of ice changed this season?

#### Measurements that help

- Minutes walk from the furthest stall
- Days the point was dry
- Ice cost as a share of a day's sales

**Measures to consider:** [Free drinking-water point within a short walk of every row](#rec-water-point) · [Shared produce cooling, fair-price ice and heat-triggered cash for vendors](#rec-spoilage-ice)

**Depends on:** Water supply, a named person paid to keep it clean and working, and a way to report when it fails.

### Bus, auto-rickshaw and delivery waiting edge

**Around 13:30:** Few people wait, but those who do have no shade.

**At the edges of the day:** Shoppers, children and vendors heading home crowd the kerb in low sun.

#### Heat sources

- Exposed kerb with no shelter
- Vehicle exhaust and engine heat
- Hot paving and road

#### Who works or waits here

Shoppers with children, older people, vendors arriving and leaving, delivery riders.

#### Ask the vendors and porters

- Where do people wait, and for how long?
- Is there shade or a seat?
- Who comes on foot from far away?

#### Measurements that help

- People waiting at peak hours
- Shade over the waiting area through the day

**Measures to consider:** [Vendor-co-designed, storm-proof shared shade with pitch protection](#rec-vendor-shade) · [Trees for long-term shade at the market's edges](#rec-street-trees) · [Two-way market heat boards and trusted vendor messengers](#rec-market-messengers)

**Depends on:** The road authority and transport operators, as well as the market.

### Nearby storage room, rest bench and crèche room

**Around 13:30:** A cool room could serve as rest and child care, if it is open and someone minds the stalls.

**At the edges of the day:** It is a store for goods and carts.

#### Heat sources

- A tin roof over a small room
- Little air movement
- Power cuts that stop fans

#### Who works or waits here

Vendors storing goods overnight, parents with young children, older vendors needing to sit.

#### Ask the vendors and porters

- Is there anywhere cool to sit for ten minutes?
- Where are children during school closures?
- Who holds the key?

#### Measurements that help

- Room temperature in the afternoon
- Use by vendors and children on hot days

**Measures to consider:** [A shaded place to sit and recover](#rec-shaded-rest) · [A cool, supervised corner for children near the market](#rec-shaded-creche)

**Depends on:** Who owns the room, whether it can be cooled cheaply, and paid staff if children are cared for.

Health and equity

## What heat does in markets, and why it is unequal

Heat harms the body the same way everywhere. Who it harms most in a market depends on how the market is run, who holds space in it, and who looks after whom.

Physiology and microclimate

Heat load, dehydration, strain on the heart and kidneys, heat exhaustion and heat stroke, and the sun, surfaces and air that drive them.

Market governance, space and care

Toilets, stall security, queue rules, licences, eviction risk, debt, childcare and who sits on market committees: the arrangements that decide whether people can drink, rest and stop.

- Toilets as heat-health infrastructure

Where toilets are poor or missing, people drink less, and that can strain the urinary tract and kidneys as well as add to heat strain.

- Stall security and queue economics

Leaving a stall can cost goods or sales; stepping out of a porter queue can cost a paid load. A break has a price before anyone says no.

- Spoilage and the heat credit squeeze

Fewer customers and spoiled stock fall on the same day as a loan repayment or cart rent, so heat can turn a bad day into debt.

- Menstruation, pregnancy and breastfeeding in open public space

Privacy, clean water, a place to change, sit or feed a baby, and a way to rest without losing a pitch decide whether these are manageable in heat.

- Childcare and school closures

When heat closes schools, children may come to hot stalls, or a parent, often a mother, loses a day's trade.

- Licences, tenure and eviction during upgrades

Street improvements, shade projects and greening can push informal, migrant, women and transgender vendors off their pitches when their space is not protected first.

- Vendors left out of warnings and services

Deaf and disabled vendors, migrants, people who do not read the local language and people without smartphones are easily missed by alerts, signs and services.

- Who sits on market committees

Women vendors are often absent from the market boards and municipal meetings where shade, toilets and water are decided.

Protection without eviction or lost turns

Beautification that clears informal stalls, shade that blocks fire lanes or footfall, or a cooling centre that leaves a vendor to abandon goods or a porter to lose a paid load is not protection.

## The intervention library

10 measures in 6 families, each with what it addresses, how it works, when it fits, what it needs, who runs it, and the kind of evidence behind it. Where this page has no checked source for a measure, the record says so and is marked a proposal requiring validation.

What the evidence types mean

**Occupational guidance, written for workplaces**: What occupational agencies recommend from accumulated experience. It was written for employers and workplaces; a market has no single employer, so who could take each step differs.

**Study of the harm, not of this measure**: A study that measured the problem this measure addresses. It shows why the measure matters, not that the measure works.

**Measured temperature, not health or earnings**: A field or experimental measurement of temperature under shade or on surfaces. It shows a physical effect where it was measured; effects on health, rest or earnings were not measured.

**Field report from markets**: An account of what was done in a market or vending setting, by the people who did it or reported on it. None here is an independent evaluation.

**Proposal requiring validation**: Common Shade's reading of what could help, built from the problems documented on this page. Nobody has yet tested it in a market in the sources checked.

### Shade, microclimate and storm-proof canopies

Cut direct sun and radiant bounce where people trade, load and wait.

#### Vendor-co-designed, storm-proof shared shade with pitch protection

Measured temperature, not health or earnings Field report from markets

**Addresses:** Direct sun and radiant heat over stalls and aisles, shade that arrives late or tears in pre-monsoon winds, and eviction during street upgrades.

How it works, when it fits, and its evidence

**How it works**: Anchored, wind-rated shade over rows of stalls or single carts, put up before the hot season, with written protection so that vendors keep their pitches after the work.

**Market types and settings**: Open street rows, weekly markets and vending zones with no tree cover or building overhang.

**Works if**: Up before the heat begins, not in the middle of it; Anchored for pre-monsoon gusts, with headroom for air to move and clear fire access; Designed with licensed and unlicensed vendors, who keep their places

**Take care**: Reflective coatings on paving without shade overhead can add radiant heat to people standing on them; Late, unanchored nets can tear or collapse in strong winds; Designs that clear hawkers to make room protect no one who traded there

**Upfront**: From a cart umbrella to engineered canopies over a row; local costing needed.

**Running**: Checks before each season, storm-anchor inspection and fabric replacement every few seasons.

**Who installs, runs and maintains it**: City and local governments, vendor associations and community groups.

**Vendors' and porters' part**: Vendors choose where shade goes and how it is anchored, and agree how pitches are kept.

**Equity and access**: Cuts radiant heat for the people standing longest in the sun, if no one loses a pitch to make room.

**Evidence**:
- **Measured temperature, not health or earnings.** In a small trial in a university car park in Pathum Thani, Thailand, artificial canopy materials of the kind used over market stalls lowered air temperature by 1.6-2.3 °C, and dense tree clusters by up to 2.3-3 °C. It measured air temperature, not a working market or anyone's health. Across 20 shaded and sunlit street sites in Singapore on one very hot afternoon, shade lowered mean radiant temperature by about 10 °C on average, far more than air temperature. [Sustainability (MDPI), 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/8/4653); [City University of Hong Kong (research output), 2025](https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/harnessing-street-shade-to-mitigate-heat-stress-an-in-situ-parall/)
- **Field report from markets.** In Nagpur in May 2026, temporary green nets over busy junctions kept collapsing in strong winds; commuters and officials asked for durable structures and a long-term heat action plan instead of seasonal fixes. [The Hitavada, 2026](https://www.thehitavada.com//Encyc/2026/5/11/green-nets-at-city-squares-relief-measure-or-another-half-planned-half-planned-civic-experiment.html)
- Shade lowers measured temperatures; no evaluation of market shade on vendors' health or earnings was found in the sources checked.

**Can people actually use this?**: Not assessed yet for this measure. The questions are on [Heat, health and the ability to act](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-health-and-equity/#taxonomy).

**Measure before and after**: Mean radiant temperature under stalls around 13:00; canopies still standing after pre-monsoon storms; vendors on the same pitches a year later.

**Cycles it touches**: [The heat credit squeeze](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#squeeze) · [The green-displacement loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#greendisplace) · [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#poverty)

**Pairs with**: [Trees for long-term shade at the market's edges](#rec-street-trees) · [Free drinking-water point within a short walk of every row](#rec-water-point)

#### Trees for long-term shade at the market's edges

Measured temperature, not health or earnings

**Addresses:** Shade that lasts for decades over waiting areas, rest points and the market's edges.

How it works, when it fits, and its evidence

**How it works**: Trees planted where they have water and room to grow, alongside shade people can use this season while the trees grow.

**Market types and settings**: Market edges, bus stops and rest points with space for roots and canopy.

**Works if**: Water and growing space secured before planting; Someone paid to care for the trees; Planting planned so that it does not push vendors or residents out

**Take care**: A young tree gives little shade for years; Greening that raises rents or clears stalls displaces the people it was meant to cool

**Upfront**: Saplings, pits, guards and watering; local costing needed.

**Running**: Watering and care for several years.

**Who installs, runs and maintains it**: City and local governments, with vendor associations and residents.

**Vendors' and porters' part**: Vendors and residents help decide where trees go.

**Equity and access**: Long-term shade for everyone at the edge, if greening does not displace them.

**Evidence**:
- **Measured temperature, not health or earnings.** In a small trial in a university car park in Pathum Thani, Thailand, artificial canopy materials of the kind used over market stalls lowered air temperature by 1.6-2.3 °C, and dense tree clusters by up to 2.3-3 °C. It measured air temperature, not a working market or anyone's health. [Sustainability (MDPI), 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/8/4653)
- Measured cooling under tree clusters comes from one small trial, not a working market.

**Can people actually use this?**: Not assessed yet for this measure. The questions are on [Heat, health and the ability to act](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-health-and-equity/#taxonomy).

**Measure before and after**: Shade cover over waiting and rest areas through the day; tree survival after three years.

**Cycles it touches**: [The green-displacement loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#greendisplace)

### Water, sanitation and menstrual hygiene

Free drinking water and clean, safe toilets treated as heat infrastructure.

#### Free, clean, women-safe market toilets with water and menstrual hygiene

Study of the harm, not of this measure

**Addresses:** Vendors, especially women, drinking less in hot shifts because toilets are far, locked, paid, dirty or unsafe.

How it works, when it fits, and its evidence

**How it works**: Free, maintained, well-lit, separate and wheelchair-accessible toilets with running water, soap, locks and menstrual-waste bins, treated as part of the city's heat plan, inside or beside markets.

**Market types and settings**: Open street markets, weekly haats, bus-stand vending zones and wholesale mandis.

**Works if**: No fee for vendors, at least during heat alerts; A named, paid caretaker cleans and stocks water every day; Open from early-morning to evening trading

**Take care**: A locked or dry block can be counted as delivered while vendors still drink less; Safe access for transgender and disabled vendors needs planning, not assumption

**Upfront**: Depends on whether an existing block is repaired or a new one built; local costing needed.

**Running**: Caretaker wages, cleaning supplies, water and lighting.

**Who installs, runs and maintains it**: City and local governments, water and sanitation authorities, and market committees with women vendors' oversight.

**Vendors' and porters' part**: Women vendors help set opening hours and check that the block works.

**Equity and access**: Removes the reason many women vendors drink less, which matters most in the hottest hours.

**Evidence**:
- **Study of the harm, not of this measure.** In a study of Indian women workers across several kinds of work, 87% of those with poor or no toilets at work reported genitourinary problems, and about 1 in 10 showed heat strain and moderate dehydration. [Global Health Action, 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5025522/)
- A study links poor toilets at work to genitourinary problems among women workers; no evaluation of a market toilet upgrade on heat illness was found in the sources checked.

**Can people actually use this?**:
**Who controls access**: Every vendor, porter and shopper, whatever their licence status.

**What using it costs in money, time or earnings**: Free to use, at least for vendors; any fee falls hardest on the lowest earners.

**Who does the additional work**: Daily cleaning and restocking.

**Is that work paid and supported**: Paid when a caretaker is employed; unpaid if left to vendors.

**Who might be left out**: Transgender and disabled vendors if access is not planned; anyone when it is locked during trading.

**What personal information it needs**: None.

**Can the people it serves shape it**: Through vendor associations and women vendors on the market committee.

**Who is accountable if it fails**: The city or utility that owns the block, and the market committee.

**What to check locally**: Whether it is open, clean and watered through the whole trading day.

**Measure before and after**: Days and hours the block is open, clean and watered during trading; how much vendors drink; reported genitourinary and heat symptoms.

**Cycles it touches**: [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#poverty) · [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#clinic)

**Pairs with**: [Free drinking-water point within a short walk of every row](#rec-water-point) · [Stall-minding buddy rosters and protected porter queue turns](#rec-stall-buddy)

#### Free drinking-water point within a short walk of every row

Proposal requiring validation

**Addresses:** Vendors leaving their stalls, or spending their earnings, to drink.

How it works, when it fits, and its evidence

**How it works**: A tap stand, refill point or cooler topped up through the day, close to where people trade, in shade so the water stays cool.

**Market types and settings**: Every market row, loading bay and waiting edge.

**Works if**: Someone paid to keep it supplied, clean and tested; Fixed quickly when it breaks, with a way to report it; Free or very cheap, and reachable by people using wheelchairs

**Take care**: Water left in the sun warms up and is drunk less; A point at one end of a long market helps only the stalls near it

**Upfront**: Low to moderate, depending on supply; local costing needed.

**Running**: Water, cleaning and testing.

**Who installs, runs and maintains it**: Water utilities, city governments and market committees.

**Vendors' and porters' part**: Vendors say where it is needed and report when it fails.

**Equity and access**: Helps solo vendors and porters most, who cannot leave to find water.

**Evidence**:
- **Proposal requiring validation.** No evaluation of market water points on vendors' heat illness was found in the sources checked.

**Can people actually use this?**: Not assessed yet for this measure. The questions are on [Heat, health and the ability to act](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-health-and-equity/#taxonomy).

**Measure before and after**: Minutes walk from the furthest stall; days the point was dry; how much vendors drink on hot days.

**Cycles it touches**: [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#poverty)

**Pairs with**: [Free, clean, women-safe market toilets with water and menstrual hygiene](#rec-market-toilets) · [Stall-minding buddy rosters and protected porter queue turns](#rec-stall-buddy)

### Stall-minding, rest and porter-queue arrangements

Make it possible to drink, use a toilet and rest without losing goods or a paid turn.

#### Stall-minding buddy rosters and protected porter queue turns

Occupational guidance, written for workplaces

**Addresses:** Vendors who cannot leave goods unguarded to drink, cool down or use a toilet, and porters who lose a paid load if they step out of the queue.

How it works, when it fits, and its evidence

**How it works**: Neighbouring stallholders agree a short-break rotation, of 10 to 15 minutes, to watch each other's stock in the hottest hours; porters' and loaders' associations hold a worker's place in the queue during agreed water and shade breaks.

**Market types and settings**: Dense street rows, solo women-run vegetable and snack stalls, and wholesale loading bays.

**Works if**: Organised through vendor or porter associations people trust; Water and a safe toilet close enough to reach and return within the break; Newcomers and migrant hawkers included, not only people with ties on the row

**Take care**: Informal arrangements can leave out people without friends on the row; Minding stalls is work: if it falls on the same people every day, it becomes an unpaid load; It adds to water, toilets and shade; it does not replace them

**Upfront**: No equipment to buy. Time and coordination: local costing needed.

**Running**: Not assessed: local costing needed.

**Who installs, runs and maintains it**: Vendor associations, women's collectives, porters' unions and market committees.

**Vendors' and porters' part**: Vendors and porters agree the pairs, the times and the queue rule themselves.

**Equity and access**: Removes the daily choice between guarding stock or income and drinking or using a toilet, especially for women trading alone.

**Evidence**:
- **Occupational guidance, written for workplaces.** Occupational heat guidance describes workers watching one another for signs of heat illness as part of protection in hot work; it was written for workplaces, not markets. [Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety, 2024](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/phys_agents/heat_control.html)
- Workplace guidance describes workers watching one another in heat; stall-minding rosters and protected queue turns have not been evaluated in markets in the sources checked.

**Can people actually use this?**:
**Who controls access**: Every vendor and porter on the row or in the queue, including newcomers and migrants.

**What using it costs in money, time or earnings**: No money; time spent minding a neighbour's stall.

**Who does the additional work**: Minding stalls and holding queue places.

**Is that work paid and supported**: Unpaid unless an association pays row leaders or stewards.

**Who might be left out**: Vendors without ties on the row, and porters outside the association that controls the queue.

**What personal information it needs**: None.

**Can the people it serves shape it**: Vendors and porters set the rules themselves.

**Who is accountable if it fails**: The association or market committee that agreed it.

**What to check locally**: Who gets breaks, who does the minding, and whether anyone lost a turn.

**Measure before and after**: Breaks taken per shift by solo vendors; missed porter turns; self-reported heat symptoms.

**Cycles it touches**: [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#poverty) · [The heat credit squeeze](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#squeeze)

**Pairs with**: [Free drinking-water point within a short walk of every row](#rec-water-point) · [Free, clean, women-safe market toilets with water and menstrual hygiene](#rec-market-toilets) · [A shaded place to sit and recover](#rec-shaded-rest)

#### A shaded place to sit and recover

Proposal requiring validation

**Addresses:** Nowhere cool to pause in the hottest hours.

How it works, when it fits, and its evidence

**How it works**: A shaded bench or rest point with water nearby, serving vendors, porters and shoppers, including older people and people with children.

**Market types and settings**: Beside loading bays, at the market's edge or in a nearby public room.

**Works if**: Close enough to the stalls and queue to use in a short break; Paired with stall-minding and protected queue turns, so resting costs nothing

**Take care**: A rest place helps only if people can afford to stop: porters paid by the load lose money when they rest

**Upfront**: Low: shade and seating; local costing needed.

**Running**: Cleaning and upkeep.

**Who installs, runs and maintains it**: Market committees and city governments.

**Vendors' and porters' part**: Vendors and porters say where it would be used.

**Equity and access**: Older, pregnant and disabled vendors and porters gain most from a place to sit.

**Evidence**:
- **Proposal requiring validation.** No evaluation of market rest points was found in the sources checked.

**Can people actually use this?**: Not assessed yet for this measure. The questions are on [Heat, health and the ability to act](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-health-and-equity/#taxonomy).

**Measure before and after**: Use of the rest point on hot afternoons; breaks taken by porters.

**Cycles it touches**: [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#poverty)

### Produce cooling, ice and livelihood protection

Slow spoilage and cushion earnings when heat empties the aisles.

#### Shared produce cooling, fair-price ice and heat-triggered cash for vendors

Field report from markets

**Addresses:** Midday spoilage of vegetables, flowers and fish, rising ice costs, and falling footfall that pushes vendors into debt.

How it works, when it fits, and its evidence

**How it works**: Shaded evaporative produce chambers in dry heat, or a negotiated fair price for ice in fish and humid markets, together with cash that arrives when heat stops trading, paid by mobile money without needing a formal shop lease.

**Market types and settings**: Produce, flower and fish markets where unsold stock at the end of the day is a direct loss.

**Works if**: The cooling method suits the humidity: evaporative chambers work in dry heat, not in monsoon humidity; Cash arrives within days, not weeks, and reaches vendors without formal papers

**Take care**: Shared cold storage can price out the smallest sellers through daily fees; Who controls communal storage decides who benefits

**Upfront**: Depends on the measure; a negotiated ice price needs coordination rather than money.

**Running**: Upkeep of chambers, ice supply, or the cost of a cash or insurance scheme.

**Who installs, runs and maintains it**: Market committees, fisheries and agriculture agencies, cooperatives, insurers and city governments.

**Vendors' and porters' part**: Vendors help set prices, storage rules and how cash is triggered.

**Equity and access**: Protects the smallest traders, who borrow each morning to buy stock, from heat turning into debt.

**Evidence**:
- **Field report from markets.** A 2024 report by Greenpeace India and the National Hawkers Federation recorded the experiences of over 700 street vendors through one of India's worst heat waves, and the toll it took on their livelihoods. [Greenpeace India and National Hawkers Federation, 2024](https://www.greenpeace.org/india/en/press/16737/new-report-by-greenpeace-india-and-national-hawkers-federation-reveals-harsh-heatwave-toll-on-street-vendors/)
- The survey shows the loss, not a remedy; no evaluation of combined cooling and cash packages on vendors' incomes was found in the sources checked. An ice-price agreement for fish traders in Sri Lanka (2023) is described elsewhere on the site but is not cited here, because its original notice is no longer online to check.

**Can people actually use this?**: Not assessed yet for this measure. The questions are on [Heat, health and the ability to act](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-health-and-equity/#taxonomy).

**Measure before and after**: Share of stock spoiled before closing; ice cost as a share of daily sales; missed loan repayments in hot months.

**Cycles it touches**: [The heat credit squeeze](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#squeeze) · [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#poverty)

**Pairs with**: [Vendor-co-designed, storm-proof shared shade with pitch protection](#rec-vendor-shade)

### Women's safety, childcare and inclusive access

Make cooling spaces, child care and warnings reach women, disabled, migrant and transgender vendors.

#### A cool, supervised corner for children near the market

Study of the harm, not of this measure

**Addresses:** Children brought to hot stalls, or a day's trade lost, when heat closes schools.

How it works, when it fits, and its evidence

**How it works**: A cooled, safe, supervised corner for young children in a market cooling room, community hall or anganwadi nearby, open during trading hours and on school heat-closure days.

**Market types and settings**: Neighbourhood markets and vending clusters where parents, mostly women, trade with young children.

**Works if**: Paid, trusted local caregivers, not unpaid volunteers; Open during real market hours; Free, with no employer or address papers needed

**Take care**: Drinking water, oral rehydration salts, clean mats and backup power for fans keep the room from overheating in afternoon power cuts; Child care with no paid staff becomes one more unpaid job for women

**Upfront**: Cooling, mats and fans in an existing public room; local costing needed.

**Running**: Caregivers' pay, water and electricity in alert weeks.

**Who installs, runs and maintains it**: City and local governments, women's collectives, NGOs and market associations.

**Vendors' and porters' part**: Parents help set hours and choose caregivers.

**Equity and access**: Keeps young children out of direct heat while parents keep earning, and older siblings in school.

**Evidence**:
- **Study of the harm, not of this measure.** Heatwaves disrupted schooling for an estimated 171 million students in 2024; South Asia was the most affected region. [UNICEF, 2025](https://www.unicef.org/reports/learning-interrupted-global-snapshot-2024)
- School heat closures are documented at scale; market-linked crèches have not been evaluated in the sources checked.

**Can people actually use this?**: Not assessed yet for this measure. The questions are on [Heat, health and the ability to act](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-health-and-equity/#taxonomy).

**Measure before and after**: Children attending on alert days; parents' trading hours kept; children's heat-symptom visits.

**Cycles it touches**: [The lost-schooling loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#school) · [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#poverty)

### Market governance, tenure protection and two-way alerts

A seat for vendors in design, protection for pitches, and alerts with real services behind them.

#### Two-way market heat boards and trusted vendor messengers

Field report from markets

**Addresses:** Alerts that are app-only or text-heavy, missing vendors who cannot read them, are deaf, speak another language or have no smartphone, and advice with no service behind it.

How it works, when it fits, and its evidence

**How it works**: Colour-coded heat boards at market entrances and on carts, with voice-note alerts in local languages sent to elected vendor leaders, who pass them along the row and report back missing water, broken fans or emergencies.

**Market types and settings**: Any open market, labour chowk or multilingual mandi.

**Works if**: Every alert names a water point, toilet or cool space that is open; A city or clinic contact acts on what vendors report

**Take care**: 'Stay indoors' advice does not fit people who have to trade to eat; Boards that rely on reading or hearing miss deaf and non-literate vendors unless they use colour and pictures

**Upfront**: Low: weatherproof boards and a messaging group.

**Running**: Phone credit or small stipends for messengers on alert days, and the city's follow-through.

**Who installs, runs and maintains it**: Weather services, vendor unions, community groups and municipal heat cells.

**Vendors' and porters' part**: Vendors elect the messengers and use the channel to report problems.

**Equity and access**: Reaches vendors without smartphones, reading or the local language, and gives them a way to be heard.

**Evidence**:
- **Field report from markets.** In Delhi in 2025, the India Meteorological Department, worker organisations and Greenpeace India set up colour-coded heat boards at vending carts and sent bilingual audio and text alerts on WhatsApp to worker leaders, who passed them on. [Greenpeace India, 2025](https://www.greenpeace.org/india/en/story/18953/beyond-the-forecast-inside-indias-first-community-led-early-warning-system-for-extreme-weather-for-informal-workers/)
- Described as set up in Delhi; its effect on health or behaviour has not been evaluated in the sources checked.

**Can people actually use this?**: Not assessed yet for this measure. The questions are on [Heat, health and the ability to act](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-health-and-equity/#taxonomy).

**Measure before and after**: Share of vendors on the row who know the day's alert level; time to fix a reported broken water point or toilet.

**Cycles it touches**: [The invisible-deaths loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#invisible) · [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#clinic)

#### Pitch protection and vendors on the committee before any upgrade

Proposal requiring validation

**Addresses:** Shade, greening or street works that push informal, migrant, women and transgender vendors off their pitches, and decisions made without them.

How it works, when it fits, and its evidence

**How it works**: Before works begin, licensed and unlicensed vendors are listed, their places guaranteed in writing, and women vendors given seats on the committee that designs the upgrade.

**Market types and settings**: Any market or vending street facing an upgrade, cooling project or beautification scheme.

**Works if**: The list includes unlicensed and migrant vendors; Women vendors have a vote, not only a consultation

**Take care**: A consultation after the design is fixed changes little

**Upfront**: Coordination time.

**Running**: Committee time.

**Who installs, runs and maintains it**: City governments, street-vending committees and vendor associations.

**Vendors' and porters' part**: Vendors sit on the committee that decides.

**Equity and access**: Keeps the people a cooling project was meant for in the place it cools.

**Evidence**:
- **Proposal requiring validation.** Common Shade's reading of the displacement risk; no evaluation of pitch protection in heat projects was found in the sources checked.

**Can people actually use this?**: Not assessed yet for this measure. The questions are on [Heat, health and the ability to act](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-health-and-equity/#taxonomy).

**Measure before and after**: Vendors on the same pitches a year after the works; women vendors on the committee.

## Four worked examples

Each follows the same path: what was observed, what is still unknown, candidate measures, the arrangements among the market, the city and vendors' associations that make them usable, and how to check they worked.

### A women-majority vegetable market with no safe toilets and a sharp afternoon drop-off

Illustrative example

- **Observed problem**Most sellers are women trading alone. The nearest toilet is paid and closes at 18:00; several sellers say they drink little until they get home. Many leave by early afternoon.

- **Missing information**Whether sellers leave early because of heat, the toilet, low footfall or care at home; how much they drink; whether the block has water.

- **Candidate measures**[Free, clean, women-safe market toilets with water and menstrual hygiene](#rec-market-toilets) · [Free drinking-water point within a short walk of every row](#rec-water-point) · [Stall-minding buddy rosters and protected porter queue turns](#rec-stall-buddy) · [Vendor-co-designed, storm-proof shared shade with pitch protection](#rec-vendor-shade)

- **Market, city and association arrangements**The city waives the fee for vendors and pays a caretaker; women vendors set hours with the market committee; neighbours agree a stall-minding rota for the hottest hours.

- **Verification plan**Hours the block is open and watered; how much sellers drink on hot days; how late they stay; reported symptoms over a hot month.

### A wholesale loading yard where porters skip water and shade to keep their turn

Illustrative example

- **Observed problem**Porters paid by the load queue in the open from 05:00. Anyone who steps away loses their place.

- **Missing information**How the queue is run and by whom; loads per hour through the day; where water and shade could go.

- **Candidate measures**[Stall-minding buddy rosters and protected porter queue turns](#rec-stall-buddy) · [A shaded place to sit and recover](#rec-shaded-rest) · [Free drinking-water point within a short walk of every row](#rec-water-point)

- **Market, city and association arrangements**The porters' association agrees that a place is held during a water break; the market committee puts shade and water beside the queue.

- **Verification plan**Breaks taken without losing a turn; loads per day before and after; reported heat symptoms.

### A coastal fish and produce market where midday spoilage and ice prices wipe out margins

Illustrative example

- **Observed problem**Fish sellers buy ice each morning; in the hottest weeks prices rise and footfall falls. Some miss loan repayments.

- **Missing information**Ice cost as a share of sales; how much stock is thrown away; who controls cold storage; whether a cash scheme could reach sellers without papers.

- **Candidate measures**[Shared produce cooling, fair-price ice and heat-triggered cash for vendors](#rec-spoilage-ice) · [Vendor-co-designed, storm-proof shared shade with pitch protection](#rec-vendor-shade)

- **Market, city and association arrangements**The fisheries agency and the market committee bring traders and ice suppliers together on price; a heat-triggered cash scheme is explored with sellers.

- **Verification plan**Ice cost and spoilage before and after; missed repayments in hot months.

### A 'cool street' upgrade where unlicensed vendors risk displacement

Illustrative example

- **Observed problem**The city plans trees, shade and new paving on a busy vending street. Licensed vendors were consulted; unlicensed vendors were not counted.

- **Missing information**Who trades there and when, including unlicensed and migrant vendors; whether the paving is reflective; where vendors go during works.

- **Candidate measures**[Pitch protection and vendors on the committee before any upgrade](#rec-pitch-protection) · [Vendor-co-designed, storm-proof shared shade with pitch protection](#rec-vendor-shade) · [Trees for long-term shade at the market's edges](#rec-street-trees)

- **Market, city and association arrangements**Every vendor is listed and their place guaranteed before works; women vendors sit on the design committee; reflective paving is paired with shade.

- **Verification plan**Vendors on the same pitches a year later; radiant temperature at stalls before and after.

What is not known

## Evidence gaps, stated plainly

- Few independent evaluations of market shade on vendors' health or earnings: the measurements found are of temperature.
- Little measured evidence that sanitation upgrades in Asian markets reduce heat-linked dehydration or urinary problems.
- Stall-minding rosters, protected queue turns and market crèches are described or proposed, not evaluated.
- Heat alerts for vendors are documented as set up; their effect on what vendors do, or on illness, is not yet measured.
- Little is known about heat among deaf, disabled, migrant and transgender vendors in particular.
