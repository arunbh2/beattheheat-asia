# Walk the market together: a heat check for street vendors, porters and market teams

Market, street or ward: ______________________ · Date and time: ____________ · Walked with: ______________________

Walk the market in the hottest trading hours, around 12:00 to 15:00, with women vendors, porters and market staff. Note what people face, what stands in the way of a fix, and three steps to agree before the next hot spell.

## 1. Six quick checks across the market

What to look at together | Questions to ask vendors and porters | What we saw or heard | What gets in the way (fees, locks, wind, rent, eviction fear) |

Shade and surfaces | Which stalls and queues sit in full sun? Is hot paving or shop air-conditioner exhaust heating the aisle? Will canopies survive pre-monsoon wind? | | |
Drinking water | Is free, safe, cool drinking water within a two-minute walk of every row? Who refills and cleans it every day? | | |
Toilets and hygiene | Are clean, safe, lit toilets open and free through all trading hours, with water, locks and menstrual-waste bins? Do vendors drink less to avoid them? | | |
Stall-minding and queues | Can a vendor alone leave the stall for 15 minutes without losing goods? Can a porter rest without losing a paid load? | | |
Stock, ice and income | What spoils fastest by midday? Has the price of ice risen? Which vendors lose most when footfall drops? | | |
Children and access | Are children sitting at hot stalls? Can deaf, disabled, migrant and transgender vendors reach warnings, water and rest safely? | | |

## 2. Protection check before any upgrade starts

- No eviction or pitch loss: licensed and unlicensed vendors have their space guaranteed before any shade, tree planting or street work begins
- No unpaid burden: upkeep (water, toilet cleaning, child care) is paid work, not left to women vendors for free
- Ready before the heat: shade anchors, taps and boards are checked before the season starts
- Two-way alerts: every heat alert names an open water point, rest space and clinic contact, and who fixes them when they break
