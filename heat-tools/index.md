# Which heat tool fits the decision in front of you?

Find tools for warnings, risk assessment, cooling and protection planning. Compare what they need, what they produce, and where their limits matter.

A navigation and comparison service. Inclusion is not endorsement or certification. Check local applicability, current source information and professional requirements before relying on a tool. It is not a weather feed, a risk score or a complete list, and Common Shade has not run any of these tools itself.

## Three illustrative workflows

For a practitioner, the useful answer is often a combination: regional context, an official warning or local measurement, then institutional action. These are illustrative workflows, not validated protocols, automated recommendations or endorsed procedures, and no partnership with any provider is implied.

Illustrative workflow, not an official recommendation

### An Indian city preparing its heat plan

- [CEEW District Heat Risk Assessment](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-tools/ceew/)

**Why:** Establish district context; commission finer local assessment where needed.

**What it leaves open:** District ranking does not measure street-level or individual risk. Climate and social inputs have different reference years.

- [IMD Heat Wave Guidance](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-tools/imd/)

**Why:** Consult current official warnings and local activation rules.

**What it leaves open:** Check issue and validity times at source. Experimental heat index is not a WBGT measurement or workplace safety clearance.

- [WHO Heat–Health Action Plans Guidance (2026)](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-tools/who_hhap/) + [Common Shade: Intersections](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-tools/cs_intersections/)

**Why:** Review planning responsibilities and access barriers.

**What it leaves open:** Developed for WHO Europe and adaptable elsewhere; local law, climate and institutional roles need checking. Not a validated vulnerability score or a prediction of outcomes in a real street.

- [Common Shade: Plan a response](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-tools/cs_plan/)

**Why:** Record agreed actions, payers, triggers and follow-up.

**What it leaves open:** Named roles are proposals until agreed; completion does not establish funding or readiness.

## How this catalogue works

It lists 16 outside resources and 4 Common Shade pages, researched up to 2026-11-10. That number describes this starter catalogue only, not the size of the field or how well heat protection works.

**Public description reviewed**: The provider's public description was read.

**Selected methodology documentation reviewed**: Parts of the method documentation were read.

**Official indexed description reviewed; direct verification incomplete**: The official description was found in indexed text, but direct access was incomplete. That does not mean the service is broken.

Reviewed never means validated or certified, and none of these tools was run by Common Shade. "Not verified" means nobody has checked it yet, which is different from "no". Free to read is not an open-data licence, and a listed API is not a connection Common Shade has tried. Global coverage is not validation in an Asian city, and different heat measures (air temperature, heat index, WBGT, UTCI) are never interchangeable. Live warnings stay with the official agencies that issue them.

Records are reviewed monthly, with a broader search each quarter; a record older than 90 days is flagged for recheck and kept, not deleted. Know of a tool we have missed, or a detail that has changed? [Tell us, with its source](https://beattheheat.asia/join/?about=Heat%20Tool%20Finder#way-share): suggestions are checked before they appear. The catalogue is open as [one JSON file](https://beattheheat.asia/data/heat-tools/catalogue.json).

Inspired by UNEP FI's approach to making risk tools easier to compare. Independently developed by Common Shade.
