    Understand heat

    El Niño and heat in Asia

    How a change in the Pacific affects rainfall, heat and the choices available to people.

    In two minutes

    • El Niño is a warming of the central and eastern tropical Pacific, coupled with a shift in winds and rainfall. It is one phase of ENSO, which swings every three to seven years on average.
    • It can tilt the odds towards drier or hotter conditions in parts of Asia. It does not decide whether a heatwave, drought or flood happens in any one place.
    • The current bulletin expects El Niño to persist into early 2027 and then weaken. For March to May 2027, NOAA's odds favour El Niño, most likely weak or moderate.
    • Effects differ by region and season. India, Bangladesh, Nepal, mainland Southeast Asia, Indonesia and the Philippines each have their own pattern and their own national service.
    • The useful response is early, ordinary preparation, tied to the official local warning when it comes.

    Current bulletin: NOAA Climate Prediction Center, issued 12 November 2026: El Niño Advisory. A strong-to-very strong El Niño is likely through January to March 2027 (a greater than 79% chance), before it begins to weaken in early 2027. How to read it

    01An ordinary decision

    What to prepare before the warnings arrive

    Illustrative

    A municipal team hears on the news that El Niño may last into the coming hot season. Nobody has issued a heat warning for the town, and none will come for months. The team has to decide now what to repair, staff and budget before the first warning arrives.

    What the outlook gives

    A seasonal outlook tells the team that the odds in the Pacific favour El Niño for the next few months, and how confident forecasters are. It is a reason to start ordinary preparation early.

    What the team still needs

    It cannot tell the team which week will be hottest, which ward will suffer, or whether the water supply will hold. For that the team needs its national weather service's seasonal outlook for its region, its own records of past summers, and, closer to the time, the official local warning.

    Sources: WMO Bulletin, vol. 75(2), 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    02The science

    What El Niño is

    A pattern of ocean and air together

    ENSO, short for El Niño-Southern Oscillation, is a natural pattern in the tropical Pacific that involves both the ocean and the atmosphere. It has three states: El Niño, La Niña and neutral, when conditions are near their long-term average. Its phases swing back and forth every three to seven years on average.

    Sources: NOAA Climate.gov, 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    The usual pattern

    In most years the trade winds blow from east to west across the tropical Pacific. They push warm surface water towards the western Pacific, near Indonesia and the Philippines, where a deep pool of warm water builds up. In the east, off South America, colder water rises from below to replace it, a process called upwelling. Warm water in the west heats the air above it, which rises, forms clouds and brings rain; drier air sinks over the cooler east. This loop of rising and sinking air is called the Walker circulation.

    Sources: NOAA Climate.gov, 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    What changes in El Niño

    In El Niño the trade winds weaken. Warm surface water spreads farther east, and the central and eastern Pacific become warmer than average. Upwelling in the east weakens and the boundary between warm surface water and cold deep water, the thermocline, deepens there. The area of rising air and heavy rain shifts towards the central or eastern Pacific, while sinking air, and dryness, increase over Indonesia.

    Ocean and atmosphere reinforce each other: warmer water weakens the winds, and weaker winds let the water warm further. La Niña is roughly the reverse: cooler water strengthens the trade winds and increases upwelling, reinforcing the cooling.

    Sources: NOAA Climate.gov, 2026 (opens in a new tab) · WMO Bulletin, vol. 75(2), 2026 (opens in a new tab) · NOAA Climate Prediction Center, 12 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Sources: NOAA Climate.gov, 2026 (opens in a new tab) · WMO Bulletin, vol. 75(2), 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Anomalies and indices

    An anomaly is a departure from a stated reference average. NOAA measures ENSO from sea surface temperature departures in the Niño-3.4 region of the east-central equatorial Pacific (5°N to 5°S, 120°W to 170°W), against a 1991 to 2020 base period. A Pacific sea surface temperature anomaly of +2.5 °C is not a forecast that air temperatures anywhere in Asia will rise by 2.5 °C.

    NOAA now verifies its outlooks with the Relative Oceanic Niño Index (RONI): a three-month running mean of Niño-3.4 anomalies with the average tropical anomaly subtracted, so that warming of the whole tropics does not count as El Niño. El Niño is declared at +0.5 °C or more. The latest RONI values are estimates that can change for up to two months. Other agencies, and many news reports, use the older Oceanic Niño Index or the plain Niño 3.4 anomaly, which can differ; always check which index a figure uses.

    Sources: NOAA Climate Prediction Center, November 2026 (opens in a new tab) · NOAA Climate Prediction Center, 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Indices: NOAA's RONI and its definition (opens in a new tab) · the older Oceanic Niño Index (ONI) (opens in a new tab)

    03Across the region

    How a change in the Pacific reaches Asia

    Connections over distance

    When the area of rising air over the Pacific shifts, the large circulation that links it to the rest of the tropics shifts too. Scientists call these long-distance links teleconnections. In everyday terms, a change in where the Pacific rains can change where air sinks and skies stay clear thousands of kilometres away, including over parts of Asia.

    Sources: NOAA Climate.gov, 2026 (opens in a new tab) · WMO Bulletin, vol. 75(2), 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    From rain and cloud to heat

    Rainfall and temperature move together in much of the region: Southeast Asia's regional centre describes opposite rainfall, and consequently temperature, conditions in La Niña years to those in El Niño years. Where rain is scarcer than usual, the same season can also run hotter.

    Sources: ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre, 16 October 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Season, place and other drivers

    The same El Niño can matter a great deal in one season and little in another. Over Southeast Asia, its drying effect is typically stronger over the southern and eastern parts of the region from June to October, and over most of the Maritime Continent from September to November. India's Ministry of Earth Sciences notes that the Indian Ocean Dipole, the Madden-Julian Oscillation and regional ocean-atmosphere interactions also shape the monsoon. No two El Niño events produce identical impacts, and outcomes depend on the pattern of Pacific warming, the timing and other climate drivers.

    Sources: ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre, 16 October 2026 (opens in a new tab) · Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Earth Sciences, India, 5 August 2026 (opens in a new tab) · WMO Bulletin, vol. 75(2), 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Peak and effects at different times

    An event's peak in the Pacific and its effects in a region need not coincide. This year's El Niño is forecast to peak around the end of 2026, while India's heatwave season runs mainly from April to June. Effects arrive through each region's own seasonal cycle, so there is no single delay that applies everywhere.

    Sources: World Meteorological Organization, 21 October 2026 (opens in a new tab) · WMO Bulletin, vol. 75(2), 2026 (opens in a new tab) · Rohini, Rajeevan and Srivastava (open access, PMC), 2016 (opens in a new tab)

    04Five regions

    South and Southeast Asia, region by region

    Each region keeps to what its own sources show. Historical patterns are kept apart from this season's outlook, and nothing here is extended from one region to another.

    India and the South Asian monsoon

    Historical pattern
    El Niño has historically been associated with weaker summer monsoon rainfall over parts of India, but it is not the only influence: the Indian Ocean Dipole and the Madden-Julian Oscillation matter too. In India's records for 1961 to 2013, most years with above-normal heatwave activity in April to June followed El Niño events.

    Sources: Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Earth Sciences, India, 5 August 2026 (opens in a new tab) · Rohini, Rajeevan and Srivastava (open access, PMC), 2016 (opens in a new tab)

    This season
    India's Ministry of Earth Sciences reported in August 2026 that INCOIS assessed El Niño as likely to persist from June 2026 to February 2027, with probabilities of 70 to 90 per cent. IMD releases its seasonal outlooks for the hot season between March and May.

    Sources: Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Earth Sciences, India, 5 August 2026 (opens in a new tab) · Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC), 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Cannot be inferred
    An India-wide pattern does not give the outlook for one district or city, and the monsoon relationship does not carry over to all of South Asia.
    National or regional service
    India Meteorological Department (opens in a new tab)
    A question to prepare
    Which wards and services had the most heat-related problems in the last hot season, and are their repairs funded before March?

    Bangladesh and Nepal

    Historical pattern
    These neighbours do not simply follow India's pattern. Nepal's Department of Hydrology and Meteorology data, as reported, show eight of nine El Niño years with reduced monsoon rainfall, and one with above-average rain. For Bangladesh, this page found no national statement of a typical El Niño relationship; its weather department's monsoon outlook for July to September 2026 expected near-normal rainfall overall, with above-normal temperature across the country.

    Sources: The Kathmandu Post (reporting DHM), 2 June 2026 (opens in a new tab) · Bangladesh Meteorological Department with RIMES, July 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    This season
    No current outlook for the 2027 hot season from either service was verified here.
    Cannot be inferred
    A pattern for one country, or one season, does not transfer to its neighbour or to a single district.
    National or regional service
    Bangladesh Meteorological Department (opens in a new tab) · Nepal Department of Hydrology and Meteorology (opens in a new tab)
    A question to prepare
    Who receives the national service's heat and rainfall bulletins in our area, and who passes them on to the people most exposed?

    Mainland Southeast Asia

    Historical pattern
    El Niño typically brings drier-than-average conditions to Southeast Asia, with the effect usually stronger over the southern and eastern parts of the region from June to October, and more pronounced in moderate or strong events.

    Sources: ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre, 16 October 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    This season
    ASMC's seasonal forecast of 5 November 2026 predicts below-normal rainfall over southeastern mainland Southeast Asia for November 2026 to January 2027, and above-normal temperature over the ASEAN region. Towards the end of the year the northern ASEAN region enters its dry season, with a possible rise in hotspots and haze.

    Sources: ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre, 5 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Cannot be inferred
    A regional tercile forecast does not give any one province's rainfall, and the northern and southern parts of the mainland have different seasons.
    National or regional service
    ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre (opens in a new tab)
    A question to prepare
    If the dry season runs hotter, which water points, schools and worksites lose water or shade first, and who can fix them?

    Indonesia and the maritime region

    Historical pattern
    El Niño typically brings drier-than-average rainfall to most of the Maritime Continent from September to November, raising the risk of forest fires and smoke haze. In El Niño, sinking air over Indonesia brings higher pressure and dryness.

    Sources: ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre, 16 October 2026 (opens in a new tab) · NOAA Climate.gov, 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    This season
    NOAA's November discussion notes suppressed rainfall persisting over Indonesia. ASMC's forecast predicts below-normal rainfall over parts of the southeastern and northeastern Maritime Continent through January 2027; hotspots with local smoke plumes may still occur where rain stays below normal.

    Sources: NOAA Climate Prediction Center, 12 November 2026 (opens in a new tab) · ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre, 5 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Cannot be inferred
    Rainfall varies sharply between islands; a regional forecast does not show one island's water supply or fire risk.
    National or regional service
    BMKG, Indonesia (opens in a new tab) · Meteorological Service Singapore (opens in a new tab)
    A question to prepare
    Where do smoke and heat arrive together, and do clinics and schools there have a plan for both?

    The Philippines

    Historical pattern
    PAGASA advises that El Niño increases the likelihood of below-normal rainfall, which may bring dry spells and droughts in some areas, while above-normal rainfall may still occur over the western part of the country during the southwest monsoon (Habagat).

    Sources: PAGASA, Philippines, 6 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    This season
    PAGASA's current monthly outlook was not verified here; check PAGASA's latest El Niño advisory and climate outlook.
    Cannot be inferred
    Rainfall differs between Luzon, the Visayas and Mindanao, and between the archipelago's eastern and western sides.
    National or regional service
    PAGASA (opens in a new tab)
    A question to prepare
    Which barangays relied on water deliveries in past dry spells, and is that arrangement funded for the coming months?

    05Daily life

    How it can travel through daily life

    Each chain below may happen; whether it does depends on local conditions, which are what preparation can change.

    1. Drier conditions→, which can lead to pressure on water supply→, which can lead to less reliable water and cooling

      Whether this happens depends on how a town's water is stored and pumped, whether supply depends on electricity or tankers, and who has storage at home.

      On Common Shade: The energy–water loop · A water point at the market

    2. Hotter days→, which can lead to more demand for cooling→, which can lead to possible pressure on electricity

      It depends on how much spare capacity the grid has, how much cooling is electric, and whether outages fall on the hottest hours.

      On Common Shade: The energy–water loop · Cooling options

    3. Work disrupted by heat→, which can lead to less income→, which can lead to less money for protection

      It depends on whether workers can take breaks without losing pay, whether wage support exists, and whether employers adjust hours.

      On Common Shade: Heat rules at work · Paying when heat strikes

    4. Schools or care services close→, which can lead to care moves into homes→, which can lead to more unpaid care, and lost earnings for whoever provides it

      It depends on whether closures come with alternatives, and who is expected to stay home.

      On Common Shade: Who heat hits hardest · Who could help a household

    5. Dry vegetation and fires lit by people→, which can lead to more fire and smoke haze→, which can lead to heat and smoke together

      Most relevant where land is cleared by burning and where El Niño brings drier months, as in parts of the Maritime Continent.

      On Common Shade: Intersections

      Sources: ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre, 16 October 2026 (opens in a new tab) · ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre, 5 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Where the pathways meet

    These pathways tend to meet in the same places. A pump that needs power, a clinic that needs water, a worker who cannot afford a break and a school that closes early can all fall on one household in the same week. Which of them matters depends on the local season and on how a town's services are arranged, which is why the outlook alone cannot say. Follow one market through a hot season.

    Who has fewer choices

    The same dry, hot season lands differently. A street vendor who cannot leave a stall, a construction worker paid by the day, an older person living alone under a tin roof, a person with a disability who cannot reach a cooling space, and a mother who stays home when the school shuts all face the heat with fewer choices than a salaried office worker with a fan and a refrigerator.

    Illustrative

    Picture a household in a dense ward: the father does day-labour, the mother sells vegetables, the grandmother has diabetes. If water is cut in the afternoon and the school closes for heat, someone loses a day's earnings to collect water and look after the children, and the grandmother waits longer in the clinic queue. None of this is a forecast; it shows where preparation helps.

    06The current outlook

    El Niño watch

    Implications for Asia's next hot season

    Official status
    El Niño Advisory NOAA's alert status when El Niño conditions are observed and expected to continue.
    Bulletin
    NOAA Climate Prediction Center, ENSO Diagnostic Discussion, issued 12 November 2026
    Forecast period
    January to March 2027 for strength; ENSO phase odds to April to June 2027

    Observed What changed

    El Niño strengthened compared with the previous month: sea surface temperature anomalies exceeded 4.0 °C and extended farther west across the eastern equatorial Pacific. The weekly Niño-3.4 index reached +2.5 °C, and rainfall stayed suppressed over Indonesia.

    Forecast What the bulletin expects

    A strong-to-very strong El Niño is likely through January to March 2027 (a greater than 79% chance), before it begins to weaken in early 2027.

    For March to May 2027, NOAA puts the chance of El Niño at 80%, most likely weak (53%) or moderate (25%); for April to June 2027, ENSO-neutral becomes more likely (61%) than El Niño (38%).

    Our reading What it means for preparation

    The odds favour El Niño persisting into the start of the hot season in much of South and Southeast Asia, and ASMC expects above-normal temperature over the ASEAN region through January 2027. That is a reason to get ordinary heat protection ready early. It is not a forecast of a heatwave anywhere.

    Uncertain What remains uncertain locally

    A Pacific probability does not give any town's chance of a heatwave, a water shortage or a flood. NOAA's strength odds describe the Pacific, not the strength of local impacts. Local outcomes depend on the season, geography, other ocean drivers and local warnings.

    This panel reflects NOAA's discussion of 12 November 2026. NOAA's next discussion is scheduled for 10 December 2026.

    Four probabilities that are easy to confuse

    Phase

    The chance that ENSO is in El Niño, neutral or La Niña in a given three-month season.

    NOAA (November 2026): El Niño 80% for March to May 2027.

    Strength

    The chance that the Pacific index falls in a strength band. A stronger Pacific event does not necessarily mean stronger local impacts.

    NOAA (November 2026), March to May 2027: weak 53%, moderate 25%, strong 2%, very strong 0%.

    Seasonal climate

    The chance of above- or below-normal rainfall or temperature in a region over a season, from a regional or national service.

    ASMC (5 November 2026): above-normal temperature over the ASEAN region, November 2026 to January 2027.

    Local warning

    An official warning of heat, rain or storms for a district over the coming days. This is what sets local action moving.

    Issued by the national weather service for your district, as the event approaches.

    Sources: NOAA Climate Prediction Center, November 2026 (opens in a new tab) · NOAA Climate Prediction Center, November 2026 (opens in a new tab) · ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre, 5 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Uncertainty, and long-term warming

    Forecasts made in the northern spring are less reliable than at other times of year. Forecasters call this the spring predictability barrier, though it is more a dip in accuracy than a wall. It matters here because the odds for April to June 2027 rest partly on that season: NOAA's chance of El Niño falls from 80% for March to May to 38% for April to June, when neutral becomes more likely.

    El Niño sits on top of long-term warming rather than replacing it. Scientists have low confidence in predicting exactly how a warmer world will change ENSO itself. Attributing any single heatwave or flood solely to El Niño needs a study of that event.

    Sources: NOAA Climate.gov ENSO blog, 2015 (opens in a new tab) · NOAA Climate Prediction Center, November 2026 (opens in a new tab) · NOAA Climate.gov, 2026 (opens in a new tab) · NOAA Climate Prediction Center, 12 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    When bulletins seem to disagree

    Bulletins can seem to disagree because they measure different things. NOAA's strength odds use RONI over three-month seasons; WMO's seasonal update forecasts the plain Niño 3.4 anomaly from many models. Read each with its own date, index and period, and never average them.

    What decision are you making?

    Municipality

    • Pick one public service to protect first, and the sites where people wait or work in the heat.
    • Name who authorises, pays for, runs and repairs each measure; they can be different bodies.
    • Agree what an official local warning changes: water hours, opening times, staff on duty.
    • Check that warnings reach people who cannot read, hear or get online.

    On Common Shade: El Niño and India's smaller cities · The one-page worksheet · Plan a response

    Employer

    • Plan rest breaks, shade and water at work before the hot season, without loss of pay.
    • Move the heaviest work away from the hottest hours when a warning is issued.
    • Know the signs of heat illness, and who to call.
    • Check national rules on heat at work.

    On Common Shade: Heat rules at work · Practical actions: workplaces · Find a heat tool

    Community organisation

    • Map who is most exposed: people living alone, outdoor workers, people with disabilities, households without water storage.
    • Agree with the municipality how you will report failures and pass on warnings.
    • Keep a list of cool, accessible places and their opening hours.
    • Plan for care when schools or clinics change hours.

    Funder

    • Fund recurrent costs, such as water, staff and repairs, not only installation.
    • Release money on agreed triggers, so it arrives before the heat, not after.
    • Ask how reach will be counted: sites working out of sites needed, and who was left out.
    • Support national services' outlooks reaching local decision-makers.

    08Questions

    Questions that improve decisions

    Does El Niño mean every place will have a heatwave?

    No. El Niño raises or lowers the likelihood of hotter, drier, wetter or cooler conditions in different regions. It does not predict that a particular heatwave will happen anywhere.

    Sources: WMO Bulletin, vol. 75(2), 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Does a strong El Niño mean severe local impact?

    Not necessarily. NOAA states that the strength of El Niño does not necessarily correspond with the strength of its influence or expected impact. Even a very strong event makes typical impacts more likely, not certain.

    Sources: NOAA Climate Prediction Center, November 2026 (opens in a new tab) · NOAA Climate Prediction Center, 12 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Does El Niño guarantee drought or a failed monsoon?

    No. In India, El Niño has historically been associated with weaker monsoon rain over parts of the country, but other drivers also shape the monsoon. In Nepal, one of nine El Niño years in its national records brought above-average rain.

    Sources: Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Earth Sciences, India, 5 August 2026 (opens in a new tab) · The Kathmandu Post (reporting DHM), 2 June 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Can floods happen during an El Niño?

    Yes. An ENSO forecast shifts the odds; it does not rule out heavy rain. In the Philippines, PAGASA notes that above-normal rainfall may still occur over the western part of the country during the southwest monsoon in an El Niño year.

    Sources: WMO Bulletin, vol. 75(2), 2026 (opens in a new tab) · PAGASA, Philippines, 6 July 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    Does an ENSO-neutral period remove heat risk?

    No. Heat depends on the local climate, the season, long-term warming and other drivers such as the Indian Ocean Dipole. NOAA's odds favour neutral conditions by April to June 2027, which is much of India's heatwave season.

    Sources: Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Earth Sciences, India, 5 August 2026 (opens in a new tab) · NOAA Climate Prediction Center, November 2026 (opens in a new tab) · WMO Bulletin, vol. 75(2), 2026 (opens in a new tab) · Rohini, Rajeevan and Srivastava (open access, PMC), 2016 (opens in a new tab)

    What does "super El Niño" mean?

    It is not an official category. NOAA's strength table uses bands in 0.5 °C steps of the RONI index, the highest being 2.0 °C or more (very strong). NOAA calls an event historic if its three-month RONI reaches +2.5 °C or more, and gave that about a 60% chance for October to December and November to January. Use the issuing agency's own terms, with their index and period.

    Sources: NOAA Climate Prediction Center, November 2026 (opens in a new tab) · NOAA Climate Prediction Center, 12 November 2026 (opens in a new tab)

    What local information is worth checking next?

    Your national weather service's seasonal outlook for your region, its heat and rainfall warnings for your district, and your own records of what failed in the last hot season. Regional centres such as ASMC add the regional picture.

    Sources: ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre, 5 November 2026 (opens in a new tab) · WMO Bulletin, vol. 75(2), 2026 (opens in a new tab)
