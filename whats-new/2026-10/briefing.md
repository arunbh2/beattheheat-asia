## Developments worth understanding

### Support announced for the Southeast Asia Heat Health Hub's next stage

- Happened: 8 October 2026 (Announced)
- Reviewed by Common Shade: 31 October 2026

Common Shade's reading: Regional sharing of heat-health evidence gains support for a further stage, with named work in two countries. It is one of the coordinating roles that our trace of where money goes found thinnest.

What the sources establish:

- Philanthropy Asia Alliance announced support for the next stage of the Global Heat Health Information Network's Southeast Asia Hub, including heat-health work in Indonesia (Makassar and two more cities) and Thailand in 2027, and preparations for a second Southeast Asia Heat Health Forum. The amount was not disclosed. [1] Establishes: That the support was announced, and the places it names. It does not establish an amount, a period, or that money has been committed or paid.

Limits:

- An announcement is not money received: the amount, the period and any payment are not disclosed.
- No application route was verified; this is not an open funding call.

What would strengthen or change this reading: A published amount or grant agreement, and heat-health work starting in the named cities in 2027.

### A February announcement for South Asia, first reviewed this month

- Happened: 20 February 2026 (Announced)
- Reviewed by Common Shade: 31 October 2026
- Outside this edition's period. Why it is here: It was announced in February 2026, before this edition's period, and Common Shade first reviewed and added it in October 2026. It is included because, read beside the Hub announcement, it names support for the same kind of role. It is not an October event.

Common Shade's reading: Support for shared heat-risk thresholds and evidence across South Asia, a role that sits between health agencies and weather services rather than inside either.

What the sources establish:

- The World Meteorological Organization reported on 20 February 2026 that two philanthropic foundations fund a South Asia Climate-Health Desk under the WHO-WMO Climate and Health Joint Programme, and a South Asia Scientific Research Consortium, through a grant to IISER Pune, to develop tailored heat-risk thresholds. Amounts were not disclosed. The funders are named in the funding record. [2] Establishes: That the funding was announced, and for what. It does not establish amounts, that money has been paid, or that thresholds have been published.

Limits:

- Amounts were not disclosed, and money committed or paid is not verified.
- The source is a report by one of the bodies hosting the desk, not the funders' own grant records.

What would strengthen or change this reading: Tailored heat-risk thresholds published for use in warnings, and the consortium's first published research.

### The Global Heat Forum, and a city heat plan reported for Bangladesh

- Happened: 6 October 2026 (The Forum opened (6 to 9 October 2026))
- Reviewed by Common Shade: 31 October 2026

Common Shade's reading: A gathering and four assessments are steps in shared understanding, not protection delivered. The Narayanganj plan matters as a first city plan in Bangladesh; it is older than this edition, and whether its actions have budget lines is not verified.

What the sources establish:

- Nearly 600 scientists, business leaders, philanthropists and health experts met in Bangkok from 6 to 9 October 2026. The Forum released the first country-level heat and health assessments for Bangladesh, Bhutan, India and Nepal, and a roadmap for investment and action on extreme heat. [3] Establishes: That the Forum took place and released the assessments and a roadmap. A roadmap is not funding, and the source is a press release from an organiser.
- Bangladesh's heat and health assessment, released at the Forum, notes the country's first urban heatwave action plan, for Narayanganj, alongside national guidelines on heat-related illness and a public campaign. [4] Establishes: That the assessment reports the plan. It does not date the plan.
- The plan itself records its approval by Narayanganj City Corporation on 22 September 2025, and gives each short-term action a tentative cost with potential funding sources. [5] Establishes: That the plan is a 2025 development, reported in October 2026. Costs and potential funders in a plan are not budget lines. The approval date was read from the plan's front matter by a reviewer; the site's automated check confirmed the plan's text but did not test the date.

Limits:

- Some reports described a four-country initiative or coalition. Established sources describe four country assessments, so that framing was held back.
- The Narayanganj plan was approved in September 2025; October 2026 is when it was reported, not when it happened.

What would strengthen or change this reading: Budget lines for the Narayanganj plan's actions, and the Forum roadmap's investments becoming funded commitments.

## An opportunity the evidence supports

### Heat-safe work: solutions measured in studies, little funding traced

Several solutions for safer work in the heat have been measured in a study, while our trace found no funding announcements for them; much of this work is paid from public budgets and by employers, which the trace cannot see.

- The problem: Outdoor and indoor workers exposed to heat during working hours, and the pay and health they lose to it.
- What to examine: The solutions grouped under the heat-safe work action: measures at the workplace, set or encouraged by workplace rules.
- The evidence:
  - Solutions for safer work in the heat in the library have been measured in a study or independently evaluated, as shown on the evidence map. [6] Establishes: That studies exist for these solutions in some settings. It does not establish that they work at scale or in every place.
  - Not found: Our funding trace found no funding announcements for heat-safe work. [7] Establishes: A gap in traced funding, not an absence of funding. Searched: Public funding announcements traced by Common Shade up to 31 October 2026, mostly investment in businesses. Public budgets and employers' own spending are not covered.
- Its limits: A study in one setting is not proof of effect at scale. The trace cannot see public budgets or employers' spending, where much of this work is paid for.
- Who could take part (a proposal): A possible arrangement, for discussion. No organisation has agreed to it. Authorise: The authorities that set workplace rules. Deliver: Employers, with worker organisations. Finance: Employers and public budgets, with philanthropy for evaluation. Maintain: Labour inspectorates and worker organisations.
- The main unresolved dependency: Whether public budgets and employers already pay for this work, which the funding trace cannot see.
- A next question to check locally: Ask the labour authority, or a large employer of outdoor workers near you, which heat measures are budgeted and who pays for them.

## A question that matters for action

### What keeps a heat-health pilot going after its first grant?

> How do heat-health pilots in Southeast Asia keep their institutional footing once their first funding ends?

- Why it matters now: The support announced this month is for a next stage. Whether earlier pilots continued once funding ended bears on how such stages are designed.
- What is known: A funder's own account of Southeast Asia notes that successful heat-health pilots often lose their institutional footing once funding ends. It is a single account, labelled uncertain.
- What is not known: Which parts of a pilot continue, who pays for them, and why some stop.
- Which decision depends on it: How funders and public agencies design grants and hand-overs, for example whether a grant plans for running costs after it ends.
- What could answer it: Following five to eight heat-health pilots for two years after their first funding, recording who kept which part going and why (our suggestion).

## What to watch

- Amounts and agreements for both announcements. Evidence that would matter: A published amount, grant agreement or annual report entry.
- Heat-health work starting in Indonesia and Thailand in 2027. Evidence that would matter: Work under way in the named cities, with who runs it.
- The Forum roadmap. Evidence that would matter: Investments named in it becoming funded commitments.

## For your work

- City and public-service teams: Does your city's heat plan name a budget line for each action, and who holds it? Narayanganj's plan gives tentative costs and potential funders, which is a first step.
- Worker and community organisations: Which heat measures at work are already paid for by employers or public budgets where you work? Our funding trace cannot see them.
- Funders and philanthropic institutions: Where support is announced for a next stage, what would show that the earlier stage continued once its first grant ended?
- Researchers and ecosystem builders: Could a two-year follow-up of a handful of heat-health pilots answer this edition's question, and who holds the records it would need?
