    El Niño set to bring more heat into 2027

    World Meteorological Organization · Global

    By mid-August 2026 El Niño had become firmly established. WMO's State of the Climate release says it has been intensifying since August and is expected to be the strongest on record by December. That is a forecast, not an observed record: NOAA's discussion of 12 November 2026 put the chance of a historic event (a three-month RONI of +2.5 °C or more) at about 60% for October to December 2026 and for November 2026 to January 2027. WMO calls El Niño a risk multiplier for health and writes that WHO has, for the first time, classified the anticipated health impacts as a health emergency. WHO's own plan describes a Grade 3 response, its internal grading of its operational response; no declaration of a public health emergency under the International Health Regulations was found.

    What to watchEarly warnings and heat plans ready for the 2027 hot season, above all in Southeast Asia, where El Niño and warming broke records in April 2016.

    2026 will be one of the four warmest years on record

    World Meteorological Organization · Global

    WMO's State of the Climate Update 2026, released as COP31 opened, finds that the past 12 years have each been the warmest on record and the past four the top four. The global average surface temperature for January to September 2026 was about 1.47 °C above the pre-industrial average. In early November the EU's Copernicus service said 2026 is most likely to be the second-hottest year on record. It estimates that 146.5 million people in India were exposed to extreme heat in an April 2026 heatwave, and that the number of countries reporting multi-hazard early warning systems rose from 56 in 2015 to 136 in 2025.

    What to watchA finding, not a commitment: watch whether heat plans and budgets are sized for the heat these years now bring, not the heat people remember.

    Implications for Asia's next hot season

    Official status
    El Niño Advisory NOAA's alert status when El Niño conditions are observed and expected to continue.
    Issuing agency and bulletin
    NOAA Climate Prediction Center, ENSO Diagnostic Discussion, issued 12 November 2026
    Forecast period
    Strength: January to March 2027. Phase odds: January to March 2027 to April to June 2027.
    Next scheduled bulletin
    10 December 2026

    What changed? No substantive change since NOAA's discussion of 12 November 2026: no newer monthly discussion has been issued (the next is due 10 December 2026). NOAA's weekly update of 23 November, the Bureau of Meteorology's weekly values to 22 November and ASMC's status page are consistent with it. WMO's update of 20 November 2026 is a project update on preparedness, not a new forecast.

    Observed What the measurements show

    El Niño strengthened compared with the previous month: sea surface temperature anomalies exceeded 4.0 °C and extended farther west across the eastern equatorial Pacific. The weekly Niño-3.4 index reached +2.5 °C, and rainfall stayed suppressed over Indonesia.

    NOAA's monthly review of conditions in the tropical Pacific. The +2.5 °C Niño-3.4 figure is the latest weekly value it reports, not the three-month RONI used to classify events.

    Forecast What the bulletin expects

    A strong-to-very strong El Niño is likely through January to March 2027 (a greater than 79% chance), before it begins to weaken in early 2027.

    For March to May 2027, NOAA Climate Prediction Center puts the chance of El Niño at 80%, most likely weak (53%) or moderate (25%); for April to June 2027, ENSO-neutral becomes more likely (61%) than El Niño (38%).

    Our reading What it means for preparation

    The odds favour El Niño persisting into the start of the hot season in much of South and Southeast Asia, then easing. That is a reason to get ordinary heat protection working early, in the places where people already spend the hottest hours. It is not a forecast of a heatwave anywhere.

    Uncertain What remains uncertain locally

    A Pacific probability is not any town's chance of a heatwave, a water shortage or a flood. NOAA's strength odds describe the Pacific index, not the strength of local impacts. Local outcomes depend on the season, geography, other drivers such as the Indian Ocean Dipole, long-term warming and local conditions; national seasonal outlooks and official local warnings carry that detail.

      How records are kept

      What this page is for

      • What changed in heat policy, finance, research and practice, with dates and sources.
      • Which developments concern a place, an action or a group of people: use the filters.
      • For each promise, what the evidence confirms, what is unknown, and what would show the next step.
      • Where to explore the implications: actions, country pages and funding pages.

      Signals follows developments outside Common Shade. Watch Common Shade grow records changes to this site; the evidence map appraises what is known; the funding pages explain financial arrangements and the money traced. Watch Common Shade grow · Evidence map · Paying for protection · Funding traced

      Method

      • One canonical record per initiative, with stable identifiers for each record, deliverable and milestone; later developments are added to the same record, and nothing inconvenient is deleted.
      • A commitment is tracked deliverable by deliverable. Funding announced, committed, paid out and spent are separate steps, as are a rule's adoption, the date it takes effect, and evidence that it is applied.
      • Each milestone names its sources and what each one establishes. Whether it is confirmed comes from the site's source check (data/inbox/*/link-check.json): a page that merely opens is not evidence, and a secondary report is shown as reported until the original is checked.
      • An evaluation is recorded with its findings, positive, mixed or negative, and is not treated as proof of cause.
      • Interpretation is labelled as Common Shade's reading and kept apart from what sources say. Broad climate or health funding is never shown as money for heat unless the source says so.
      • Maintenance: node tools/signals-review.js lists records due for review and what each source's check found, and turns a proposed milestone into a record only with its evidence. Nothing is published without review.

      A review re-reads the sources cited here and the site's source-check records. It is not a full search for newer developments; a missing update means none was found in these sources, not that nothing happened.

      Know of a development we have missed, or one that has moved? Tell us, with its source. The whole record is open: signals.json, with its source-check records, and a plain-text version. See also State of the field and Heat in the news.
