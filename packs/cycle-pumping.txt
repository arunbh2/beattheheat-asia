CONTENT

The groundwater loop
Dry fields need more water, so farmers pump harder, aquifers fall and the shortage deepens.
Evidence for the cycle as a whole: established.

THE LINKS IN THE CYCLE
- Water shortages, which leads to irrigation demand soars: Taps run dry and wells fall just as people, crops and animals need more water to cope with the heat. Drought and salt water can leave water unusable as well as scarce: in April 2024, UNICEF estimated 73,900 households in the Mekong Delta short of water, with women and girls carrying most of the extra fetching. [https://www.wri.org/insights/responding-day-zero-equitably-water-crisis-lessons-cape-town-and-chennai] [https://www.unicef.org/media/156076/file/Viet_Nam_Humanitarian_Situation_Report_No._01_%28drought%29_reporting_period__-27_April_2024.pdf]
- Irrigation demand soars, which leads to groundwater over-pumped: Hot, dry fields need far more water to keep crops alive. Watering fields cools the air but makes it more humid, which can raise the humid heat that farmworkers feel. [https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08238] [https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/moist-heat-stress-extremes-in-india-enhanced-by-irrigation/]
- Groundwater over-pumped, which leads to water shortages: Farmers and cities pump deeper and harder, draining aquifers faster than rain can refill them. In India, warming is linked to more pumping for irrigation and faster groundwater loss, leaving less in reserve for the next hot season. [https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08238] [https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/publications/publication/?seqNo115=387051]

STUDIES BEHIND IT
- Minderhoud et al., Environmental Research Communications, 2020: Groundwater extraction may drown mega-delta. Groundwater pumping has sped up the sinking of the Mekong delta, to more than 2.5 cm a year in some areas. Where: Mekong delta, Vietnam. Limit it states: Sinking rates vary across the delta; the top rate is not everywhere. [https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2515-7620/ab5e21]
- Rodell, Velicogna and Famiglietti, Nature, 2009: Satellite-based estimates of groundwater depletion in India. Satellites show Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana lost 109 km³ of groundwater between 2002 and 2008, mainly to irrigation, while rainfall stayed close to normal. Where: Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana, India. Limit it states: An older period; it measures water lost, not the heat link itself. [https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08238]

ACTIONS THAT COULD INTERRUPT IT
- Smart irrigation and groundwater rules: Drip irrigation, metering, aquifer recharge and fair pumping rules keep wells from running dry. Watch for: Watering fields cools the air but makes it more humid, which can raise the humid heat that farmworkers feel. Saving water and protecting workers are worth checking together. [https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/moist-heat-stress-extremes-in-india-enhanced-by-irrigation/]
