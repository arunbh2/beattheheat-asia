CONTENT

The energy–water loop
Water systems need power and power plants need water. In a heatwave, each failure deepens the other.
Evidence for the cycle as a whole: established.

THE LINKS IN THE CYCLE
- Power cuts and grid stress, which leads to water shortages: Peak demand and heat-stressed lines and transformers push grids to the edge. Outages switch off cooling when it matters most. Heat and low water can strike the grid together: in northern Viet Nam in June 2023, heat drove demand up while low reservoirs left only 23.7% of hydropower capacity available, with plant and transmission problems adding to the strain. [https://manilatimes.net/2023/08/12/business/foreign-business/power-crisis-cost-vietnam-14b-wb/1904920/amp] [https://en.evn.com.vn/d6/news/Ministry-of-Industry-and-Trade-informs-about-power-supply-situation-in-hot-season-66-163-3527.aspx]
- Water shortages, which leads to power plants cut output: Taps run dry and wells fall just as people, crops and animals need more water to cope with the heat. Drought and salt water can leave water unusable as well as scarce: in April 2024, UNICEF estimated 73,900 households in the Mekong Delta short of water, with women and girls carrying most of the extra fetching. [https://www.wri.org/insights/responding-day-zero-equitably-water-crisis-lessons-cape-town-and-chennai] [https://www.unicef.org/media/156076/file/Viet_Nam_Humanitarian_Situation_Report_No._01_%28drought%29_reporting_period__-27_April_2024.pdf]
- Power plants cut output, which leads to power cuts and grid stress: Coal, gas and nuclear plants need cool water. When rivers run low or warm, they have to produce less. [https://www.wri.org/insights/40-indias-thermal-power-plants-are-water-scarce-areas-threatening-shutdowns]

STUDIES BEHIND IT
- World Bank estimate, reported by AFP via The Manila Times, 2023: Power crisis cost Vietnam $1.4B. Heatwaves in May and June 2023 pushed up demand while hydropower reservoirs ran low, and rolling blackouts hit northern Vietnam. The World Bank’s early estimate put the cost at about US$1.4 billion, roughly 0.3% of GDP. Where: Northern Vietnam. Limit it states: An early estimate reported in the press; drought and demand both played a part. [https://manilatimes.net/2023/08/12/business/foreign-business/power-crisis-cost-vietnam-14b-wb/1904920/amp]
- World Resources Institute, 2018: 40% of India’s thermal power plants are in water-scarce areas, threatening shutdowns. 14 of India’s 20 largest thermal utilities shut down at least once for lack of water between 2013 and 2016. Water shortages cost 14 TWh of power in 2016. Where: India. Limit it states: Data from before 2017; plants and water rules have changed since. [https://www.wri.org/insights/40-indias-thermal-power-plants-are-water-scarce-areas-threatening-shutdowns]

ACTIONS THAT COULD INTERRUPT IT
- Power that doesn’t need water: Solar and wind use very little water, and dry cooling protects thermal plants when rivers run low or warm.
- Backup power for water: Solar pumps and backup supply keep water flowing when the grid fails.
