CONTENT

The cooling trap
Air conditioning heats the street, and a hotter city needs even more air conditioning.
Evidence for the cycle as a whole: established.

THE LINKS IN THE CYCLE
- Cooling demand peaks, which leads to urban heat islands: As temperatures climb, so does the demand for fans and air conditioning, pushing electricity use to new peaks. Air conditioners push their heat outside: in a model of city blocks in Rajkot, condensers on outside walls could warm the street air by more than 1 °C and raise cooling demand further. [https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-cooling] [https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-cooling-in-southeast-asia] [https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-62477-4]
- Urban heat islands, which leads to cooling demand peaks: Dense surfaces, little shade and waste heat keep city cores several degrees hotter than their surroundings, especially at night. This is the urban heat island effect. [https://www.thegpsc.org/sites/gpsc/files/cooling_singapore_-_digital_urban_climate_twin.pdf] [https://spc.tn.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/Heat_Mitigation_Strategy.pdf]

STUDIES BEHIND IT
- Salamanca et al., Journal of Geophysical Research, 2014 (summary by Arizona State University): Excess heat from air conditioners causes higher nighttime temperatures. In a Phoenix heatwave, waste heat from air conditioners raised night-time air temperatures by more than 1 °C in parts of the city. Where: Phoenix, United States. Limit it states: A desert city outside Asia; the size of the effect in humid Asian cities is not measured here. [https://news.asu.edu/content/excess-heat-air-conditioners-causes-higher-nighttime-temperatures]
- International Energy Agency, 2018: The Future of Cooling. China, India and Indonesia are expected to account for half of the world’s air conditioners by 2050. Where: Global. Limit it states: A projection to 2050 that assumes current trends. [https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-cooling]
- Tamil Nadu State Planning Commission, 2024: Beating the Heat: Tamil Nadu Heat Mitigation Strategy. Chennai’s built-up area more than doubled between 2000 and 2020, and night-time land surface temperatures in its urban areas run about 2 °C higher than on its peri-urban edges. Where: Chennai and Tamil Nadu, India. Limit it states: Land surface temperature, not the air temperature people breathe; the two differ. [https://spc.tn.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/Heat_Mitigation_Strategy.pdf]
- Cooling Singapore, citing Chow and Roth, 2006: Cooling Singapore: digital urban climate twin. Singapore’s commercial centre has measured up to about 7 °C warmer than its outskirts on calm, clear nights. Where: Singapore. Limit it states: The 7 °C is a maximum on calm, clear nights, not a typical night. [https://www.thegpsc.org/sites/gpsc/files/cooling_singapore_-_digital_urban_climate_twin.pdf]

ACTIONS THAT COULD INTERRUPT IT
- Cool by design: Shading, ventilation, reflective roofs and efficient cooling cut the need for air conditioning, and the waste heat it dumps outside.
- Green, cool cities: Trees, water, cool roofs and cool pavements lower city temperatures and flatten peak demand.
