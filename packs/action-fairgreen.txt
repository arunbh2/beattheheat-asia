CONTENT

Green without pushing people out
Secure tenure, rent safeguards and designing with residents let poorer neighbourhoods gain shade and parks without losing their homes. Small, local greening that is “just green enough” protects people better than flagship projects.
Who could act: City and local governments, National governments, Community and civil society groups.
Over what time: years.
How progress could be measured: Original residents still living in a greened neighbourhood five years on.

EXAMPLE
In Bangkok, the Baan Mankong programme of the Community Organizations Development Institute gives canal-side communities subsidies and cheap loans to secure land and rebuild their homes. Along Khlong Lat Phrao, where flood works needed the canal banks, communities planned their own moves and rebuilding instead of being evicted. [https://proof.csmonitor.com/Environment/2019/0703/When-a-city-of-canals-floods-what-happens-to-waterway-shantytowns]

VICIOUS CYCLES IT COULD HELP INTERRUPT
- The green-displacement loop: Green space follows wealth. When greening reaches poorer districts without protection, rents rise, residents are pushed into hotter housing, and the gap grows.
