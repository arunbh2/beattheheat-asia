CONTENT

Keep city trees alive
Water, healthy soil, room for roots, heat-hardy species and a running budget for upkeep keep trees alive through hotter, drier summers, so their shade lasts. Looking after existing trees comes before planting new ones.
Who could act: City and local governments, Water authorities, Community and civil society groups.
Over what time: years.
How progress could be measured: Trees alive and growing three years after planting, against trees planted.

EXAMPLE
Berlin wrote a shared “good care” manual for its 12 districts, setting maintenance standards for street trees and green spaces, with drought-resistant species for replanting and a calendar of care. It followed losses such as a park where more than 500 of 870 new trees died within ten years despite watering. [https://doi.org/10.4337/9781800376762]

VICIOUS CYCLES IT COULD HELP INTERRUPT
- The dying-green loop: Heat and drought kill city trees. Without their shade the streets get hotter, and hotter streets kill more trees.
