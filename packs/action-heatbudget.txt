CONTENT

Dedicated heat budgets
Ring-fenced funding for heat action, set aside before the season, so protection doesn’t compete with the emergency.
Who could act: National governments, City and local governments, Development banks, donors and climate funds.
Over what time: years.
How progress could be measured: Heat budget set aside before the season starts.

EXAMPLE
Since August 2026, India’s disaster response funds can pay for heatwave relief; preparing for heat is still paid for separately. In Nepal, Madhesh Province and five municipalities committed 5% of sectoral budgets to adaptation and disaster risk reduction, with support from Practical Action and Mercy Corps. In Bangkok, a 2026 financing strategy with UNDP costs the city’s heat plan at about THB 146 million to set up and THB 10.8 million a year to run, and proposes climate budgeting so that adaptation spending can be identified and reported. [https://www.ceew.in/publications/nepal-heat-health-action] [https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2026-10/en-undp-bma_financial_strategy_on_urban_heat.pdf]

VICIOUS CYCLES IT COULD HELP INTERRUPT
- The reactive-spending loop: Stretched budgets skimp on prevention. Emergencies multiply, and costly emergency response stretches budgets further.
- The protection-gap loop: As insurers retreat, public budgets absorb the losses, protection is cut, and the risk drives insurers further away.
