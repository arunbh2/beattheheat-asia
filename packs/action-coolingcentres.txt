CONTENT

Cooling centres and check-ins
Public cooling spaces, water points and check-ins on older people living alone can offer relief in a heatwave. How much they protect health depends on who can reach them, how long people stay, and how hot it is once they leave.
Who could act: City and local governments, Community and civil society groups, Health authorities.
Over what time: season.
How progress could be measured: Cooling centre visits, and check-ins on people living alone.

EXAMPLE
Narayanganj's 2025 heat action plan in Bangladesh proposes cooling centres in accessible public buildings and counts their daily users as an indicator, a way to see whether people use them. [https://southasia.iclei.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Heat-Action-Plan_Narayanganj_Final_9th-Sept_2025_compressed.pdf]

WATCH FOR
- A 2025 review found no study that measured health outcomes for people using cooling centres; in simulated heatwaves, the benefits were short-lived. That is not evidence of no benefit. [https://researchportal.ukhsa.gov.uk/en/publications/public-health-effectiveness-of-cooling-centres-during-periods-of-/]
- Running costs can decide whether a cooling space keeps working. Bangkok reported that money to build is generally available but running costs are not always covered; consultation participants said some childcare centres held back from air conditioning over electricity costs. [https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2026-10/en-undp-bma_financial_strategy_on_urban_heat.pdf]

VICIOUS CYCLES IT COULD HELP INTERRUPT
- The invisible-deaths loop: Heat deaths go uncounted, so heat protection goes unfunded, so more people die uncounted.
