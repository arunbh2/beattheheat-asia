HOW TO USE THIS PACK
Load it into a notebook tool, or give it to an assistant and ask it to answer only from this pack and to name the source behind each point. An assistant can be wrong, even with a good pack in front of it. The sources in each pack are the authority: check any answer you rely on against them.
Evidence labels used across Common Shade: known (measured in a study or set out in an official record), inferred (models and projections), uncertain (reports not yet confirmed).

QUESTIONS TO START WITH
- What is "Heat-safe pregnancy and newborn care", who would carry it out, and over what time?
- Which vicious cycles could it help interrupt, and how?
- What does it depend on to work, and what side effects are noted?
- What examples does the pack give, and what do their sources show?
- How would progress be measured?
- What would I need to find out before proposing this in my place?

CONTENT

Heat-safe pregnancy and newborn care
Heat advice and checks in antenatal care, lighter work and rest for pregnant workers, cool labour wards and newborn units, and visits to new mothers in a heatwave.
Who could act: Health authorities, Employers and businesses, Community and civil society groups.
Over what time: season.
How progress could be measured: Antenatal and newborn services with heat advice, cool rooms and heat protocols.

EXAMPLE
Tamil Nadu’s heat strategy proposes screening for heat risk in the check-ups of the state’s pregnancy scheme. [https://spc.tn.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/Heat_Mitigation_Strategy.pdf]
