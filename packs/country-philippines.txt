## Heat context

**Climate:** Predominantly tropical maritime and humid. A hot dry rainfall season is not equivalent to a low-humidity hot-dry climate. Conditions vary by location and season. **Seasonality:** PAGASA identifies a generally hot dry season in March–May; this is not a guarantee of safe temperatures in other months. **Night heat:** The National Adaptation Plan describes rising maximum and minimum temperatures, with minimum temperatures rising faster over the period discussed. Indoor night exposure is not measured by satellite surface temperature or a daytime station heat index. **Trend scope:** NAP climate discussion uses historical records, including 1951–2020 anomalies relative to 1991–2020; no historical trend is labelled a 2026 local measurement. **Evidence:**
- Official guidance or data **Kind:** measured PAGASA describes high humidity and the broad seasonal pattern. **Source:** [PAGASA climate of the Philippines ](https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/information/climate-philippines)
- Official guidance or data **Kind:** measured NAP discusses rising daytime and nighttime temperature indicators; no city-specific change rate extrapolated here. **Source:** [Philippine National Adaptation Plan 2023–2050 ](https://www.climate.gov.ph/public/ckfinder/userfiles/files/Knowledge/PH%20NAP%202023-2050.pdf)

## Warnings

**Known, inferred, uncertain:** **Known:** Public information services exist. **Inferred:** Link alerts to named staff, services and spending authority. **Uncertain:** Current reach, uptime, local calibration, household reception and action completion. **National:** PAGASA publishes heat-index information; DOST describes the iHeatMAP platform and localised heat information. **Metric limit:** Heat index combines air temperature and humidity. It is not air temperature, indoor temperature, land-surface temperature or WBGT. Do not replace worksite exposure assessment with one public index. **Last mile:** Online dissemination is documented. Reliable reach to people without smartphones, language/accessibility coverage, message receipt and funded action are not established by platform availability. **Local example:** QC has local heat-action arrangements; a C40 roadmap brief describes its iRISE-UP context. Exact current local trigger documents still need confirmation. ## Rules for heat at work

**Known, inferred, uncertain:** **Known:** General OSH statute and recent advisory circulation. **Inferred:** Worker-verified implementation and inspection can connect work protection to income resilience. **Uncertain:** Full 2026 operative texts, enforcement, informal-worker coverage and claims experience. **Binding background:** RA 11058 (2018) establishes private-sector OSH duties and worker rights, including duties to manage hazardous conditions; it excludes the public sector from its stated coverage. **Recent update:** PMAP circulated Labor Advisory 20, series 2026, on 27 October. Its accessible account describes workplace-specific risk assessment, rest/water/cooling and adjusting unsafe work. **Verification boundary:** DOLE live pages and original signed advisory were not retrieved. PMAP is an employer association describing its own circulation, not an independent legal determination. No new numeric threshold, wage entitlement, universal stop-work rule or exact WBGT limit is asserted here. **Income and voice:** Whether rest is paid, which contractors/platform workers are covered, how grievances work and how protection is inspected need instrument- and workplace-specific verification. [Republic Act 11058, occupational safety and health ](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11058_2018.html) · [PMAP circulation of DOLE signed advisory 20-2026 ](https://pmap.org.ph/circular-no-2026-070-labor-advisory-no-20-series-of-2026-guidelines-on-the-prevention-and-management-of-heat-stress-during-el-nino-and-other-periods-of-extreme-heat/) **Evidence status:** reported

## Cooling and buildings

- Official guidance or data **Philippine Green Building Code (2015)** **Fact:** The original code, retrieved from ADB’s legal repository, covers envelope, ventilation, roof insulation, colour and mechanical systems for qualifying buildings. **Limit:** Coverage is conditional on occupancy/size and other rules; do not assume it covers every existing low-income dwelling. No cooling effect or health benefit is inferred from compliance alone. [Philippine Green Building Code 2015 original document via ADB ](https://lpr.adb.org/sites/default/files/resource/555/philippine-green-building-code.pdf.pdf)
- Official guidance or data **DOE appliance labelling/performance framework** **Fact:** The UP National Administrative Register search record lists 2024 AC labelling guidance and amendments to PELP/MEPP instruments. **Limit:** Legal-deposit/index evidence establishes listed instruments, not every operative threshold, enforcement rate or appliance performance. Full latest texts and approved labels would be worth checking for procurement. [DOE 2024 AC labelling guidance legal deposit index ](https://naro.law.upd.edu.ph/search?agency=department-of-energy&layout=cards&page=4)
- Reported, not evaluated **Quezon City Green Building Code update** **Fact:** City government reports a November 2025 code launch aligned with its climate plan and national framework. **Limit:** Announcement reviewed; full current ordinance, transition provisions and local enforcement not verified. [QC Green Building Code launch ](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/quezon-city-launches-landmark-green-building-code-to-accelerate-climate-action/)
- Reported, not evaluated **Refrigerant transition and servicing** **Fact:** UNIDO reports 2026 work with DENR/Philippine Ozone Desk on HCFC phase-out, future Kigali implementation and technical capacity for alternatives. **Limit:** Do not conflate ozone-depleting HCFC phase-out with HFC phase-down. Equipment-specific refrigerant/GWP, charge limits, safety and recovery arrangements would be worth verifying. No refrigerant retrofit is prescribed. ## Cooling design options

- Design concept **Envelope, shade and ventilation** **Costs:** **Currency:** PHP **Note:** No comparable local lifecycle cost quote verified. No USD or FX conversion is invented; a fund envelope is not a unit intervention cost. **Type:** passive **Evidence scope:** Researcher design interpretation, not a measured performance claim or endorsement. **Mechanism:** Reduce solar/radiant gain and use available air movement; ventilation would ideally consider outdoor heat, air quality and clinical infection-control needs. **Suits:** both **Suits why:** Relevant in either climate; humid conditions limit what ventilation alone can achieve. **Tradeoffs and upkeep:** Roof strength/typhoon wind, fire, damp, safe access, tenancy permission and maintenance. **Source context:** [Philippine Green Building Code 2015 original document via ADB ](https://lpr.adb.org/sites/default/files/resource/555/philippine-green-building-code.pdf.pdf)
- Design concept **Efficient fans as a complementary option** **Costs:** **Currency:** PHP **Note:** No comparable local lifecycle cost quote verified. No USD or FX conversion is invented; a fund envelope is not a unit intervention cost. **Type:** active **Evidence scope:** Researcher design interpretation, not a measured performance claim or endorsement. **Mechanism:** Move air around people rather than refrigerate the room. **Suits:** both **Suits why:** Benefit is conditional on humidity, air temperature, workload and vulnerability; not a universal safe-use prescription. **Tradeoffs and upkeep:** No power means no electric fan; repair and safe wiring matter. Do not infer illness prevention from comfort. **Source context:** [Philippine National Adaptation Plan 2023–2050 ](https://www.climate.gov.ph/public/ckfinder/userfiles/files/Knowledge/PH%20NAP%202023-2050.pdf)
- Design concept **Targeted air-conditioned refuges** **Costs:** **Currency:** PHP **Note:** No comparable local lifecycle cost quote verified. No USD or FX conversion is invented; a fund envelope is not a unit intervention cost. **Type:** active **Evidence scope:** Researcher design interpretation, not a measured performance claim or endorsement. **Mechanism:** Provide a cooled indoor area; actual humidity control and heat-load performance would be worth checking. **Suits:** both **Suits why:** Refrigeration can cool in either climate; hot-humid design requires latent-load/humidity assessment. **Tradeoffs and upkeep:** Peak electricity, refrigerant leakage/GWP, street waste heat (loop cooling), outage vulnerability, queues, access and recurring cost. **Source context:** [QC library cooling centres ](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qcpl-libraries-as-cooling-centers/)
- Design concept **Maintained trees and public shade** **Costs:** **Currency:** PHP **Note:** No comparable local lifecycle cost quote verified. No USD or FX conversion is invented; a fund envelope is not a unit intervention cost. **Type:** passive **Evidence scope:** Researcher design interpretation, not a measured performance claim or endorsement. **Mechanism:** Intercept solar radiation and, where conditions permit, support evapotranspiration. **Suits:** both **Suits why:** Shade is useful in dry and humid settings; water, canopy and airflow influence results. **Tradeoffs and upkeep:** Water scarcity, storm damage, root space, irrigation upkeep, displacement and inaccessible paths; protect tenure and users. **Source context:** [Philippine Green Building Code 2015 original document via ADB ](https://lpr.adb.org/sites/default/files/resource/555/philippine-green-building-code.pdf.pdf)
- Design concept **Evaporative cooling screening** **Costs:** **Currency:** PHP **Note:** No comparable local lifecycle cost quote verified. No USD or FX conversion is invented; a fund envelope is not a unit intervention cost. **Type:** active **Evidence scope:** Researcher design interpretation, not a measured performance claim or endorsement. **Mechanism:** Water evaporation can reduce dry-bulb temperature when sufficient evaporative potential exists. **Suits:** hot-dry **Suits why:** High humidity limits evaporation; no blanket recommendation for Philippine humid settings. **Tradeoffs and upkeep:** Additional humidity, water use, hygiene, indoor moisture and electrical/maintenance needs. Not a verified national programme. **Source context:** [PAGASA climate of the Philippines ](https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/information/climate-philippines)

## Gaps in what we found

- A consolidated, adopted national heat action plan with operational triggers, institutional mandates and itemised recurring finance was not verified; sectoral guidance and local initiatives were found.
- Final adopted heat plans and heat-specific budget lines for most selected cities were not verified.
- No independent outcome evaluation of the selected cooling-centre or awareness programmes was verified.
- Indoor/night exposure, unpaid care, wage continuity, disability access and migrant/informal-worker reach remain incompletely documented in the reviewed sources.
- No comparable local upfront/running/upkeep prices, poorest-quintile affordability assessment or heat-specific parametric policy wording verified.
- No named worker-led or disability-led implementation partner was verified in this focused collection. This is not a claim that none exists.

## Fairness and delivery checks

Design concept **Scope:** Researcher checks for local co-design, not measured claims about every Philippine programme. **Checks:**
- Include women and unpaid caregivers when schedules, access and household costs change.
- Confirm accessible travel, entrances, toilets, seating and communication with disabled and older users; a designation does not establish accessibility.
- Check whether renters can authorise roof work and whether improvements raise rents or displace users.
- Include migrants, outdoor/informal workers and people without smartphones or cooling at home; verify who is actually reached.
- Protect wage continuity and worker voice; do not use monitoring for surveillance, retaliation or unsafe output targets.
- Budget drinking water, cleaning, servicing, electricity and replacement; account for outages and competing water use.

## How the cities were chosen

**Cities selected:** 8 **Rule:** Combine named historical high population-exposure cities with a locally evidenced urban implementation case and regional heat-information/response and PAGASA station-extreme cases. **Historical research:** 2020 study covers 139 cities, largely using 2014–2016 hot-dry-season land-surface data and 2015 population indicators. Its exposure component is population density; it is not a current thermometer ranking. **City list limit:** These eight are evidence-selected priorities, not a verified national top-eight ranking in 2026. No comparable current dataset combining humidity, indoor/night heat, people exposed and vulnerability was verified. **Geographic limit:** Selected cases cover Luzon/Metro Manila and Mindanao; Visayas city coverage remains incomplete. That does not establish lower heat risk or less activity there. **Source:** [2020 Philippine city heat-health risk study ](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-15218-8)

## Who is working on it

Listing does not imply partnership, endorsement or ranking.

- **[DOST–PAGASA ](https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/)** Provides heat-index information; DOST describes its localised iHeatMAP service.
- **[Department of Health ](https://doh.gov.ph/)** DOH resources identify 2025 heat-impact guidelines and facility continuity planning.
- **[Department of Labor and Employment ](https://dole.gov.ph/)** The 2018 OSH law sets the protection framework; PMAP confirms circulation of new 2026 heat guidance. Full new advisory not reviewed.
- **[Department of Education ](https://www.deped.gov.ph/)** A schools division circulates the 2026 El Niño preparedness/protection memorandum; exact operating rules need the full document.
- **[Department of Energy ](https://www.doe.gov.ph/)** Legal-deposit records list AC labelling and performance framework instruments.
- **[Department of Public Works and Highways ](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/)** Its Green Building Code addresses cooling-related envelope and systems requirements for qualifying buildings.
- **[Climate Change Commission ](https://climate.gov.ph/)** Publishes the NAP and provides PSF adaptation-finance information.
- **[People’s Survival Fund / Board route ](https://climate.gov.ph/peoples-survival-fund)** Provides an adaptation project-finance mechanism; no heat-specific award is asserted in this review.
- **[Quezon City Government ](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/)** Reports a Heat Action Team and library cooling centres, alongside building-code and city-network activity.
- **[Philippine Red Cross ](https://redcross.org.ph/)** PRC publishes heat-emergency communications; IFRC documents REACH heat-awareness and community work.
- **[International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies ](https://www.ifrc.org/)** Its 2025 Philippines report documents REACH heat awareness and community resilience work.
- **[Zurich Climate Resilience Alliance ](https://zcralliance.org/)** The alliance’s Philippines page describes urban heat/flood resilience work through PRC.
- **[C40 Cities ](https://www.c40.org/)** Published a QC cooling-roadmap RFP; city government reports accelerator participation.
- **[Philippine Space Agency ](https://philsa.gov.ph/)** Listed as a HEATMAP-PH project partner in a project accepted in May 2026.
- **[Adamson University STARlab ](https://www.adamson.edu.ph/)** Named in the HEATMAP-PH partner list for heat-risk mapping work.
- **[Komunidad Global Pte. Ltd. ](https://www.komunidad.global/)** IFRC names Komunidad among coordination partners in early-warning work; CTCN identifies it as a private-sector climate-data organisation active in the Philippines.
- **[People Management Association of the Philippines ](https://pmap.org.ph/)** Circulated a 27 October 2026 account of DOLE’s new heat advisory to members.
