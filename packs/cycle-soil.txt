The dry-soil loop
Heat dries the soil. Dry soil can’t cool itself, so the land heats up faster.
Evidence for the cycle as a whole: established.

THE LINKS IN THE CYCLE
- heat
- nature
- Drying soils: Heat pulls moisture from the ground when crops and plants need it most. In India's 2015 heatwave, drying soils and the heat they gave off added to the second peak of the heat, though not the first. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2010.02.004] [https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180003614]

STUDIES BEHIND IT
- Seneviratne et al., Earth-Science Reviews, 2010: Investigating soil moisture–climate interactions in a changing climate: a review. Reviews the evidence that dry soils amplify hot temperatures: with less water to evaporate, more of the sun’s energy goes into heating the air. Where: Global. Limit it states: Explains a general mechanism; how strong it is varies by region and season. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2010.02.004]

ACTIONS THAT COULD INTERRUPT IT
- Deep emission cuts: Every fraction of a degree avoided weakens every loop that runs through land, ice and forests at once. It also keeps more places within the limits to adaptation, where protecting people still works.
- Keep water in the soil: Mulching, agroforestry, water harvesting and restoring degraded land help soils hold moisture and stay cooler.
