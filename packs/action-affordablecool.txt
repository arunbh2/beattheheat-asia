CONTENT

Affordable cooling for the poorest
Lifeline electricity tariffs, cool roofs and cooling suited to the local heat bring safer cooling within reach of households that cannot afford it. Which kind helps depends on the climate and the person: a fan that helps in humid heat may not help in very hot, dry air.
Who could act: Utilities and energy regulators, City and local governments, National governments.
Over what time: years.
How progress could be measured: Low-income homes with affordable cooling.

EXAMPLE
None recorded yet.

WATCH FOR
- In laboratory trials with older adults, fans slightly cooled the body in hot, humid air but raised body temperature in very hot, dry air. These were short trials, not homes. [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12308431/]

VICIOUS CYCLES IT COULD HELP INTERRUPT
- The energy-poverty trap: People who can’t afford to cool fall ill. Illness brings debt, and debt puts cooling even further out of reach.
