The melting-ice loop
Heat melts ice. Darker ground and water absorb more sunlight, and the melting speeds up.
Evidence for the cycle as a whole: established.

THE LINKS IN THE CYCLE

- nature
- Glacier and snow melt: Mountain glaciers and snowpacks that feed rivers through the dry season are shrinking. [https://lib.icimod.org/record/36322]

STUDIES BEHIND IT
- Ibel, Mölg and Sommer, The Cryosphere, 2025: Tropical glaciers on Puncak Jaya close to extinction. Puncak Jaya’s glaciers, the last in Southeast Asia, have lost more than 99% of their area since 1850 and about 65% since 2018. About 0.165 km² remained in 2024, and they will very likely be gone around 2030. Where: Puncak Jaya, Indonesia. Limit it states: One site; the 2030 date is the authors’ estimate. [https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/19/6629/2025/]
- ICIMOD, 2023: Water, ice, society, and ecosystems in the Hindu Kush Himalaya: an outlook. Glaciers lost ice 65% faster in the 2010s than in the decade before. At 1.5–2 °C of warming, glaciers across the region lose 30–50% of their volume by 2100. Where: Hindu Kush Himalaya. Limit it states: Volume losses are projections that depend on how much warming happens. [https://lib.icimod.org/record/36322]

ACTIONS THAT COULD INTERRUPT IT
- Deep emission cuts: Every fraction of a degree avoided weakens every loop that runs through land, ice and forests at once. It also keeps more places within the limits to adaptation, where protecting people still works.
- Cut black carbon: Cleaner stoves, engines and brick kilns and an end to open burning cut black carbon, the soot that darkens snow and ice.
