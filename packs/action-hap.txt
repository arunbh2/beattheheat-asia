HOW TO USE THIS PACK
Load it into a notebook tool, or give it to an assistant and ask it to answer only from this pack and to name the source behind each point. An assistant can be wrong, even with a good pack in front of it. The sources in each pack are the authority: check any answer you rely on against them.
Evidence labels used across Common Shade: known (measured in a study or set out in an official record), inferred (models and projections), uncertain (reports not yet confirmed).

QUESTIONS TO START WITH
- What is "Heat action plans", who would carry it out, and over what time?
- Which vicious cycles could it help interrupt, and how?
- What does it depend on to work, and what side effects are noted?
- What examples does the pack give, and what do their sources show?
- How would progress be measured?
- What would I need to find out before proposing this in my place?

CONTENT

Heat action plans
Early warnings, public cooling spaces and outreach to the people most at risk prevent illness before it reaches hospital.
Who could act: City and local governments, Health authorities, Weather and climate services.
Over what time: season.
How progress could be measured: Hours from heat alert to a coordinated response.

EXAMPLE
Ahmedabad launched South Asia’s first city heat action plan in 2013; by June 2026, India had more than 250. In Nepal, Dhangadhi, which passes 40 °C every year, published a heat action plan in 2025 with the Nepal Red Cross Society. In Bangladesh, Practical Action is working with communities and local authorities in Khulna to build a heat early warning system that responds to them, where none existed before. [https://www.climatecentre.org/publications/16234/dhangadhi-heat-action-plan-2025/] [https://practicalaction.org/news-stories/living-in-a-hotter-world-a-mission-to-find-hidden-urban-hotspots/]

VICIOUS CYCLES IT COULD HELP INTERRUPT
- The overwhelmed-clinic loop: Heat illness floods clinics. Overwhelmed clinics treat people later, and more cases become severe.
