Heat, health and the ability to act

# Rest without losing income

What would make advice to stop working possible to follow?

“Take a break in the shade” is good advice that many people cannot follow. Street vendors, day-wage and contract workers, home-based pieceworkers and agricultural labourers often lose earnings for every hour they stop. Whether rest is possible depends on how people are paid, who sets the schedule and the targets, whether breaks are paid, and whether reporting feeling unwell is safe.

### What the evidence says

- Guidance WHO recommends Occupational Heat Action Programmes that consider local weather, specific jobs and worker vulnerabilities, and names older workers and pregnant women among those more vulnerable. [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/workplace-heat-stress), 2025
- Implementer or industry report An apparel-industry report (The Heat is On, 2025) discusses allowances in production schedules and rest without penalty or loss of income. [Cornell Global Labor Institute and IFC, with ILO Better Work](https://betterwork.org/wp-content/uploads/Heat_Stress-Report_Oct-31_2025.pdf), 2025
- Implementer or industry report The same report addresses pregnant women and breastfeeding mothers, unpaid care work, and says potable drinking water is critical. [Cornell Global Labor Institute and IFC, with ILO Better Work](https://betterwork.org/wp-content/uploads/Heat_Stress-Report_Oct-31_2025.pdf), 2025
- Programme record Bangladesh runs a pilot Employment Injury Scheme with a central fund (ILO project record). [International Labour Organization](https://www.ilo.org/projects-and-partnerships/projects/implementation-pilot-employment-injury-scheme-eis-bangladesh)
- Worker-organisation research A Bangladeshi labour institute has called for informal-sector workers to be included in the Employment Injury Scheme (2026). [Bangladesh Institute of Labour Studies](https://bilsbd.org/news/bils-calls-to-include-informal-sector-in-the-employment-injury-scheme/), 2026
- Implementer or industry report SEWA, an organisation of women in informal work, set up a Climate Welfare Facility, documented by CGAP. [CGAP](https://www.cgap.org/research/publication/confronting-climate-and-health-nexus-lessons-self-employed-womens-association), 2026

Each line says what kind of evidence it is. Guidance recommends; a study observes; a report describes what an organisation did. None of them alone shows that a measure improved health.

## Can this worker use the protection?

Illustrative comparison. It does not calculate safe working time or set medical limits.

The physical measure stays the same: **A shaded rest area with drinking water at the worksite**. Change the arrangements around one worker and see what changes.

- **How they are paid:** Resting costs no pay, so the shade can be used when needed. Rest may cost nothing directly, but a supervisor who judges output can make breaks feel risky. *What would change it: A clear rule that breaks are paid, known to workers and supervisors.* Every minute in the shade is output lost, so many will skip rest even when it is provided. *What would change it: Piece rates or targets adjusted in heat, or a paid allowance for rest.*
- **Permission to rest:** The shade is usable when symptoms start. People who feel unwell may not ask, especially new, young or migrant workers. *What would change it: Rest as a right in heat, not a favour; supervisors accountable for allowing it.* The shade exists but is not for them. *What would change it: A written work-rest arrangement that covers everyone exposed.*
- **Output targets:** Rest does not mean falling behind. Workers make up rest time later, or skip it. *What would change it: Targets and delivery dates that move in heat, agreed with buyers where they set them.*
- **Care around the shift:** Breaks can be taken as planned. Skipping breaks to finish early becomes the only way to meet care needs. *What would change it: Hours that fit care, or childcare that stays open in heat.*
- **Getting to and from work:** Changing hours to cooler times is workable. Moving work to early morning or evening may mean travelling in the dark or with no transport at all. *What would change it: Transport at the new hours, and safe routes, before schedules change.*

## Arrangements that make rest possible

- [Heat-safe work](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#heatrules) Paid rest, adjusted targets and schedules negotiated with workers, with shade, water and sanitation.**Watch for:** Protections work only when workers can use them. In Hong Kong, some outdoor cleaners given cooling vests were stopped from wearing them because the vests covered a company logo; in Cambodia, researchers found workers whose unions had bargained over heat exposure better protected, as Oxfam America reports.
- [Income protection in heatwaves](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#heatcash) Income support or cash when heat stops work; forecast-triggered help arrives before the heat.**Watch for:** A payout trigger set too high can pay nothing: the first year of a heat insurance scheme for informal women workers in India paid nothing for that reason, until the threshold was redesigned.
- [Social protection floors](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#socialprotection) Basic income security that keeps households above the line between one hot season and the next.
- [Anticipatory action](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#anticipatory) Release money and act on the forecast, before the heatwave peaks, instead of paying for the emergency afterwards.

Read carefully

- Insurance can help, but payouts depend on triggers that may not match a worker’s loss (basis risk) and on exclusions; it does not guarantee timely or adequate protection.
- Proposed payment schemes are not entitlements until they exist in law or contract.
- Employers and, in supply chains, buyers carry responsibilities that community solidarity does not replace.

## Water, toilets and dignity

“Drink water often” is hard to follow when water is far, costly or unreliable, when toilets are unsafe, unclean, inaccessible or offer no privacy, when breaks need permission, or when schedules leave no time. Menstruation adds to the need for clean, private facilities with water and disposal. People may drink less to avoid a toilet they cannot use.

### A practical check for a worksite, market or public space

Check | What to look for |

Provision | Drinking water and toilets close to where people work, enough for the number of people, separate and private where needed. |

Opening hours | Open whenever people are working, including early and late shifts. |

Access | Free or affordable, step-free, safe to reach, usable without asking permission each time. |

Maintenance | Cleaned and refilled on a schedule, with someone paid and responsible, and supplies for menstrual needs. |

User feedback | A way for users, including women and disabled people, to report problems and see them fixed. |

## Journeys to work and care

Walking, waiting at stops, queues and crowded transport, often with children or an older relative, add heat before and after work. Moving work to cooler hours can create new barriers: travelling in the dark, no transport at those times, or safety worries that fall differently on women, gender-diverse people and disabled people. Routes need assessing locally before anyone calls them safe.

## Agricultural work and the cotton value chain

From growing and harvesting to sorting, loading, ginning and processing, tasks are shared out unevenly, and so is control over pay. Taking part in the work is not the same as owning or controlling the income from it. [Heat from field to factory](https://beattheheat.asia/cotton-and-heat/) follows cotton task by task, and [Heat inside factories](https://beattheheat.asia/factory-heat/) picks up the story indoors.

### What we know, and what we do not

**What we know**:
- WHO recommends occupational heat action programmes that consider jobs and worker vulnerability.
- Industry reporting discusses production allowances and rest without loss of income.

**What has been tested**:
- Evaluations of paid rest and adjusted targets for informal and piece-rate workers are scarce in the sources checked here.

**What remains uncertain**:
- Whether paid breaks raise the use of shade and water without lowering earnings.
- Who is left out when coverage expands on paper.

**What a useful next study would ask**: Do paid breaks improve uptake of heat protection without reducing earnings, and which groups remain excluded after nominal coverage expands?

**What needs measuring**: Breaks taken, earnings in hot weeks, symptoms reported without penalty, who is covered by name.

Not the same thing: a cooler room and better health; a service being open and people being able to use it; a warning sent and protective action taken; a committee seat and a say in decisions; reported symptoms and a diagnosis; an association and a cause.
