    The heat economy · Follow the money

    Where funding for heat protection comes from,
    and where it reaches.

    A first, partial trace: 52 pieces of funding from public announcements, followed from who gave them to the cycles they could help break. It is a sample, not the whole picture, and it will grow.

    About this section. We gathered this from public websites and reports in October 2026. Being listed does not mean we recommend, partner with or rank anyone, and none of it is financial advice. Companies describe their own products; we have not tested them, so please check details with them directly. If something is missing or out of date, tell us.

    The flow

    Look through

    Amounts are of different kinds (investment rounds, approved envelopes, credit limits, guarantees) and are not a total. Funding without a published amount drops out of this view.

    Who fundsHowWhat it buildsCycles it could help break

    The role money plays

    Our reading of each piece of funding, from its type and stage. This is inferred, not stated by the funders.

    Most of the funding we could trace is commercial growth for products with a paying customer. Fewer pieces take on risk, build evidence or build the field, and services such as paid rest or shared cool rooms rarely appear, because they are usually paid for from public budgets this trace does not cover.

    Cycles we have not yet traced funding to 5

    This may mean the money exists and we have not found it yet. If you know of funding for any of these, please tell us.

    • The dry-soil loopHeat dries the soil. Dry soil can’t cool itself, so the land heats up faster.Ideas for this cycle →
    • The melting-ice loopHeat melts ice. Darker ground and water absorb more sunlight, and the melting speeds up.Ideas for this cycle →
    • The wildfire loopHeat dries forests into fuel. Fires release carbon and strip away the trees that cool the land.Ideas for this cycle →
    • The groundwater loopDry fields need more water, so farmers pump harder, aquifers fall and the shortage deepens.Ideas for this cycle →
    • The medical-debt trapTreatment costs push families into debt. Debt removes any option to rest, and working through the heat brings more illness.Ideas for this cycle →

    The health thread

    US$3.3 bn declared · US$1.1 bn committed

    Forty-eight countries have put a price on what they need each year to protect health from climate change; international funding committed in 2024 covered about a third (Lancet Countdown 2026). Clinics, heat-illness surveillance and health warnings sit inside that gap.
