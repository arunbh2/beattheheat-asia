The lesson running through all of it: protection works when someone is clearly responsible for buying it, paying for it and keeping it working, and when the people it is for can actually use it.

## How the heat economy works

Three questions come before any product: who needs protection, who can approve it, and who can pay? They are often different people. A street vendor needs shade, but the city looks after the pavement. A worker needs rest breaks, but the employer sets the schedule.

Who needs protection, who connects, who supplies

Who needs protection

Residents, workers, farmers, pupils and patients.

And those responsible for them: employers, cities, landlords, schools and health services.

The people who need protection are often not the ones who pay for it.

Who connects them

- Worker and community organisations
- Weather services and researchers
- Rules and inspection
- Buying and project preparation
- Funders and finance

Four kinds of money

- **Customers’ payments** buy products, services and upkeep.
- **Loans and investment** pay for equipment or businesses that can earn the money back.
- **Public budgets, grants and subsidies** pay for public services and help people who can’t pay.
- **Insurance premiums** buy cover that pays out under agreed conditions.

**What counts is protection people can actually use:** they can reach it, afford it and use it safely, and it keeps working.

**Some protection needs public or shared funding.** Shade in public places, paid rest for informal workers, care for people who can’t pay, and warnings that reach everyone. These are paid for through public budgets, social protection, grants and buying together.

## Nineteen kinds of protection, and what to check

For each: what it is, who usually pays, and three questions to ask a supplier.

### Cooling and safer places

### Cool roofs

Paints, coatings and sheets that reflect sunlight, so less heat gets into the building.

**Usually paid for by:** Building owners, employers, housing programmes, and city or NGO schemes for low-income homes.

**Three things to check**

- Does the roof belong to the people who live there, or will the owner agree?
- Who recoats or repairs it when it wears, and who pays?
- Has the product been tested for how much sunlight it reflects?

**Can help break:** [The cooling trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-cooling) · [The energy-poverty trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energypoverty) · [The sleepless-nights loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-sleep) · [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-poverty)

[7 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=cool-roofs)

### Cooler building design

Shading, insulation, false ceilings, ventilation and layouts that keep buildings cool with little or no power.

**Usually paid for by:** Developers, building owners, schools, clinics and public housing budgets.

**Three things to check**

- Is it designed for this climate, including humid weather and hot nights?
- Will it work in existing homes, not only new buildings?
- Who checks that it was built as designed?

**Can help break:** [The sleepless-nights loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-sleep) · [The lost-schooling loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-school) · [The cooling trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-cooling) · [The energy-poverty trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energypoverty) · [The displacement loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-displacement)

[3 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=passive-buildings)

### Shade and greenery

Shade structures, trees and planting in streets, markets, bus stops and schoolyards, and the care that keeps them alive.

**Usually paid for by:** Cities, market and transport managers, landowners and employers.

**Three things to check**

- Is there water and a paid caretaker for the dry season?
- Could the improvement push up rents or move vendors out?
- Is the shade where people actually wait, walk and work?

**Can help break:** [The dying-green loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-deadgreen) · [The green-displacement loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-greendisplace) · [The cooling trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-cooling) · [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-poverty)

### Efficient fans

Fans that move air using much less electricity, and the people who install and repair them.

**Usually paid for by:** Households, employers, and governments or utilities buying in bulk.

**Three things to check**

- Is it affordable to buy and to run?
- Can it be repaired locally?
- Fans help less in extreme heat: what else is in place on the hottest days?

**Can help break:** [The energy-poverty trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energypoverty) · [The sleepless-nights loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-sleep) · [The cooling trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-cooling) · [The global cooling loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-global)

[9 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=efficient-fans)

### Evaporative coolers

Coolers that use water to cool the air. They work well in dry heat, but much less well in humid heat.

**Usually paid for by:** Households, workshops and factories in dry climates.

**Three things to check**

- Is the air dry enough where it will be used?
- Is there enough clean water, and who cleans the pads?
- Could it make a humid room feel worse?

**Can help break:** [The energy-poverty trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energypoverty) · [The cooling trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-cooling) · [The energy–water loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energywater)

[6 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=evaporative-cooling)

### Efficient air conditioning

Air conditioners that use less electricity, sized and installed correctly, and serviced regularly.

**Usually paid for by:** Households, building owners, employers and public institutions.

**Three things to check**

- Can the people using it afford the electricity?
- What happens in a power cut?
- Does it push heat into the street or next door?

**Can help break:** [The cooling trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-cooling) · [The global cooling loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-global) · [The energy-poverty trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energypoverty) · [The sleepless-nights loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-sleep) · [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-clinic)

[16 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=efficient-ac)

### Safe servicing of cooling gases

Trained technicians who service cooling equipment safely and collect old cooling gases (refrigerants), which warm the planet if they leak.

**Usually paid for by:** Equipment owners, manufacturers and public training programmes.

**Three things to check**

- Are the technicians trained and certified?
- Where do the old gases go?
- Is the gas used safe for this setting?

**Can help break:** [The global cooling loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-global) · [The cooling trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-cooling)

### Shared cooling networks

Central plants that cool many buildings through pipes, and companies that sell cooling as a service instead of selling machines.

**Usually paid for by:** Developers, building owners and large public buildings.

**Three things to check**

- Who can connect, and at what price?
- Does it serve buildings people rely on in a heatwave?
- What does the contract say if the service fails?

**Can help break:** [The cooling trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-cooling) · [The global cooling loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-global) · [The energy–water loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energywater)

[5 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=district-cooling)

### Cold storage for food and medicine

Cold rooms, milk chillers, fridges and transport that stop food and medicines spoiling in the heat.

**Usually paid for by:** Farmers’ groups, traders, food companies and health systems.

**Three things to check**

- Are there enough users nearby to keep it running?
- Who sets the fees, and can small farmers afford them?
- Is there backup power?

**Can help break:** [The farm-debt loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-farmdebt) · [The heat credit squeeze](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-squeeze) · [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-clinic) · [The energy–water loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energywater)

[21 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=cold-chains)

### Cooling for workers

Cooling vests, shaded and cooled rest areas, and similar protection for people working in the heat.

**Usually paid for by:** Employers and public bodies.

**Three things to check**

- Do workers also get paid rest, not just equipment?
- Is it practical for the real work?
- Who cleans, recharges and replaces it?

**Can help break:** [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-poverty) · [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-clinic)

[3 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=personal-cooling)

### Services, data and care

### Heat sensors

Instruments that measure how hot a workplace, classroom or home really is, combining heat, humidity, sun and wind (often reported as WBGT, the wet-bulb globe temperature).

**Usually paid for by:** Employers, cities, schools and research programmes.

**Three things to check**

- What will people do when the reading is high?
- Who checks the sensors are accurate?
- Have workers agreed how the data is used?

**Can help break:** [The invisible-deaths loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-invisible) · [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-clinic) · [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-poverty)

[4 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=heat-sensing)

### Heat warnings and data

Forecasts, alerts and risk maps that help people and organisations prepare for heat.

**Usually paid for by:** Businesses and insurers by subscription; public budgets for public warnings.

**Three things to check**

- Do warnings reach people without smartphones, in local languages?
- Is there a plan for what happens after the alert?
- Can the public see the data?

**Can help break:** [The reactive-spending loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-reactive) · [The invisible-deaths loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-invisible) · [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-poverty) · [The heat credit squeeze](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-squeeze)

[4 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=heat-data-warnings)

### Drinking water in the heat

Safe drinking-water points, refills and reliable supply during heatwaves.

**Usually paid for by:** Cities, water utilities, employers, schools and clinics.

**Three things to check**

- Is the water free and safe to drink?
- Does it keep working in a heatwave or power cut?
- Are there toilets nearby?

**Can help break:** [The energy–water loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energywater) · [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-clinic) · [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-poverty)

### Workplace heat safety

Help for employers to assess heat risk, plan rest breaks, train staff and follow heat rules.

**Usually paid for by:** Employers, brands that buy from them, and labour departments.

**Three things to check**

- Do all workers, including contract workers, get paid rest?
- Can workers raise concerns safely?
- Who checks that the plan is followed?

**Can help break:** [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-poverty) · [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-clinic) · [The protection-gap loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-protection)

### Heat-tolerant farming

Seeds that cope with heat, and advice that helps farmers and farm workers through hot seasons.

**Usually paid for by:** Farmers and farmers’ groups, seed companies and agriculture departments.

**Three things to check**

- Can women farmers and tenant farmers get it?
- Does it protect farm workers as well as crops?
- Is local advice available?

**Can help break:** [The farm-debt loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-farmdebt) · [The dry-soil loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-soil) · [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-poverty)

### Heat-ready health care

Training, cooling and equipment that keep clinics working in a heatwave and help staff treat heat illness early.

**Usually paid for by:** Health departments, hospitals and donors.

**Three things to check**

- Is the clinic itself cool enough for patients and staff?
- Is care for heat illness free or affordable?
- Who maintains the equipment after the first year?

**Can help break:** [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-clinic) · [The invisible-deaths loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-invisible) · [The medical-debt trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-debt) · [The reactive-spending loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-reactive)

[3 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=heat-ready-healthcare)

### Power for cooling

Controls, storage and backup that keep essential cooling running and ease pressure on the grid at peak times.

**Usually paid for by:** Utilities, building owners and facility managers.

**Three things to check**

- Are clinics, water pumps and shelters protected first in a power cut?
- Who pays for backup that is rarely used?
- Does it shift costs onto households?

**Can help break:** [The energy–water loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energywater) · [The cooling trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-cooling) · [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-clinic)

[6 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=cooling-power-flexibility)

### Paying and sharing risk

### Heat insurance

Insurance that pays out automatically when heat passes a set level, often to replace lost income. It is sometimes called parametric insurance.

**Usually paid for by:** Policyholders, employers, worker organisations, and premium support from governments or donors.

**Three things to check**

- Could someone lose income on a hot day and still get no payout?
- Who pays the premium next year?
- How fast is the money paid, and how?

**Can help break:** [The protection-gap loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-protection) · [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-poverty) · [The heat credit squeeze](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-squeeze)

[4 businesses offering this ](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/businesses/?segment=heat-insurance)

### Ways to pay for cooling

Loans, pay-as-you-go plans, repayment through the electricity bill, and group buying that spread the cost of cooling.

**Usually paid for by:** Borrowers and buyers, with lenders, utilities and sometimes subsidies.

**Three things to check**

- What is the total cost, including fees?
- What happens after a missed payment?
- Who can’t qualify, for example tenants or people without a bank account?

**Can help break:** [The energy-poverty trap](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energypoverty) · [The heat credit squeeze](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-squeeze) · [The protection-gap loop](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-protection)
