The heat economy · What could come up

Businesses and services that could
help break each cycle.

32 ideas across all 21 vicious cycles, each naming the link it would cut, who would pay and what has to be true. All are labelled design concept: they are proposals to test locally, not proven models or investment advice.

How to read this. A targeted review of published sources, checked in October 2026, not a census of the market. Listing a business, investor or programme is not endorsement, partnership or investment advice, and nothing is ranked. Company descriptions are what the company says unless labelled otherwise. Amounts of different kinds (announced, closed, disbursed; equity, loans, grants, guarantees) are never added together.

The pattern. 25 of 32 ideas need public or pooled money to protect the people they are meant for; 6 could be funded by buyers under the right conditions; and for the melting-ice cycle no direct local market exists. The recurring idea is to buy maintained protection with an accountable buyer and operator, rather than assume that selling a product finishes the job. A supplier can earn from a contract while the protection stays free for the people using it.

Loops that feed the heat itself

The global cooling loop

Cooling powered by fossil fuels adds to the warming that makes us need more cooling.

Qualified cooling commissioning and servicing cooperativesDesign conceptBuyers could fund itCuts: Cooling demand peaks → Emissions from cooling rise

Who pays, and why

Building owners, hospital chains and facility contractors; public buyers subsidise service for essential small facilities.

Buy reliable efficient operation and regulatory compliance under a contracted maintenance plan.

Who benefits

Occupants and cooling users; poorest households require a funded access tranche.

How it would earn

Installation/commissioning fee plus recurring service contract; training grants fund capability, not an assumed carbon-credit revenue stream.

Price it would need

Not priced. No defensible local-currency or USD tariff verified for this proposed bundle. Quote per unit-year by equipment class and response level. Price must cover delivery, paid labour, maintenance, verification, replacement reserves and financing where applicable; test the payer budget and an explicit affordability/subsidy ceiling before quoting. No FX conversion is applicable without an amount.

What has to be true

  • Qualified technicians and compatible parts exist.
  • Equipment-specific safety, efficiency and refrigerant practices are verified.
  • Contracts pay for preventive work, not only breakdowns.

Closest examples so far

  • India’s EESL describes efficient fans/AC retrofits and private ESCO partnerships; this supports the contracting mechanism, not proof of a new cooperative. EESL building energy ESCO partnerships 2021 (opens in a new tab) ↗
  • Smart Joules has a financed cooling-service/pay-as-you-save model for commercial facilities; household access and cooperative margins are not established. Impact Fund Denmark Smart Joules climate finance (opens in a new tab) ↗

Who would need to buy or co-own it

  • Hospital and school estate managers
  • Building-owner associations
  • Public procurement agencies

Money that could fit

  • Impact Fund Denmark (formerly IFU): loan/asset finance analogue for India, subject to contracts, diligence and eligibility; not a household grant.
  • TechEmerge Sustainable Cooling Innovation Programme: buyer matching and field validation analogue; no new open call verified.

Risks

Poor servicing, unsafe refrigerant substitution and rebound can increase energy or injury risk; avoid locking customers into proprietary parts or unaffordable renewals.

The melting-ice loop

Heat melts ice. Darker ground and water absorb more sunlight, and the melting speeds up.

Public mitigation and cryosphere knowledge: no direct local ice-rescue market establishedDesign conceptNo direct marketCuts: Glacier and snow melt → Extreme heat; no credible direct local commercial service verified that cuts this feedback

Who pays, and why

Public research and mitigation budgets; downstream utilities may separately procure hazard monitoring.

Protect public knowledge and support emissions policy and downstream adaptation; these are distinct functions.

Who benefits

Mountain and downstream communities, including those unable to pay.

How it would earn

No direct local adaptation revenue model established for the feedback edge. Research or hazard-monitoring procurement can buy useful services but must not be sold as stopping glacier melt.

Price it would need

Not applicable: no credible direct local commercial price or repayment stream established for cutting the ice–warming feedback.

What has to be true

  • Collective emissions mitigation remains necessary.
  • Adaptation monitoring is not misrepresented as restoring ice or albedo.
  • Any technical intervention would require credible environmental and governance evidence before investment.

Closest examples so far

  • ICIMOD’s HI-WISE assessment connects cryosphere change to global warming and calls for regional cooperation/adaptation. It is evidence for the public-action boundary, not an ice-restoration business analogue. ICIMOD HI-WISE assessment (opens in a new tab) ↗

Who would need to buy or co-own it

  • Public climate and mountain research agencies
  • Regional intergovernmental bodies
  • Downstream public water authorities

Money that could fit

  • Collective public mitigation and research finance, rather than a matched local heat-adaptation venture investment.

Risks

Speculative geoengineering, misleading glacier-rescue credits, tourism displacement and diversion of adaptation money from community protection.

If buyers cannot fund it

No direct market case is established for the target edge. Public mitigation and cryosphere research are collective functions; downstream warning/adaptation contracts address consequences, not this feedback.

The invisible-deaths loop

Heat deaths go uncounted, so heat protection goes unfunded, so more people die uncounted.

Public heat-health data and worksite exposure serviceDesign conceptNeeds public or pooled moneyCuts: Heat deaths → Heat deaths under-recorded

Who pays, and why

Health authorities, labour inspectorates and public research infrastructure budgets.

Buy interoperable reporting, calibrated exposure data and usable decision support.

Who benefits

Residents and workers whose exposures and health outcomes are poorly represented.

How it would earn

Public infrastructure/service procurement with open specifications and safe aggregate data; protected clinical records remain under authorised control.

Price it would need

Not priced. No defensible local-currency or USD tariff verified for this proposed bundle. Quote per reporting network-year plus calibrated site maintenance; do not pay per attributed death. Price must cover delivery, paid labour, maintenance, verification, replacement reserves and financing where applicable; test the payer budget and an explicit affordability/subsidy ceiling before quoting. No FX conversion is applicable without an amount.

What has to be true

  • Clinical attribution and excess-mortality analysis have competent public/statistical oversight.
  • WBGT is measured/maintained correctly and not substituted with heat index or satellite surface temperature.
  • Data triggers action and budgets through named authorities.

Closest examples so far

  • NCDC documents digital heat-related illness/death surveillance and clinical classification materials in India. NCDC current heat-health programme (opens in a new tab) ↗
  • WHO guidance links surveillance to action and continuous review; neither source proves a private integrated WBGT/reporting contract works. WHO heat-health action planning 2026 (opens in a new tab) ↗

Who would need to buy or co-own it

  • Public-health surveillance teams
  • Labour authorities and worker organisations
  • Public meteorological/statistical agencies

Money that could fit

  • Public health procurement and research/implementation grants; no matched G3/G4 commercial investor for a free statutory service verified.

Risks

Automatic heat-death attribution, coercive worker tracking, privacy breaches, commercial data monopolies, unmaintained sensors and counting without action; no promise that better counts automatically unlock budgets.

If buyers cannot fund it

Direct user payments cannot be assumed to fund protection. Secure an appropriated public budget or explicit grant/pooled subsidy, including recurrent operations; pay the operator for a specified service, not for a hypothetical social return.

The reactive-spending loop

Stretched budgets skimp on prevention. Emergencies multiply, and costly emergency response stretches budgets further.

Who benefits

Residents and institutions needing protection, especially settings without user-fee revenue.

  • Country/municipal approval and procurement authority are confirmed.
  • Lifecycle budgets, community consent and maintenance are included.

The protection-gap loop

As insurers retreat, public budgets absorb the losses, protection is cut, and the risk drives insurers further away.

Licensed residual-risk pooling with prevention and cash fallbackDesign conceptNeeds public or pooled moneyCuts: Insurance losses and retreat → Public budgets strained

Who pays, and why

Public/worker/employer pooled funds, donor premium support and licensed risk carriers under separate contracts.

Fund frequent losses transparently, purchase suitable residual cover and maintain prevention and beneficiary servicing.

Who benefits

Workers and vulnerable communities; universal protection cannot rely on voluntary premiums from the poorest.

How it would earn

Audited service fee for public/worker-governed pool administration; licensed insurer prices risk, while public/donor funds carry explicit subsidy and frequent-loss layers.

Price it would need

Not priced. No defensible local-currency or USD tariff verified for this proposed bundle. Quote separate administration, prevention, cash reserve and actuarially priced premium; no single all-in invented tariff. Price must cover delivery, paid labour, maintenance, verification, replacement reserves and financing where applicable; test the payer budget and an explicit affordability/subsidy ceiling before quoting. No FX conversion is applicable without an amount.

What has to be true

  • Insurance licensing, fiduciary governance and independent actuarial review are secured.
  • Cash assistance has funded fallback for basis risk and non-trigger losses.
  • Renewal, exclusions, claims arrival and insurer/public contingent liabilities are disclosed.

Closest examples so far

  • CGAP describes SEWA’s Climate Welfare Facility and challenges of trigger mismatch, delays and distribution. This is a close institutional analogue, not a tested new cross-place pool. CGAP SEWA climate-health lessons (opens in a new tab) ↗
  • Swiss Re’s 2024 account documents separate insurance and income-support layers; no assumption that all later SEWA arrangements have the same carrier or terms. Swiss Re SEWA parametric income protection India (opens in a new tab) ↗

Who would need to buy or co-own it

  • Worker/community organisations as co-owners
  • Public social-protection and finance authorities
  • Licensed insurers/reinsurers

Money that could fit

  • Swiss Re — public-sector heat risk-transfer route: licensed risk structuring/capacity analogue, not philanthropic funding. Premium support, cash assistance and service fees require separate public/donor commitments.

Risks

Unfunded guarantees, correlated losses, unaffordable premiums, basis risk, moral hazard narratives that remove prevention, elite capture and a pool mistaken for free insurance capacity.
