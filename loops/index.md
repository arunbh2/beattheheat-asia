# Vicious cycles of extreme heat, and where they can be broken

21 vicious cycles in which extreme heat feeds itself, traps people or overwhelms institutions, with where each one plays out in Asia, the evidence, and the actions that could break it.

## Loops that feed the heat itself

### The global cooling loop

Cooling powered by fossil fuels adds to the warming that makes us need more cooling.

**A shop owner in a fast-growing city**The summers feel longer, so she buys a second air conditioner for the shop. It keeps her customers comfortable, and the power that runs it adds a little more to the warming that made her buy it.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Extreme heat → Energy → Cooling demand peaks → Emissions from cooling rise → back to the start. Evidence: well established.

**What this cycle costs (World):** 3×. The energy used for cooling could triple by 2050 as air conditioners grow from 1.6 billion to 5.6 billion, unless they become more efficient. [International Energy Agency, 2018](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-cooling)

**In Southeast Asia (Southeast Asia):** 40m → 300m. air conditioners in Southeast Asia between 2017 and 2040, half of them in Indonesia, unless efficiency improves. Cooling could then make up as much as 30% of the region’s peak electricity demand. [International Energy Agency, 2019](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-cooling-in-southeast-asia)

**Where it plays out:** Fast-growing cities across South and Southeast Asia, where air-conditioner ownership is rising quickly.

**Where it breaks:** [Clean, efficient cooling](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#cleancool)

**Solutions to try:** [Minimum energy performance standards for cooling appliances](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#system_minimum_energy_performance_standards) (cuts the electricity each air conditioner needs, so the same cooling adds less warming); [Transition to purpose-designed low-GWP room AC](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#system_low_gwp_room_ac_transition) (moves to refrigerants that warm the planet far less when they leak); [Solar-reflective roof coatings](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_cool_roof_coating) (keeps buildings cooler without power, so less cooling is needed at all); [National cooling action plans and cross-sector implementation roadmaps](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#system_national_cooling_action_plans) (plans the country’s cooling demand as a whole, so efficiency and clean power keep pace with growth). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-global).

- Southeast Asia’s air conditioners could rise from 40 million in 2017 to 300 million in 2040, half of them in Indonesia. Without more efficient units, cooling could drive as much as 30% of the region’s peak electricity demand. [International Energy Agency, 2019](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-cooling-in-southeast-asia)
Agency report, with projections · Southeast Asia · Region-wide energy model. Limit: A projection to 2040 under stated policies, not a forecast; it changes with efficiency rules.
- The world’s stock of air conditioners is set to grow from 1.6 billion to 5.6 billion by 2050, and the energy they use to triple, unless efficiency improves. [International Energy Agency, 2018](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-cooling)
Agency report, with projections · Global · Global energy model. Limit: A projection to 2050 that assumes current trends; better efficiency changes it.

### The dry-soil loop

Heat dries the soil. Dry soil can’t cool itself, so the land heats up faster.

**A farmer on rain-fed land**After weeks without rain, the soil in his fields cracks and the afternoons feel hotter than they used to. Without moisture to cool it, the ground bakes faster each day, and the next dry spell starts sooner.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Extreme heat → Nature and ecosystems → Drying soils → back to the start. Evidence: well established.

What this cycle costs is not yet measured.

**Where it plays out:** Rain-fed farmland on the Indian subcontinent and in mainland Southeast Asia during the hot, dry pre-monsoon months.

**Where it breaks:** [Deep emission cuts](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#emissions), [Keep water in the soil](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#soilwater)

**Solutions to try:** [Organic residue mulch for cooler crop root zones](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_organic_soil_mulch) (keeps moisture in the soil, so it can keep cooling itself); [Shade-tree agroforestry for crops](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_crop_agroforestry) (shades the ground and holds water in the land); [Permeable pavements retaining rainfall for evaporative surface cooling](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_permeable_pavements) (in towns, lets rain soak in instead of running off, so the ground stays moist longer). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-soil).

- Reviews the evidence that dry soils amplify hot temperatures: with less water to evaporate, more of the sun’s energy goes into heating the air. [Seneviratne et al., Earth-Science Reviews, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2010.02.004)
Peer-reviewed review · Global · Review of many studies. Limit: Explains a general mechanism; how strong it is varies by region and season.

### The melting-ice loop

Heat melts ice. Darker ground and water absorb more sunlight, and the melting speeds up.

**A herder high in the mountains**The snowfield her family has used for generations shrinks a little each year. The bare rock that replaces it holds the sun’s heat, and the edge of the ice retreats faster than she remembers.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Extreme heat → Nature and ecosystems → Glacier and snow melt → back to the start. Evidence: well established.

**What this cycle costs (Hindu Kush Himalaya):** 30–50%. of the region’s glacier ice could be gone by 2100 at 1.5–2 °C of warming, after melting 65% faster in the 2010s than the decade before. [ICIMOD, 2023](https://lib.icimod.org/record/36322)

**In Southeast Asia (Papua, Indonesia):** 99%. of the glacier area on Puncak Jaya, Southeast Asia’s last tropical ice, has gone since 1850. Only about 0.17 km² was left in 2024, and it will very likely disappear around 2030. [Ibel, Mölg and Sommer, The Cryosphere, 2025](https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/19/6629/2025/)

**Where it plays out:** The Hindu Kush Himalaya, whose snow and ice feed rivers such as the Indus, Ganges and Brahmaputra.

**Where it breaks:** [Deep emission cuts](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#emissions), [Cut black carbon](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#blackcarbon)

**Solutions to try:** Only cutting the warming slows this loop. These cut cooling’s own share of it; nothing local stops the melting. [Minimum energy performance standards for cooling appliances](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#system_minimum_energy_performance_standards) (cuts the electricity, and so the emissions, each air conditioner needs); [Transition to purpose-designed low-GWP room AC](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#system_low_gwp_room_ac_transition) (moves to refrigerants that warm the planet far less); [National cooling action plans and cross-sector implementation roadmaps](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#system_national_cooling_action_plans) (plans cooling growth so it adds as little warming as possible). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-ice).

- Puncak Jaya’s glaciers, the last in Southeast Asia, have lost more than 99% of their area since 1850 and about 65% since 2018. About 0.165 km² remained in 2024, and they will very likely be gone around 2030. [Ibel, Mölg and Sommer, The Cryosphere, 2025](https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/19/6629/2025/)
Peer-reviewed study · Puncak Jaya, Indonesia · One glacier field, satellite and field data. Limit: One site; the 2030 date is the authors’ estimate.
- Glaciers lost ice 65% faster in the 2010s than in the decade before. At 1.5–2 °C of warming, glaciers across the region lose 30–50% of their volume by 2100. [ICIMOD, 2023](https://lib.icimod.org/record/36322)
Agency assessment · Hindu Kush Himalaya · Region-wide synthesis. Limit: Volume losses are projections that depend on how much warming happens.

### The wildfire loop

Heat dries forests into fuel. Fires release carbon and strip away the trees that cool the land.

**A forest guard in the dry season**The undergrowth he walks through each morning is drier than it used to be. When a fire comes, it takes the trees that kept the valley cool, and the land beneath them dries out further.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Extreme heat → Nature and ecosystems → Wildfires → back to the start. Evidence: well established.

**What this cycle costs (World):** +50%. more extreme wildfires are projected by the end of the century, and up to 14% more by 2030. [UNEP and GRID-Arendal, 2022](https://www.unep.org/resources/report/spreading-wildfire-rising-threat-extraordinary-landscape-fires)

**In Southeast Asia (Indonesia):** US$16.1 billion. was the cost to Indonesia of the forest and peatland fires of June to October 2015. [World Bank, 2016](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/776101467990969768/pdf/103668-BRI-Cost-of-Fires-Knowledge-Note-PUBLIC-ADD-NEW-SERIES-Indonesia-Sustainable-Landscapes-Knowledge-Note.pdf)

**Where it plays out:** The dry-season burning belt of northern Thailand, Myanmar and Laos, and Indonesia’s peatlands.

**Where it breaks:** [Deep emission cuts](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#emissions), [Fire-smart landscapes](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#firesmart)

**Solutions to try:** Breaking this loop needs forest and fire management, which this library does not yet cover. These protect people while it turns. [Locally calibrated impact-based heat-warning triggers](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_impact_based_warning) (warns people early, based on what the heat will do locally, not just the temperature); [Pre-agreed heat anticipatory action and forecast finance](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_forecast_based_finance) (releases agreed money before the worst days, rather than after); [Pooled pre-arranged finance for heat action](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_pooled_risk_finance) (shares the cost of bad years across places, so one disaster does not empty a budget). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-fire).

- Indonesia’s forest and peatland fires of June to October 2015 cost the country an estimated US$16.1 billion. [World Bank, 2016](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/776101467990969768/pdf/103668-BRI-Cost-of-Fires-Knowledge-Note-PUBLIC-ADD-NEW-SERIES-Indonesia-Sustainable-Landscapes-Knowledge-Note.pdf)
Agency report, economic estimate · Indonesia · National, one fire season (2015). Limit: One exceptional year, in an El Niño; costs in other years differ.
- Climate change and land-use change are making wildfires worse. Extreme fires are projected to rise by up to 14% by 2030 and 50% by the end of the century. [UNEP and GRID-Arendal, 2022](https://www.unep.org/resources/report/spreading-wildfire-rising-threat-extraordinary-landscape-fires)
Agency report, with projections · Global · Global assessment. Limit: Global projections; not broken down for Asian countries.

## Loops that break the systems we rely on

### The cooling trap

Air conditioning heats the street, and a hotter city needs even more air conditioning.

**A street vendor in a dense neighbourhood**Air conditioners hum above her stall, blowing their heat into the lane where she works. The hotter the lane gets, the more of them switch on, and the afternoon becomes harder to bear.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Cooling demand peaks → Urban heat islands → back to the start. Evidence: well established.

**What this cycle costs (Singapore):** Up to 7 °C. warmer: how much hotter Singapore’s commercial centre has measured than its outskirts on calm, clear nights. [Cooling Singapore, citing Chow and Roth, 2006](https://www.thegpsc.org/sites/gpsc/files/cooling_singapore_-_digital_urban_climate_twin.pdf)

**Where it plays out:** Dense, high-rise city cores such as Hong Kong, Bangkok and Metro Manila.

**Where it breaks:** [Cool by design](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#coolbydesign), [Green, cool cities](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#greencity)

**Solutions to try:** [Solar-reflective roof coatings](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_cool_roof_coating) (keeps rooms cooler without power, so less air conditioning runs); [Street trees and retained mature canopy](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_street_trees) (shades and cools the street the air conditioners heat); [Efficient BLDC ceiling fans](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#active_bldc_ceiling_fan) (gives comfort for a fraction of the power and waste heat of air conditioning); [Coordinated fans and higher-setpoint air conditioning](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#active_fan_assisted_ac_control) (lets air conditioning run at a higher setting with a fan, cutting its load). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-cooling).

- In a Phoenix heatwave, waste heat from air conditioners raised night-time air temperatures by more than 1 °C in parts of the city. [Salamanca et al., Journal of Geophysical Research, 2014 (summary by Arizona State University)](https://news.asu.edu/content/excess-heat-air-conditioners-causes-higher-nighttime-temperatures)
Peer-reviewed modelling study · Phoenix, United States · One city, simulated heatwave. Limit: A desert city outside Asia; the size of the effect in humid Asian cities is not measured here.
**Our reading:** We use it for the mechanism, not the number: waste heat from cooling warms the street outside.
- China, India and Indonesia are expected to account for half of the world’s air conditioners by 2050. [International Energy Agency, 2018](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-cooling)
Agency report, with projections · Global · Global energy model. Limit: A projection to 2050 that assumes current trends.
- Chennai’s built-up area more than doubled between 2000 and 2020, and night-time land surface temperatures in its urban areas run about 2 °C higher than on its peri-urban edges. [Tamil Nadu State Planning Commission, 2024](https://spc.tn.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/Heat_Mitigation_Strategy.pdf)
Government strategy · Chennai and Tamil Nadu, India · State-wide, satellite land surface temperature. Limit: Land surface temperature, not the air temperature people breathe; the two differ.
- Singapore’s commercial centre has measured up to about 7 °C warmer than its outskirts on calm, clear nights. [Cooling Singapore, citing Chow and Roth, 2006](https://www.thegpsc.org/sites/gpsc/files/cooling_singapore_-_digital_urban_climate_twin.pdf)
Research programme summary of a peer-reviewed study · Singapore · City-wide measurements. Limit: The 7 °C is a maximum on calm, clear nights, not a typical night.

### The energy–water loop

Water systems need power and power plants need water. In a heatwave, each failure deepens the other.

**A water-pump operator on the city’s edge**During a heatwave the power fails, so the pumps stop and taps run dry. Meanwhile the power station upstream is short of water to cool itself, and the cuts come more often.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Power cuts and grid stress → Water shortages → Power plants cut output → back to the start. Evidence: well established.

**What this cycle costs (India):** 14 TWh. of electricity lost in India in 2016 because thermal power plants ran short of water. [World Resources Institute, 2018](https://www.wri.org/insights/40-indias-thermal-power-plants-are-water-scarce-areas-threatening-shutdowns)

**In Southeast Asia (Vietnam):** US$1.4 billion. was the World Bank’s early estimate of what northern Vietnam’s power cuts cost in May and June 2023, about 0.3% of GDP, after heatwaves pushed up demand and dried hydropower reservoirs. [World Bank estimate, reported by AFP via The Manila Times, 2023](https://manilatimes.net/2023/08/12/business/foreign-business/power-crisis-cost-vietnam-14b-wb/1904920/amp)

**Where it plays out:** Coal-fired power plants along water-stressed rivers in India.

**Where it breaks:** [Power that doesn’t need water](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#drypower), [Backup power for water](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#pumpbackup)

**Solutions to try:** [Solar/hybrid pumping with water storage for resilient water service](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#system_water_power_resilience) (keeps water pumping on solar or stored power when the grid fails); [Heatwave grid-readiness planning and emergency resource coordination](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#system_heatwave_grid_readiness) (prepares the grid and its water supply before the heat season); [Demand response and scheduled flexible loads to relieve heat-period peaks](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#system_demand_flexibility) (moves flexible loads off the peak, so the grid is less likely to fail); [Minimum energy performance standards for cooling appliances](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#system_minimum_energy_performance_standards) (lowers the peak itself, by making every air conditioner use less). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-energywater).

- Heatwaves in May and June 2023 pushed up demand while hydropower reservoirs ran low, and rolling blackouts hit northern Vietnam. The World Bank’s early estimate put the cost at about US$1.4 billion, roughly 0.3% of GDP. [World Bank estimate, reported by AFP via The Manila Times, 2023](https://manilatimes.net/2023/08/12/business/foreign-business/power-crisis-cost-vietnam-14b-wb/1904920/amp)
News report of a World Bank early estimate · Northern Vietnam · National, one season (2023). Limit: An early estimate reported in the press; drought and demand both played a part.
- 14 of India’s 20 largest thermal utilities shut down at least once for lack of water between 2013 and 2016. Water shortages cost 14 TWh of power in 2016. [World Resources Institute, 2018](https://www.wri.org/insights/40-indias-thermal-power-plants-are-water-scarce-areas-threatening-shutdowns)
Research institute analysis · India · 20 largest thermal utilities, 2013–2016. Limit: Data from before 2017; plants and water rules have changed since.

### The groundwater loop

Dry fields need more water, so farmers pump harder, aquifers fall and the shortage deepens.

**A smallholder with a borewell**As the fields dry, he pumps for longer each season. His neighbours do the same, the water table drops, and the well has to be dug deeper just to keep the crop alive.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Water shortages → Irrigation demand soars → Groundwater over-pumped → back to the start. Evidence: well established.

**What this cycle costs (India):** 109 km³. of groundwater lost from Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana in six years (2002–2008), mainly to irrigation, while rainfall stayed close to normal. [Rodell, Velicogna and Famiglietti, Nature, 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08238)

**In Southeast Asia (Mekong delta, Vietnam):** 2.5 cm+. a year: how fast parts of the Mekong delta are sinking as its aquifers are pumped down. [Minderhoud et al., Environmental Research Communications, 2020](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2515-7620/ab5e21)

**Where it plays out:** Punjab and Haryana in northwest India, where groundwater tables have been falling for decades.

**Where it breaks:** [Smart irrigation and groundwater rules](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#irrigate)

**Solutions to try:** [Organic residue mulch for cooler crop root zones](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_organic_soil_mulch) (keeps water in the root zone, so fields need less pumping); [Shade-net structures for crop microclimate protection](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_crop_shade_nets) (cuts the heat and water stress on crops); [Shade-tree agroforestry for crops](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_crop_agroforestry) (shades crops and soil, so less water is lost). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-pumping).

- Groundwater pumping has sped up the sinking of the Mekong delta, to more than 2.5 cm a year in some areas. [Minderhoud et al., Environmental Research Communications, 2020](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2515-7620/ab5e21)
Peer-reviewed modelling study · Mekong delta, Vietnam · Delta-wide model. Limit: Sinking rates vary across the delta; the top rate is not everywhere.
- Satellites show Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana lost 109 km³ of groundwater between 2002 and 2008, mainly to irrigation, while rainfall stayed close to normal. [Rodell, Velicogna and Famiglietti, Nature, 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08238)
Peer-reviewed study, satellite data · Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana, India · Three states, 2002–2008. Limit: An older period; it measures water lost, not the heat link itself.
**Our reading:** The heat link is ours: hotter, drier seasons raise the pull on groundwater that this study measures.

[Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-pumping) · [Watch the film (0:49)](https://beattheheat.asia/media/loops/pumping.mp4)

### The dying-green loop

Heat and drought kill city trees. Without their shade the streets get hotter, and hotter streets kill more trees.

**A neighbourhood gardener**She waters the street trees she planted years ago, but a hot, dry summer still kills some of them. Without their shade the pavement heats up, and the trees that remain struggle even more.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: City trees and parks die → Urban heat islands → back to the start. Evidence: emerging (each link is evidenced; the full cycle less so).

**What this cycle costs (164 cities worldwide):** 76%. of urban tree and shrub species in 164 cities could face temperatures beyond their natural range by 2050, up from 56% today. Risk is highest in cities such as New Delhi and Singapore. [Esperon-Rodriguez et al., Nature Climate Change, 2022](https://www.vibrantcitieslab.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/climate-change-increases-global-risk-to-urban-forests.pdf)

**Where it plays out:** Hot, fast-growing cities such as New Delhi and Singapore, where every urban tree species studied is at risk from rising heat.

**Where it breaks:** [Keep city trees alive](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#keepgreen)

**Solutions to try:** [Street trees and retained mature canopy](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_street_trees) (protects mature canopy and plants trees that can survive the heat); [Community maintenance arrangements backed by public/service support](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_community_maintenance) (pays for watering and care through the dry season, when young trees die); [Blue-green river corridors and naturalised drainage landscapes](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_bluegreen_corridors) (keeps water in the city’s landscape, so trees are not left dry); [Permeable pavements retaining rainfall for evaporative surface cooling](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_permeable_pavements) (lets rain reach tree roots instead of draining away). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-deadgreen).

- Of 3,129 tree and shrub species in 164 cities, 56% already face temperatures beyond those in their natural range, rising to about 76% by 2050 under a mid-range warming scenario. Risk is highest in low-latitude cities such as New Delhi and Singapore. [Esperon-Rodriguez et al., Nature Climate Change, 2022](https://www.vibrantcitieslab.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/climate-change-increases-global-risk-to-urban-forests.pdf)
Peer-reviewed study · 164 cities in 78 countries · 3,129 species. Limit: Compares climate to each species’ natural range; trees can survive outside it with care.
- Drought stops urban trees from cooling through transpiration. In one Berlin park more than 500 of 870 new trees died within ten years despite watering, and Berlin cut about 44,000 street trees between 2012 and 2019 while planting about 18,000. [Hansen et al., chapter 10 in Nature-Based Solutions for Cities, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4337/9781800376762)
Book chapter, peer-reviewed · Berlin, Jakarta, Melbourne and Santiago · Four city cases. Limit: The tree figures are from Berlin; Asian evidence on tree loss in heat is thinner.
**Our reading:** We take the lesson that new trees need years of water and care, wherever they are planted.
- Tree shade sharply lowers the radiant heat people feel by day, which is the cooling lost when trees die. [Middel et al., Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10230332-grades-shade)
Peer-reviewed study · Tempe, United States · Street-level measurements. Limit: A desert city; humid cities gain less from shade alone, though shade still cuts radiant heat.

### The overwhelmed-clinic loop

Heat illness floods clinics. Overwhelmed clinics treat people later, and more cases become severe.

**A nurse at a primary health centre**On the hottest days the waiting room fills with people who are dizzy, vomiting or confused. With so many waiting, some are seen late, and by then their illness has become more serious.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Heatstroke and heat exhaustion → Health systems overwhelmed → back to the start. Evidence: emerging (each link is evidenced; the full cycle less so).

**What breaking it saves (Ahmedabad, India):** 1,190. deaths a year were estimated to be avoided after Ahmedabad’s heat action plan prepared hospitals and warned the public. [Hess et al., Journal of Environmental and Public Health, 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6236972/)

**Where it plays out:** District and city hospitals during South Asia’s pre-monsoon heatwaves.

**Where it breaks:** [Heat action plans](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#hap), [Heat-ready health services](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#readyclinics)

**Money arranged before heat:** 13 kinds of insurance, pooled funds, credit and forecast-based finance name this cycle. [Paying when heat strikes](https://beattheheat.asia/paying-when-heat-strikes/) asks of each whether it paid, and when.

**Solutions to try:** [Heat-ready clinic protocols and staff capability](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_clinic_readiness) (prepares staff and protocols to treat heat illness early, before it turns severe); [Heat surge staffing, triage and referral coordination](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_surge_referral) (adds staff, triage and referral for heat surges); [Resilient power for health-facility cooling and cold-chain loads](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#system_health_critical_power) (keeps the clinic cool and its medicines safe when the power fails); [Locally calibrated impact-based heat-warning triggers](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_impact_based_warning) (warns ahead, so fewer people reach the clinic already severely ill). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-clinic).

- Heat-related deaths among people over 65 reached a record 167% above the 1990s level in 2023. [Lancet Countdown, The Lancet, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(24)01822-1)
Peer-reviewed global assessment · Global · Global indicators. Limit: A global figure; it does not give Asia’s share.
- After Ahmedabad’s heat action plan prepared hospitals and warned the public, an estimated 1,190 deaths a year were avoided. [Hess et al., Journal of Environmental and Public Health, 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6236972/)

## Loops that trap people

### The poverty–heat trap

People who can’t afford to stop working fall ill. Illness costs income, and lost income makes stopping even less affordable.

**A day-wage construction worker**He knows the midday sun is dangerous, but stopping means losing a day’s pay. When the heat makes him ill, he loses several days instead, and the next week he cannot afford to rest at all.

### The heat credit squeeze

Heat empties shops and small firms miss repayments. Banks tighten credit, so firms can’t afford to adapt, and the next heatwave hits harder.

**The owner of a small corner shop**In a long heatwave, customers stay indoors and sales fall, so she misses a loan repayment. The lender is more cautious next time, and she cannot borrow to buy a cooler or shade for the shop.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Small businesses close → Loan repayments slip → Borrowing costs rise → back to the start. Evidence: emerging (each link is evidenced; the full cycle less so).

**What this cycle costs (Delhi, India):** 49%. of 721 Delhi street vendors surveyed lost income in the 2024 heatwaves, and 80% saw fewer customers. [Greenpeace India and National Hawkers Federation, 2024](https://www.greenpeace.org/india/en/press/16737/new-report-by-greenpeace-india-and-national-hawkers-federation-reveals-harsh-heatwave-toll-on-street-vendors/)

**Where it plays out:** Market towns and city high streets in heat-exposed districts, and lenders whose loans are collected door to door, as in microfinance across northern and central India.

**Where it breaks:** [Heat-smart lending](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#heatlending)

**Money arranged before heat:** 2 kinds of insurance, pooled funds, credit and forecast-based finance name this cycle. [Paying when heat strikes](https://beattheheat.asia/paying-when-heat-strikes/) asks of each whether it paid, and when.

**Solutions to try:** [Heat-relevant housing and cooling credit with technical appraisal](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_heat_smart_lending) (offers credit for cooling and heat-proofing, with a check that it will work); [Public shade structures, sails and market/queue canopies](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_public_shade_structures) (shades markets and queues, so customers keep coming); [Parametric heat insurance for income shocks](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_parametric_heat_insurance) (pays out when heat crosses a set level, so repayments are not missed); [A fallback funding layer when an index misses real harm](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_basis_risk_fallback) (covers the harm an index misses, so a missed payout does not become default). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-squeeze).

- Across 2.7 million firms in 23 advanced economies, heat cut the productivity of small firms while medium and large firms showed no loss, and the largest grew revenue about 0.6 points faster than small firms after hot years. Losses lasted up to two years. Restaurants and hotels were hit hard, partly through lower demand. [OECD Economics Department, Working Paper No. 1828, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1787/19d94638-en)
Working paper · 23 advanced economies · 2.7 million firms. Limit: Rich economies; small informal firms in Asia are not covered.
- Loan-level bank data from Mexico show that ten extra days of extreme heat in a quarter raise small-business loan delinquency by about 4%, mainly among firms that depend on local customers. Banks then raise rates, ask for more collateral and lend less for two to three quarters. [Aguilar-Gomez et al., summarised in VoxEU, 2024](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/heatwaves-ripple-financial-system)
Research summary of a peer-reviewed study · Mexico · National bank loan data. Limit: Outside Asia; how Asian banks respond is not yet measured.
**Our reading:** We read it as a likely pattern for Asian lenders, to be checked against their own loan books.
- India’s microfinance industry body said loan quality worsened in April–June 2024, partly because heatwaves cut borrowers’ incomes and lenders’ collections. [Sa-Dhan, reported by Business Standard, 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/micro-loan-asset-quality-deteriorates-in-april-june-quarter-sa-dhan-124082700721_1.html)
News report of an industry statement · India · National microfinance sector, one quarter. Limit: Heat is one named cause among several, not measured on its own.
- A caution for lenders: in Cambodia, researchers found that borrowing to get through climate shocks often delayed the harm rather than removing it, leaving households more indebted for the next shock. [King’s College London, 2023](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/new-study-finds-that-microfinance-is-exacerbating-climate-precarity-and-harm-in-cambodia)
Research summary · Cambodia · Fieldwork with borrowers. Limit: About climate shocks in general, not heat alone.
- Of 721 Delhi street vendors surveyed in April and May 2024, 80% saw fewer customers during heatwaves and 49% lost income. [Greenpeace India and National Hawkers Federation, 2024](https://www.greenpeace.org/india/en/press/16737/new-report-by-greenpeace-india-and-national-hawkers-federation-reveals-harsh-heatwave-toll-on-street-vendors/)
Survey · Delhi, India · 721 street vendors. Limit: Self-reported, from one city in one season.
- Car dealers said heat cut showroom footfall by about 15% in June 2024, leaving them with 62–67 days of unsold stock against a recommended three weeks. A sign of where to look; the link to their loans is not yet measured. [Federation of Automobile Dealers Associations, reported by Orissa Post, 2024](https://www.orissapost.com/passenger-vehicle-retail-sales-dip-7-in-june-as-heatwave-curtails-showroom-visits/amp/)
News report of industry data · India · National car retail, one month. Limit: Dealers’ own account; the link to their loans is not measured.

### The farm-debt loop

A failed harvest pushes farmers into debt. Debt leaves less for seed, water and fertiliser, so the next harvest fails more easily.

**A wheat farmer before the harvest**A heatwave at the wrong moment shrinks the grain, and the harvest does not cover what he borrowed. With the loan still open, he buys less seed and fertiliser, and the next crop is more fragile.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Failing harvests → Farmers fall into debt → back to the start. Evidence: emerging (each link is evidenced; the full cycle less so).

**What this cycle costs (Philippines):** −10%. rice yield for each 1 °C rise in growing-season night temperature, at the International Rice Research Institute’s farm in the Philippines. [Peng et al., PNAS, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0403720101)

**Where it plays out:** Rain-fed farming districts of India, Pakistan and Bangladesh, where a failed season often means borrowing from moneylenders.

**Where it breaks:** [Crop insurance and fair credit](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#farmcredit)

**Money arranged before heat:** 2 kinds of insurance, pooled funds, credit and forecast-based finance name this cycle. [Paying when heat strikes](https://beattheheat.asia/paying-when-heat-strikes/) asks of each whether it paid, and when.

**Solutions to try:** [Shade-net structures for crop microclimate protection](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_crop_shade_nets) (protects crops from heat damage); [Organic residue mulch for cooler crop root zones](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#passive_organic_soil_mulch) (keeps crop roots cooler and moist); [Parametric heat insurance for income shocks](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_parametric_heat_insurance) (pays out after a heat-hit season, so farmers need not borrow for seed); [Pre-agreed heat anticipatory action and forecast finance](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_forecast_based_finance) (releases money before the damage, while it can still protect the harvest). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-farmdebt).

- Heat raises suicide rates in India only during the growing season, when it also cuts crop yields, pointing to farm income losses. The findings have been debated. [Carleton, PNAS, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5565417/)
Peer-reviewed study · India · National, 1967–2013. Limit: An association that has been debated; it does not prove debt is the cause.
- Days above 34 °C make wheat in northern India age early, and crop models may underestimate yield losses at +2 °C by as much as 50%. [Lobell, Sibley and Ortiz-Monasterio, Nature Climate Change, 2012](https://fsi.stanford.edu/node/26622)
Peer-reviewed study, satellite data · Northern India · Wheat region, satellite records. Limit: About wheat only; it shows how models can miss losses.
- At the International Rice Research Institute’s farm in the Philippines, rice yields fell by about 10% for each 1 °C rise in growing-season night temperature, while daytime highs had little effect. [Peng et al., PNAS, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0403720101)
Peer-reviewed field study · Los Baños, Philippines · One research farm, 1992–2003. Limit: One site; later studies find varied effects across regions.

## Loops inside institutions

### The invisible-deaths loop

Heat deaths go uncounted, so heat protection goes unfunded, so more people die uncounted.

**A district health officer**She knows that heat played a part in many deaths this summer, but few were recorded that way. With so few counted, it is hard to make the case for next year’s heat budget.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Heat deaths → Heat deaths under-recorded → Heat protection underfunded → back to the start. Evidence: emerging (each link is evidenced; the full cycle less so).

**What this cycle costs (India):** 48,385 vs 185. suspected heatstroke cases against confirmed heatstroke deaths in India in 2024, up to 28 July: most of the toll goes uncounted. [Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, reply in India’s Rajya Sabha, 2024](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/750681/1/PQ_265_06082024_U1693_p358_p362.pdf)

**Where it plays out:** Much of Asia, where death certificates rarely record heat as a cause.

**Where it breaks:** [Count every heat death](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#countdeaths), [Cooling centres and check-ins](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#coolingcentres)

**Solutions to try:** [Timely heat-illness and death surveillance](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_heat_illness_surveillance) (counts heat illness and deaths as they happen); [All-cause and excess-mortality analysis for heat attribution](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_excess_mortality) (measures the full toll by comparing deaths with a normal year); [Heat-death investigation and consistent certification](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_heat_death_certification) (records heat as a cause of death consistently); [Dedicated and traceable heat operating budgets](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_dedicated_heat_budgets) (turns the counted toll into money set aside for protection). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-invisible).

- Suspected heatstroke cases rose from 4,481 in 2022 to 48,385 by 28 July 2024, while confirmed heatstroke deaths rose only from 33 to 185. [Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, reply in India’s Rajya Sabha, 6 August 2024](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/750681/1/PQ_265_06082024_U1693_p358_p362.pdf)
Government data · India · National surveillance. Limit: Counts what gets reported; reporting improves over time, so part of the rise is better counting.
- In 2020, India’s crime records bureau recorded 530 heatstroke deaths while the disaster agency recorded four. Different systems count differently, which makes the full toll hard to see. [Associated Press via PBS NewsHour, 2024](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/india-likely-undercounts-heat-related-deaths-tempering-its-response-officials-say)
News report · India · National, two agencies’ records. Limit: Compares two official counts in one year.
- Death certificates usually record only the immediate cause, such as heart failure, so heat as the trigger goes unrecorded. [Down To Earth, 2024](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/lost-in-the-heat-the-critical-miscalculation-in-indias-heatwave-mortality-data)
News analysis · India · National. Limit: Describes how deaths are recorded; it does not estimate the missing number.
- Between 1 March and 26 July 2026, India’s heat surveillance, covering more than 51,000 reporting units, logged 4,853 heatstroke cases but only 20 heatstroke deaths. [Ministry of Home Affairs, reply in India’s Lok Sabha, reported by Down To Earth, 2026](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/india-updates-disaster-list-to-include-heatwaves-and-lightning-after-finance-commission-push)
Government data, reported by the press · India · More than 51,000 reporting units. Limit: A part-season count; it shows the gap, not the true toll.
- Monitors at 24 indoor and outdoor workplaces in Singapore found workers could face more heat stress than nearby national weather stations reported. [NUS Project HeatSafe, 2024](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/PRESS-RELEASE_Project-HeatSafe-18MAR2024-FOR-IMMEDIATE-RELEASE.pdf)
University research release · Singapore · 24 workplaces. Limit: One city; site conditions vary.

### The reactive-spending loop

Stretched budgets skimp on prevention. Emergencies multiply, and costly emergency response stretches budgets further.

**A municipal budget officer**With money stretched, the cooling shelters and water points she planned are cut back. When the heatwave arrives, emergency spending takes even more of the budget, and prevention is cut again.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Public budgets strained → Heat protection underfunded → Emergency services stretched → back to the start. Evidence: emerging (each link is evidenced; the full cycle less so).

**What breaking it saves (Developing countries):** US$3–16 billion. in losses could be avoided every year by spending US$800 million on early warning systems in developing countries. [Global Commission on Adaptation, 2019](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/7713887e-5c0c-5fce-9dd0-c18a8875736c)

**In Southeast Asia (Bangkok, Thailand):** Up to 0.7%. of Bangkok’s economy is what heat deaths cost, an estimated 593 a year in 2016–18. The upper estimate is more than the city’s whole public health budget for 2020. [Park and Rubinyi, World Bank working paper, 2026](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/d8ba6f53-d778-47d2-b4a6-37dff7db2466/full)

**Where it plays out:** Disaster budgets designed for floods and cyclones rather than heat.

**Where it breaks:** [Dedicated heat budgets](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#heatbudget), [Anticipatory action](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#anticipatory)

**Money arranged before heat:** 27 kinds of insurance, pooled funds, credit and forecast-based finance name this cycle. [Paying when heat strikes](https://beattheheat.asia/paying-when-heat-strikes/) asks of each whether it paid, and when.

**Solutions to try:** [Dedicated and traceable heat operating budgets](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_dedicated_heat_budgets) (sets aside and tracks money for prevention); [Pre-agreed heat anticipatory action and forecast finance](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_forecast_based_finance) (agrees in advance what money is released, and when); [Heat action plans with a seasonal responsibility matrix](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_heat_action_plan) (assigns who does what before each heat season); [Recognise heat in disaster-funding rules and eligibility](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_disaster_law_fund_access) (lets disaster funds be spent on heat). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-reactive).

- About 593 deaths a year in Bangkok in 2016–18 were attributable to heat. Valued at 0.1–0.7% of the city’s economy, the upper estimate exceeds the city’s 2020 public health budget. [Park and Rubinyi, World Bank working paper, 2026](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/d8ba6f53-d778-47d2-b4a6-37dff7db2466/full)
Working paper · Bangkok, Thailand · One city, 2016–2018. Limit: An estimate from a statistical model; the value depends on how a life is priced.
- US$800 million spent on early warning systems in developing countries could avoid US$3–16 billion in losses every year. [Global Commission on Adaptation, 2019](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/7713887e-5c0c-5fce-9dd0-c18a8875736c)
Commission report · Developing countries · Global estimate. Limit: A broad benefit estimate covering many hazards, not heat alone.
- Direct response typically gets over half of wildfire spending, while planning and prevention get less than 1%. [UNEP and GRID-Arendal, 2022](https://www.unep.org/resources/report/spreading-wildfire-rising-threat-extraordinary-landscape-fires)
Agency report · Global · Global assessment. Limit: About wildfire budgets; we use it as a pattern for heat.
- Of 37 heat action plans reviewed, 11 discussed funding, and 8 of those simply asked departments to find their own money. Only two included a vulnerability assessment. [Centre for Policy Research, 2023](https://cprindia.org/briefsreports/how-is-india-adapting-to-heatwaves-an-assessment-of-heat-action-plans-with-insights-for-transformative-climate-action/)
Research institute review · India · 37 heat action plans. Limit: Plans as of 2023; several have been updated since.
- In August 2026 India added heatwaves to its notified natural calamities, so national and state disaster response funds can pay for heatwave relief. Before, states could spend only up to 10% of their response fund on locally declared disasters such as heat. Heat mitigation projects are funded separately, from disaster mitigation funds. [Ministry of Home Affairs, reply in India’s Lok Sabha, reported by Down To Earth, 2026](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/india-updates-disaster-list-to-include-heatwaves-and-lightning-after-finance-commission-push)
Government statement, reported by the press · India · National. Limit: A rule change; how money flows in practice is still to be seen.

### The protection-gap loop

As insurers retreat, public budgets absorb the losses, protection is cut, and the risk drives insurers further away.

**A small business owner seeking insurance**After several bad years, insurers no longer cover heat losses for businesses like hers. When a heatwave hits, she turns to public relief, which strains the budget meant for protection.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person.

The cycle: Insurance losses and retreat → Public budgets strained → Heat protection underfunded → back to the start. Evidence: emerging (each link is evidenced; the full cycle less so).

**What this cycle costs (Asia):** 92%. of Asia’s US$65 billion in natural catastrophe losses in 2025 was uninsured, falling on households, businesses and governments. [Swiss Re Institute sigma, reported by Global Reinsurance](https://www.globalreinsurance.com/home/swiss-re-sigma-protection-gap-laid-bare-as-asian-cat-losses-hit-65bn/1458440.article)

**Where it plays out:** Fast-growing Asian economies where few households or small businesses carry insurance.

**Where it breaks:** [Dedicated heat budgets](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#heatbudget), [Shared risk pools](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#riskpools)

**Money arranged before heat:** 27 kinds of insurance, pooled funds, credit and forecast-based finance name this cycle. [Paying when heat strikes](https://beattheheat.asia/paying-when-heat-strikes/) asks of each whether it paid, and when.

**Solutions to try:** [Pooled pre-arranged finance for heat action](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_pooled_risk_finance) (shares heat risk across places, so no single budget carries it alone); [Targeted premium support and renewal finance](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_premium_support) (helps those most at risk keep their cover); [A fallback funding layer when an index misses real harm](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_basis_risk_fallback) (backs up index cover when it misses real harm); [Recognise heat in disaster-funding rules and eligibility](https://beattheheat.asia/solutions/#nonphysical_disaster_law_fund_access) (opens public disaster funds to heat losses). A suggested match, not a measured effect on the cycle. [Business ideas for this cycle](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-economy/opportunities/#loop-protection).

- Only about 8% of Asia’s US$65 billion in natural catastrophe losses in 2025 was insured. The rest fell on households, businesses and governments. [Swiss Re Institute sigma, reported by Global Reinsurance](https://www.globalreinsurance.com/home/swiss-re-sigma-protection-gap-laid-bare-as-asian-cat-losses-hit-65bn/1458440.article)
Industry research, reported by the press · Asia · Region-wide, 2025. Limit: Covers all natural catastrophes, not heat alone; heat losses are rarely insured at all.
- In developing markets with low insurance cover, the protection gap is driven by unaffordable premiums, limited awareness and weak distribution, and heat’s drag on growth and household incomes could shrink the customer base further. [Global Asia Insurance Partnership, 2026](https://www.gaip.global/publications/physical-risks-on-the-insurance-industry/)
Industry research report · Asia · Region-wide review. Limit: A qualitative assessment; it does not measure the whole cycle in any one place.
**Our reading:** We read it as the cycle on the map: repeated heat losses squeeze incomes, cover becomes harder to afford, and more of the loss falls on households and public budgets.
