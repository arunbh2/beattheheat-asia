    Who could help a household through a hot season

    Heat cuts the hours people can safely work and brings costs for care. Public schemes can cushion part of that shock. 55 schemes in 6 countries, what is known about who they reach, and where nothing responds.

    This is not an eligibility check. Rules change often and differ by place, so each scheme shows the date its status applies to; check with the scheme before relying on it. Households appear here only in aggregate.

    Why heat reaches household budgets

    Heat cuts the hours people can safely work, and illness brings costs and lost days. On the map these are Lost work hours and wages, Medical costs and debt and Heatstroke and heat exhaustion. Public schemes can cushion part of that shock. What the studies show:

    • Bangladesh: UNCERTAIN, self-reported: the simulation delivered a BDT 2,400 top-up. It demonstrates a tested delivery route, not a measured reduction in heat illness or lost income. unclear Limit: No independent causal heat-outcome assessment; report date and citation year differ.

    What a map could show, and how it could mislead

    A map of heat and public support is tempting, and easy to misread. Very little of this data is both openly licensed and shares a place code, so it cannot yet be joined into one map that holds up. A few cautions apply everywhere:

    • Unknown is not zero: a place without data is not a place without need or support.
    • Counts from different schemes cannot be added up to a number of protected households, because people can be counted in several.
    • No single protection score is offered here, because the parts measure different things.
    • A scheme can exist on paper while delivery differs from place to place.

    Open questions

    What the evidence has not yet answered. Study sizes are illustrative, not power calculations.

    Bangladesh

    • Does an allowance top-up before a heatwave reduce lost work and medical spending?

      What is known: A Rajshahi delivery simulation was reported. [B5]

      A study that could answer it: Randomised phased invitation or top-up timing across comparable neighbourhood clusters, with aggregate income, medical-spending and timing outcomes. (Illustrative design: 40-60 clusters; final size from power calculations.)

    • At which access step do heat-exposed urban households stop receiving support?

      What is known: HIES measures past receipt but not a linked eligibility-to-receipt ladder. [B3]

      A study that could answer it: A repeated representative household survey linked only to consented, de-identified process totals; publish aggregate transitions and reasons. (Illustrative design: 1,500-2,000 households across several cities.)

    • How often does public support meet the costs of medically diagnosed heat illness?

      What is known: A historical SSK financial-risk evaluation exists; heat-specific claims were not reported. [B7]

      A study that could answer it: Prospective facility-cost study comparing public benefits, out-of-pocket costs, referral and foregone care across one hot season. (Illustrative design: 10-15 facilities; power and case-volume check first.)

    • Does assisted application improve timely receipt among migrants and women outdoor workers?

      What is known: Delivery organisations describe support, but no comparable independent local effect was read. [B6, B12, B13]

      A study that could answer it: Randomise outreach timing among consenting community clusters and track aggregate application, receipt, cost and delay. (Illustrative design: 30-50 clusters over one application cycle.)

    • Unmeasured steps: Adequate, On time When heat arrives, how much of a household's lost earnings and care costs does an allowance or top-up cover, and does it arrive before the hottest days?

      What is known: Implementer reports count households that received cash in Rajshahi and Dhaka. None of the sources checked measures how far the money went, or when it arrived compared with the heat.

      A study that could answer it: Follow recipient households through one hot season, recording lost work days, care costs and payment dates against heat-alert dates. (Illustrative: about 300 households in two cities over one hot season)

    • Unmeasured steps: Aware, Eligible, Documented, Applied, Enrolled How many heat-exposed urban households know of an allowance, qualify for it, hold the documents, apply and are enrolled?

      What is known: None of the sources checked measures these steps for the schemes on this page in Bangladesh.

      A study that could answer it: A household survey in hot wards that asks the same people about each step, for the same schemes. (Illustrative: about 1,000 households in four wards)

    India

    • At which access step do heat-exposed informal-worker households lose the most potential support?

      What is known: Separate administrative stocks and a small commissioned facilitation assessment; no complete representative ladder.

      A study that could answer it: Voluntary repeated survey before, during and after a hot season, publishing only aggregate group and step results. (About 1,500 households across six districts; final size depends on detectable differences.)

    • Does receiving an existing transfer before peak heat improve coping compared with later receipt?

      What is known: Entitlements and heat earnings-loss evidence; little verified payment-timing evidence.

      A study that could answer it: Evaluate an authorised timing change with aggregate earnings, care costs and borrowing outcomes. (About 2,000 households across 40-60 clusters, subject to power analysis.)

    • How much of household heat-illness cost is met through PM-JAY and free public care?

      What is known: Hospital-cover rules and service policies; no verified heat-specific cost pathway.

      A study that could answer it: Consent-based care-cost study across public and empanelled facilities, publishing aggregate results. (About 600 episodes across 12 facilities in one season.)

    • Does assisted scheme navigation improve timely receipt during a hot season?

      What is known: Delivery models and commissioned assessment; no verified heat-specific causal estimate.

      A study that could answer it: Phased rollout of additional support against existing services, measuring receipt and timing; providers on equal terms. (About 60 communities and 1,800 households; subject to power analysis.)

    • Can public works cushion heat income loss while reducing exposure?

      What is known: Historical learning protection evidence; no verified 2026-framework heat evaluation.

      A study that could answer it: Evaluate adjusted hours, rest and water provision alongside work access and wage arrival. (About 50 worksites across four districts in one hot season.)

    • Unmeasured step: Adequate Is what a household receives from rural work, pensions or food rations enough to cover a hot season's lost earnings and care costs?

      What is known: Documents, applications, enrolment, receipt and payment timing are measured for some schemes, such as fund transfer orders for rural work. No source checked measures whether support is adequate in a hot season.

      A study that could answer it: Compare the support received with heat-season losses in the same households, in a hot month and a cooler one. (Illustrative: about 600 households in three districts)

    • Unmeasured steps: Aware, Eligible How many informal workers exposed to heat know which schemes they could use, and how many of them qualify?

      What is known: Later steps are measured for some schemes; awareness and eligibility are not, in the sources checked.

      A study that could answer it: Pair a short awareness survey with an eligibility screen for the same workers, at worksites and markets. (Illustrative: about 800 workers in two cities)

    Indonesia

    • What share of diagnosed work-related heat claims reach benefit payment?

      What is known: JKK benefits exist; heat-specific acceptance and delay were not verified. [I5, I6]

      A study that could answer it: Prospective multi-site claims pathway study, publishing aggregate submitted, accepted and paid counts and time intervals. (Illustrative: several provinces over two hot seasons; sample based on case volume.)

    • Does contribution support keep informal workers insured through the hot season?

      What is known: A registration experiment and a new contribution-saving route exist. [I16, I9]

      A study that could answer it: Cluster randomisation of assisted contribution-saving access, measuring active coverage, care costs and retention in aggregate. (Illustrative: 40-60 clusters; final size from power calculations.)

    • Does a pre-heat top-up reduce income loss, debt and delayed care?

      What is known: PKH delivery and general economic evaluations exist, without a verified heat effect. [I4, I17]

      A study that could answer it: Pre-specified phased top-up trial using forecasts, with household outcomes reported only in aggregate. (Illustrative: two hot seasons across 40-60 clusters.)

    • Which heat-related crop losses meet AUTP damage rules and receive timely support?

      What is known: Drought insurance terms and extension routes are published. [I11]

      A study that could answer it: Crop-season panel combining weather, field loss assessment and anonymised aggregate claim outcomes, without publishing farm locations. (Illustrative: 1,000-1,500 insured and comparable uninsured farm households.)

    • Unmeasured steps: On time, Adequate Do cash transfers reach families before or after the hottest weeks, and is the amount enough to cover lost work?

      What is known: Government reports count the families paid each quarter, without payment dates set against heat or amounts set against losses.

      A study that could answer it: Link payment dates with heat records and a short household survey on lost work and spending. (Illustrative: about 500 families in two provinces over one year)

    • Unmeasured steps: Aware, Eligible, Documented, Applied Where do informal workers stop between hearing of worker insurance and applying for it?

      What is known: An evaluation in Bandung and Medan measured enrolment: registration help raised it by 3.5 percentage points and full subsidy by 18.6, while information alone had no measured effect. Awareness, documents and applications were not measured as separate steps.

      A study that could answer it: Follow the same workers through awareness, documents and application, with and without help to register. (Illustrative: about 1,000 workers in two cities)

    Nepal

    • Does pre-heat cash reduce lost earnings and medical spending for outdoor-worker households?

      What is known: An urban heat protocol exists without a completed impact evaluation in the sources read. [N12]

      A study that could answer it: Randomised or pre-specified phased delivery across comparable neighbourhoods, recording timing, income, care and coping costs in aggregate. (Illustrative design: 40-60 clusters over two hot seasons; power calculation before final sampling.)

    • Which step limits qualifying SSF access among informal workers?

      What is known: Pre-launch awareness data and later registration reports describe different periods. [N7, N8]

      A study that could answer it: Follow a representative cohort from information through contribution qualification and benefit receipt, with anonymised aggregate reporting. (Illustrative design: 1,500-2,000 workers across several municipalities.)

    • How often does public insurance meet the full cost of heat-related care?

      What is known: Enrolment is measured; current heat-specific claims and complete costs are not. [N4, N5, N22]

      A study that could answer it: Prospective facility and household cost study with diagnosed heat cases, matched service records under consent, and published aggregate results only. (Illustrative design: 10-15 facilities across a hot season; case-volume check before sizing.)

    • Does a supported transfer process reduce allowance interruption after migration?

      What is known: A documented allowance transfer route exists; actual seasonal continuity was not measured. [N1]

      A study that could answer it: Phased assistance through wards, comparing aggregate transfer completion, payment interruption and travel cost. (Illustrative design: 20-30 ward pairs over one renewal and payment cycle.)

    • Unmeasured steps: Adequate, On time Do allowances arrive on time and cover what a hot season costs older people and outdoor-worker households?

      What is known: Awareness of the Social Security Fund, enrolment and receipt are measured in the sources checked; the adequacy and timing of allowances in hot months are not.

      A study that could answer it: Record payment dates and heat-season costs for allowance households in the Tarai. (Illustrative: about 400 households in four municipalities)

    • Unmeasured steps: Eligible, Documented, Applied Among people who qualify for an allowance, how many hold the documents and have applied?

      What is known: In the sources checked, eligibility, documents and applications are not measured as separate steps.

      A study that could answer it: A records review with local governments that counts eligible people, documents held and applications made. (Illustrative: six wards in three municipalities)

    Singapore

    • How often do work-related heat illnesses move from report to accepted and paid WICA claim?

      What is known: Heat is recognised and benefit types are published; heat-specific process counts were not verified. [S13, S14, S15]

      A study that could answer it: Secure aggregate reporting of notified, filed, accepted and paid claims with time intervals and broad worker categories. (Illustrative: all available cases across several hot seasons; no individual case details published.)

    • Does PCP access reduce total costs and delayed care during hot periods for covered migrant workers?

      What is known: A patient-experience survey exists, without a heat-specific causal comparison. [S17]

      A study that could answer it: Prospective hot-season facility and worker survey with pre-specified comparison periods, full care costs and aggregate results. (Illustrative: 10-15 clinics; final size based on expected heat case volume.)

    • How consistently do workers receive and use paid heat-rest breaks?

      What is known: Official guidance recognises specified breaks as paid work; implementation outcomes were not verified. [S14]

      A study that could answer it: Confidential worker and employer survey plus aggregate work-schedule observations across heat levels, without individual tracking. (Illustrative: 40-60 worksites across several exposed sectors.)

    • Which access steps explain non-receipt of temporary support during heat-related household income loss?

      What is known: National ComCare receipt counts and assisted-application routes exist. [S1, S29]

      A study that could answer it: Representative repeated household survey measuring awareness through on-time receipt, including explicit residency eligibility checks. (Illustrative: 1,500-2,000 households over two hot seasons.)

    • Unmeasured steps: Adequate, On time Does temporary support cover the income lost when heat cuts work, and how soon does it arrive?

      What is known: Counts of households receiving ComCare support and of workers receiving the Workfare Income Supplement are published; adequacy and timing against heat-related income loss are not, in the sources checked.

      A study that could answer it: A survey of support recipients in outdoor work that compares lost earnings in hot months with the support received and when it came. (Illustrative: about 300 households)

    • Unmeasured steps: Aware, Eligible, Documented, Applied, Enrolled Do migrant workers know which medical and injury protections apply to heat illness, and do they start a claim?

      What is known: None of the sources checked measures awareness, applications or claims started among migrant workers for heat illness.

      A study that could answer it: Short surveys at dormitories and worker centres, with partner organisations, followed by records of claims started. (Illustrative: about 500 workers)

    Thailand

    • Does Section 40 sickness compensation reach informal workers diagnosed with heat illness?

      What is known: Current benefit changes are published; heat-specific applications and payments were not verified. [T3]

      A study that could answer it: Prospective claims-pathway study tracking aggregate awareness, documentation, accepted claims, value and delay. (Illustrative: several provinces over two hot seasons; final size based on case flow.)

    • Does heat-triggered insurance preserve earnings and reduce debt without changing essential work hours?

      What is known: A worker-network pilot and partner reports exist without an independent impact estimate. [T15, T16]

      A study that could answer it: Pre-registered phased rollout with agreed comparison clusters, measuring income, care costs, rest and coping in aggregate. (Illustrative: 40-60 clusters across two hot seasons.)

    • How different are social-insurance coverage maps based on residence and employer registration?

      What is known: SSO metadata describes different location concepts by section. [T11]

      A study that could answer it: Secure institutional aggregation of both location definitions; publish only province-level differences and uncertainty. (Illustrative: one year of all available provincial aggregate records.)

    • How much heat-related crop loss is met by declared-disaster relief, and how quickly?

      What is known: Crop-relief rates, documents and procedure are published. [T5]

      A study that could answer it: Crop-season household panel and administrative aggregate audit of declarations, assessed losses, payment dates and recovery. (Illustrative: 1,000-1,500 households across several agroclimatic areas.)

    • Unmeasured steps: Aware, Eligible, Documented, Applied, Enrolled, Received, Adequate For older people and informal workers, which is the first step of access where support stops in a hot season?

      What is known: None of the sources checked measures any step of access for the schemes on this page in Thailand.

      A study that could answer it: A household survey that follows the old-age allowance and Section 40 through all eight steps, for the same people. (Illustrative: about 1,000 households in three provinces)

    • Unmeasured step: On time Does crop relief after a declared disaster arrive in time to matter for the next season?

      What is known: None of the sources checked measures when relief reaches farmers compared with the loss.

      A study that could answer it: Compare declaration, claim and payment dates for crop relief across districts. (Illustrative: records from ten districts over two seasons)

    What it took to add this

    1. The question. Which public schemes could carry a household through a hot season, in each country researched?
    2. The search. 131 sources were gathered for checking.
    3. The checking. Each source was opened and read for the terms and numbers it was cited for: 98 confirmed, and 33 could not be (16 blocked automated reading, 14 did not respond, 3 did not show the expected text).
    4. What we set aside. 101 items that rested on what could not be confirmed were set aside, regraded or reworded, each with its reason.
    5. What is still open. Questions remain for every step of access nobody has measured.
