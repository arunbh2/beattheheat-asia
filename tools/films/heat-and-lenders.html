Paying for protection

When heat
reaches
the bank

How a heatwave travels from a day’s wages to a lender’s books.

1 · The household

A hotter day, a smaller wage

Heat cuts working hours. Incomes fall first for vendors, farmers and daily-wage workers.

2 · The repayment

The instalment waits

Repayments slow, and field visits stop in the heat. In 2025, heatwaves cut what lenders collected in the June quarter.

3 · The lender

The strain moves up

Lenders face a cash crunch and costlier funding. About 60% of India’s ₹3.81 trillion microfinance portfolio is in farming and allied work.

4 · The rules

Regulators are moving

The Reserve Bank of India now treats heatwaves as a prudential risk. Disclosure of heat exposure is still voluntary for some.

What could help, in combination

Three ways to pay for protection

Parametric insurancePays out automatically when heat crosses a set line, so informal workers can afford to stop.
Blended financePatient capital takes early risk, so private money can follow, for example into storage that cuts heat spoilage.
Green and resilience bondsLarge, long-term capital for resilient buildings, water and city infrastructure.

And what makes it work

Data, terms and pooled projects

Shared heat-risk dataOne standard picture of exposure, place by place.
Lending tied to heat outcomesBetter terms for cooler rooms and cool roofs, measured.
City-level platformsMany small projects, pooled so banks can finance them.
