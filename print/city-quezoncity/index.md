# Extreme heat in Quezon City

A Metro Manila city that has set up its own heat action team and opened libraries as cooling centres.

**Heat type.** Mostly humid heat.

**Heat action plan** Parts in place, no full plan foundIn June 2026 the city government set up a City Heat Action Team and designated chief heat action officers. Through the C40 Cool Cities Accelerator it has committed to clear heat action leadership, a stronger early warning system and wider access to cooling during heat emergencies within two years. 32 air-conditioned community libraries serve as cooling centres.

## Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Quezon City

Cycle | Why here | One action that breaks it |

**The energy-poverty trap** | Heat indices averaged 42 to 44 °C in 2024 and 2025, and extreme heat drives up electricity use that strains family budgets. | Free care for heat illness |
**The energy–water loop** | Prolonged dry periods strain water access and raise the cost of essential goods and services. | Power that doesn’t need water |

## Who heat hits hardest here

- Older people
- People with disabilities or long-term illness

## Four questions for Quezon City’s heat plan

- Is it funded, with a named owner?
- Does it assess who is most vulnerable, and where they live?
- Does it have legal backing, so departments are required to act?
- Does it count indoor and night-time heat, not only daytime highs?
