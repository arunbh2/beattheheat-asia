Four heats in a home

A heat warning gives one number. A family under a tin roof lives with four, and the worst of it often comes after dark.

  1. Outdoor air. The temperature a weather station measures, in the shade. It is the number in almost every heat warning, but not what people feel inside a tin-roofed room.
  2. The roof. Sun-baked sheet metal gets far hotter than the air. Roofs of informal homes in Mumbai and Pune measured above 50 °C when the air was about 30 °C.
  3. Radiant heat. A hot ceiling gives off heat onto the people below, like standing near a stove, so a room can feel hotter than its air. This is what mean radiant temperature measures.
  4. Indoor air. The air inside, held in by thin walls, few windows and crowded rooms.
At night. After sunset the street starts to cool, but thin-roofed homes cool slowly. In low-income homes measured in Dhaka, Delhi, Faisalabad and rural Maharashtra, temperatures in tin-roofed houses swung 3–4 °C more over the month than the outdoor air, and stayed warmer indoors because they cooled so slowly at night.
7.6 °Chotter, at most, for people in Ahmedabad’s informal settlements than for people in formal housing, in a survey of 860 households.
Up to 9 °Chotter indoors than outside, in pilot measurements in informal settlements in Kenya, Tanzania and Zimbabwe.
2–5 °Ccooler indoors under reflective roof paint than under ordinary roofs, in Ahmedabad’s informal settlements.

Indoors is not always hotter at midday: in an experiment in The Gambia, metal-roofed houses were about 1.4 °C cooler than the outdoor shade by day, but warmer at night. The danger is nights that do not cool.

Eight questions for your heat action plan

  • Does your heat warning count hot nights, not only daytime highs?
  • Do you know which neighbourhoods live under metal, asbestos or plastic roofs?
  • Have you measured indoor and night-time temperatures in a sample of those homes? Low-cost loggers can do it.
  • Is there a cool-roof programme for informal settlements, and does it reach renters?
  • Are cooling spaces open in the evening and at night, near where people sleep?
  • Do outreach teams check on people who are alone, older or ill, including after dark?
  • Do housing upgrades protect tenants from rent rises and eviction?
  • Is the plan funded, with a named owner? In a review of 37 Indian heat action plans, 11 discussed funding.
For heat plans. Heat warnings measure the street. Heat plans should protect the room: count indoor and night-time heat, warn for hot nights, and pay for cooler roofs where people sleep.
