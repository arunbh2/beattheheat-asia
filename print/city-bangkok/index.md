# Extreme heat in Bangkok

A tropical megacity where heat costs lives, livelihoods and a share of the city’s economy.

**Heat type.** Mostly humid heat.

**Heat action plan** Heat plan in forceFor the 2026 hot season Bangkok opened 304 cooling centres in schools, health centres and district offices. The city’s own Urban Heat Management Action Plan for 2026 has 22 indicators and a committee chaired by a deputy governor; its targets include cooling rooms in 80% of schools and public health service centres, at least two cooling points in every district, and home visits to at least 5,000 vulnerable households a year during heat alerts. A 2026 financing strategy with UNDP costs the plan at about THB 146 million to set up and THB 10.8 million a year to run, mostly from existing city budgets, and notes that running costs are not always covered.

## Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Bangkok

Cycle | Why here | One action that breaks it |

**The poverty–heat trap** | Nearly 80% of informal workers surveyed cut their hours in the heat; 75% of home-based workers lost a day’s work a month to heat illness. | Heat-safe work |
**The reactive-spending loop** | Heat deaths, about 593 a year, cost up to 0.7% of the city’s economy: more than its 2020 public health budget. | Dedicated heat budgets |
**The sleepless-nights loop** | One of the tropical cities whose nights stay hot. | Cool nights and support |
**The green-displacement loop** | Fieldwork is tracing how green urban renewal affects the city’s lower-income residents. | Green without pushing people out |

## Who heat hits hardest here

- Workers in heat-exposed jobs
- People in informal settlements

## Four questions for Bangkok’s heat plan

- Is it funded, with a named owner?
- Does it assess who is most vulnerable, and where they live?
- Does it have legal backing, so departments are required to act?
- Does it count indoor and night-time heat, not only daytime highs?
