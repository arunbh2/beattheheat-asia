Extreme heat in Bangkok

A tropical megacity where heat costs lives, livelihoods and a share of the city’s economy.

Heat type. Mostly humid heat.
Heat action plan Heat plan in force

For the 2026 hot season Bangkok opened 304 cooling centres in schools, health centres and district offices, under a heat management plan developed with the World Bank.

Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Bangkok

CycleWhy hereOne action that breaks it
The poverty–heat trapNearly 80% of informal workers surveyed cut their hours in the heat; 75% of home-based workers lost a day’s work a month to heat illness.Heat-safe work
The reactive-spending loopHeat deaths, about 593 a year, cost up to 0.7% of the city’s economy: more than its 2020 public health budget.Dedicated heat budgets
The sleepless-nights loopOne of the tropical cities whose nights stay hot.Cool nights and support
The green-displacement loopFieldwork is tracing how green urban renewal affects the city’s lower-income residents.Green without pushing people out

Who heat hits hardest here

  • Workers in heat-exposed jobs
  • People in informal settlements

Four questions for Bangkok’s heat plan

  • Is it funded, with a named owner?
  • Does it assess who is most vulnerable, and where they live?
  • Does it have legal backing, so departments must act?
  • Does it count indoor and night-time heat, not only daytime highs?
