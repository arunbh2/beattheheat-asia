Cooling options: Homes

What to build or change, what keeps it working, the traps to avoid and rough costs.

Protects. Sleep, care work, and the older people, young children and sick people who spend the hottest hours indoors.
Key caution. Check the roof type, fire safety, tenure and ventilation before any retrofit.

What the evidence says

  • In 16 Ahmedabad slum homes measured in September 2017, rooms under a modular roof averaged about 33.8 °C against 36.8 °C under asbestos sheets. White paint on tin did not beat asbestos or concrete in the same comparison: measure before you promise.
  • In a two-year trial in Burkina Faso, cool roofs lowered indoor temperatures by 1.2 °C in tin-and-mud homes and 1.7 °C in tin-roofed homes, and residents’ heart rates fell. The same team began a year-long trial in about 400 Ahmedabad homes in 2025.
How you will know it works. Indoor temperature at 3 p.m. in treated and untreated homes, logged through the season
Five traps that waste cooling money: Buying before diagnosing · Built, with no one to keep it · Green without water · Promising degrees you haven’t measured · A list of activities, not a model.
Kitchen heat. Cooking adds heat on top of the weather, often in the smallest room and usually for women; in Nepal’s Madhesh Province it falls hardest on women in the poorest single-room homes.

Ask: How much heat does the stove give off, and does anyone sit beside it for long? · Can hot air and smoke get out: a window, a vent, a gap under the roof? · Is there smoke from wood, dung or kerosene adding to the strain? · How long is cooking each day, and can some of it move to cooler hours? · Can the household afford cleaner fuel through the season, not only once? · Is the electricity reliable enough for a fan or an electric cooker when it is hottest?

Before you choose, ask: Does it need electricity, and is the supply reliable in the hottest weeks? · What happens in a power cut: does it stop, or keep cooling without power? · Does it need water, and where will that water come from in a dry spell? · Who services or repairs it, and how fast? · Who owns the running cost each year, and from which budget? · Who can use it: renters, women, older people, people with disabilities? Keep the outcomes apart: a cooler room, comfort, less heat strain, better health and kept income are different outcomes; measure the one you mean.
