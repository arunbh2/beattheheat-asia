# Cooling options: Homes

What to build or change, what keeps it working, the traps to avoid and rough costs.

**Protects.** Sleep, care work, and the older people, young children and sick people who spend the hottest hours indoors.

**Key caution.** Check the roof type, fire safety, tenure and ventilation before any retrofit.

## Options, and what keeps each working

Option | What it does | Keeps working with | Avoid | Rough cost |

**Reflective roof coating** | A white, solar-reflective coat on the roof, so less of the sun’s heat passes into the room below. | Repainting every few years, and a thermometer to check it works. | Promising a fixed number of degrees before you have measured. | About US$2.6–US$3.7 per m² (₹250–₹350 at Indian prices) |
**Lime whitewash** | A cheap seasonal white coat that households can apply themselves when a commercial coating is out of reach. | Recoating every season, by a named group. | Treating it as a lasting retrofit. | No solid range: get local quotes |
**Better roof panels** | Replace or cover thin tin or asbestos sheets with an insulated roof panel. | A structural check, and secure enough tenure to make the investment safe. | Scaling up before checking structure and tenure. | No solid range: get local quotes |
**Insulation under the roof** | A fire-safe layer or false ceiling that slows heat coming through the roof. | A fire-safety review of the material. | Flammable sheets near kitchens or wiring. | No solid range: get local quotes |
**Shade on walls and windows** | Bamboo blinds, jute screens or awnings on the sunny side stop sunlight before it reaches the room. | Fixing and replacing screens each season. | Blocking airflow or all daylight. | No solid range: get local quotes |
**Let the air through** | Higher vents and openings that let hot air out at night. | Security, rain protection and mosquito screens. | Openings that let in dust, rain or intruders. | No solid range: get local quotes |

## What the evidence says

- In 16 homes in an Ahmedabad informal settlement, measured in September 2017, rooms under a modular roof averaged about 33.8 °C against 36.8 °C under asbestos sheets. White paint on tin did not beat asbestos or concrete in the same comparison: measure before you promise.
- In a two-year trial in Burkina Faso, cool roofs lowered indoor temperatures by 1.2 °C in tin-and-mud homes and 1.7 °C in tin-roofed homes, and residents’ heart rates fell. The same team began a year-long trial in about 400 Ahmedabad homes in 2025.

**How you will know it works.** Indoor temperature at 3 p.m. in treated and untreated homes, logged through the season

**Five traps that waste cooling money:** Buying before diagnosing · Built, with no one to keep it · Green without water · Promising degrees you haven’t measured · A list of activities, not a model.

**Kitchen heat.** Cooking adds heat on top of the weather, often in the smallest room and usually for women; in Nepal’s Madhesh Province it falls hardest on women in the poorest single-room homes.**Ask:** How much heat does the stove give off, and does anyone sit beside it for long? · Can hot air and smoke get out: a window, a vent, a gap under the roof? · Is there smoke from wood, dung or kerosene adding to the strain? · How long is cooking each day, and can some of it move to cooler hours? · Can the household afford cleaner fuel through the season, not only once? · Is the electricity reliable enough for a fan or an electric cooker when it is hottest?

**Before you choose, ask:** Does it need electricity, and is the supply reliable in the hottest weeks? · What happens in a power cut: does it stop, or keep cooling without power? · Does it need water, and where will that water come from in a dry spell? · Who services or repairs it, and how fast? · Who owns the running cost each year, and from which budget? · Who can use it: renters, women, older people, people with disabilities? **Keep the outcomes apart:** a cooler room, comfort, less heat strain, better health and kept income are different outcomes; measure the one you mean.
