# Cooling options: Making it last

What to build or change, what keeps it working, the traps to avoid and rough costs.

**Protects.** Every asset above: who targets it, who opens it on an alert, who pays for upkeep, and who hears complaints.

**Key caution.** Often the most useful layer, and the easiest to leave unfunded.

## Options, and what keeps each working

Option | What it does | Keeps working with | Avoid | Rough cost |

**Link the heat plan to the assets** | A ward or settlement plan that says which shelters, water points and cool spaces open at each alert level, and who opens them. | A yearly review with the people who use them. | A plan with no budget or named owners. | About US$5,300–US$26,000 per plan (₹5 lakh–₹25 lakh at Indian prices) |
**An upkeep agreement** | A written deal between the council, community groups, schools or vendors on who repaints, waters, prunes, refills and repairs. | Money from the running budget, not only the building budget. | Paying to build without paying to maintain. | About US$2,100–US$11,000 per year, for a cluster of assets (₹2 lakh–₹10 lakh at Indian prices) |
**Map risk with the people exposed** | Walk the area with workers, women, older and disabled people, migrants and renters to see who is exposed, why warnings fail, and where assets could go. | Repeating it as people and places change. | Relying on satellite heat maps alone. | About US$5,300–US$21,000 per baseline (₹5 lakh–₹20 lakh at Indian prices) |
**A way to complain and be heard** | An accessible route for complaints and feedback, set up before work starts. | Someone who answers within a set time. | Starting work where affected people have no voice or remedy. | About US$1,100–US$5,300 per set-up (₹1 lakh–₹5 lakh at Indian prices) |
**Find the hooks in existing plans** | Search city, disaster, water and climate plans and budgets for mandates and money that cooling can use. | Checking what you find with the officials concerned. | Assuming nothing exists because nobody uses the words “nature-based solutions”. | About US$1,100–US$5,300 per review (₹1 lakh–₹5 lakh at Indian prices) |

## What the evidence says

- In Melbourne, officials found money for new green infrastructure was usually available, but running budgets were under pressure and rarely increased, even as the area to maintain grew.
- In Santiago, without long-term financing, only better-off municipalities could take part in green-space programmes, because only they could afford the extra upkeep.
- Less than 10% of the climate finance that reaches cities goes to adaptation and resilience.
- The IUCN Global Standard for Nature-based Solutions makes inclusive governance and equity one of its eight criteria.

**How you will know it works.** Cooling assets still working after three years, each with a named owner and an upkeep budget

**Five traps that waste cooling money:** Buying before diagnosing · Built, with no one to keep it · Green without water · Promising degrees you haven’t measured · A list of activities, not a model.

Costs are rough planning ranges for early budgeting, not quotes. They are Indian prices, from a practitioner’s planning workbook, shown in US dollars at about ₹95 to US$1 (mid-2026) with the rupee range alongside. India has some of the lowest building prices in Asia, so expect most Southeast Asian cities to pay more: see the regional prices below. Where a public figure exists, it is linked.
