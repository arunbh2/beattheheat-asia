# Extreme heat in Dhaka

One of the world’s densest cities, where heat costs workdays and fills tin-roofed homes.

**Heat type.** Mostly humid heat.

**Heat action plan** Parts in place, no full plan foundDhaka North City Corporation appointed Asia’s first Chief Heat Officer in 2023. No city heat action plan found yet.

## Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Dhaka

Cycle | Why here | One action that breaks it |

**The poverty–heat trap** | Heat-related illness cost Bangladesh 250 million workdays in 2024, worth up to US$1.78 billion. | Heat-safe work |
**The energy-poverty trap** | Tin-roofed informal settlements trap heat for people who can’t afford to cool. | Free care for heat illness |
**The displacement loop** | Migrants land in settlements on the edges of the city, often in heat-trapping housing. | Income protection in heatwaves |
**The lost-schooling loop** | Bangladesh closed schools for about 33 million children in its April 2024 heatwave. | Keep children in school |

## Who heat hits hardest here

- Workers in heat-exposed jobs
- People in informal settlements

## A worked plan to learn from

**Dhaka acts before the heat peaks** (Dhaka, Bangladesh, 2023 onwards)

## Four questions for Dhaka’s heat plan

- Is it funded, with a named owner?
- Does it assess who is most vulnerable, and where they live?
- Does it have legal backing, so departments are required to act?
- Does it count indoor and night-time heat, not only daytime highs?
