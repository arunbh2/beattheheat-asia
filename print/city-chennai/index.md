# Extreme heat in Chennai

One of India’s hottest and most humid big cities, where homes stay hot long after sunset.

**Heat type.** Mostly humid coastal heat.

**Heat action plan** Parts in place, no full plan foundIn 2025 Tamil Nadu began preparing heatwave action plans for its major cities, including Chennai. In July 2026 the Greater Chennai Corporation and the Council on Energy, Environment and Water agreed to set up a unit to identify and prioritise heat measures across heat-vulnerable wards and zones. No published city heat action plan found.

## Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Chennai

Cycle | Why here | One action that breaks it |

**The sleepless-nights loop** | In 50 low- and middle-income homes tracked from October 2025 to April 2026, indoor temperatures rarely fell below 31 °C and often stayed above 34 °C for hours after sunset. | Cool nights and support |
**The overwhelmed-clinic loop** | For the 2026 hot season, government hospitals set up dedicated heatstroke wards with ICU beds, hydration counters and rapid cooling. | Heat action plans |
**The poverty–heat trap** | Across 11 districts of Tamil Nadu, 90.3% of the outdoor workers in a large study reported at least one symptom of heat illness. | Heat-safe work |

## Who heat hits hardest here

- Workers in heat-exposed jobs
- People in informal settlements

## Four questions for Chennai’s heat plan

- Is it funded, with a named owner?
- Does it assess who is most vulnerable, and where they live?
- Does it have legal backing, so departments are required to act?
- Does it count indoor and night-time heat, not only daytime highs?
