# Cooling options: Markets and street vending

What to build or change, what keeps it working, the traps to avoid and rough costs.

**Protects.** Vendors’ income and stock, customers, and the porters who load and unload in the heat.

**Key caution.** Design it with the vendors and protect their place, or the upgrade becomes an eviction.

## Options, and what keeps each working

Option | What it does | Keeps working with | Avoid | Rough cost |

**Shade over stalls and aisles** | Nets, fabric or a light frame with climbing plants over the places vendors sit. | Storm-proof fixing and someone to replace worn cloth. | Shade that blocks fire access or loading. | About US$3.2–US$9.5 per m² for shade nets (₹300–₹900 at Indian prices) |
**Water and rehydration points** | Safe drinking water for vendors and customers. | A refill and cleaning roster agreed with the market association. | Points nobody is responsible for. | About US$110–US$530 per site (₹10,000–₹50,000 at Indian prices) |
**Planted drains and rain gardens** | Planted channels and hollows that soak up runoff so the market does not flood after a storm. | Rubbish collection, or they clog. | Building them where waste is dumped. | About US$5.3–US$32 per m² (₹500–₹3,000 at Indian prices) |
**A whole-market package** | Shade, water, cool roofs, drainage, trees and waste collection together, run with the vendors. | A vendor committee with a say over the design and the upkeep. | Beautification that pushes informal vendors out. | About US$11,000–US$53,000 per market cluster (₹10 lakh–₹50 lakh at Indian prices) |

## What the evidence says

- India’s Street Vendors Act (2014) gives vendors 40% of the seats on each town vending committee and says no vendor may be evicted or relocated until a survey of existing vendors is complete: a hook for designing market cooling with vendors, not around them.

**How you will know it works.** Vendors with shade and water at their stall, and vendors still trading there a year later

**Five traps that waste cooling money:** Buying before diagnosing · Built, with no one to keep it · Green without water · Promising degrees you haven’t measured · A list of activities, not a model.

**Before you choose, ask:** Does it need electricity, and is the supply reliable in the hottest weeks? · What happens in a power cut: does it stop, or keep cooling without power? · Does it need water, and where will that water come from in a dry spell? · Who services or repairs it, and how fast? · Who owns the running cost each year, and from which budget? · Who can use it: renters, women, older people, people with disabilities? **Keep the outcomes apart:** a cooler room, comfort, less heat strain, better health and kept income are different outcomes; measure the one you mean.
