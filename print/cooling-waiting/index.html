Cooling options: Bus stops and queues

What to build or change, what keeps it working, the traps to avoid and rough costs.

Protects. Commuters, patients queuing at clinics, people waiting for rations or wages, and visitors.
Key caution. Put the shade where people actually stand, at the hours they wait.

What the evidence says

  • Measuring how hot people actually feel on summer days in Tempe, Arizona, researchers found shade from buildings cooled most by day, then trees, then light structures such as sails. Air temperature barely differed between them, and every kind of shade held heat after sunset.
How you will know it works. People waiting in shade at the busiest hour, counted at each stop
Five traps that waste cooling money: Buying before diagnosing · Built, with no one to keep it · Green without water · Promising degrees you haven’t measured · A list of activities, not a model.
Before you choose, ask: Does it need electricity, and is the supply reliable in the hottest weeks? · What happens in a power cut: does it stop, or keep cooling without power? · Does it need water, and where will that water come from in a dry spell? · Who services or repairs it, and how fast? · Who owns the running cost each year, and from which budget? · Who can use it: renters, women, older people, people with disabilities? Keep the outcomes apart: a cooler room, comfort, less heat strain, better health and kept income are different outcomes; measure the one you mean.
