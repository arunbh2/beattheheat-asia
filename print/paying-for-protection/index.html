Paying for heat protection

Protection only works if someone pays for it, the money arrives in time, and it is still there next season.

What needs paying for, and when must the money arrive?

NeedRoute to look intoThe question to answer
Shade, cooler buildings and water pointsPublic investment, grants or infrastructure financeWho pays to build it, and who pays to run it afterwards?
Upkeep, staff and outreachRecurring public, employer or programme budgetsIs the money there every season, not just the first?
Action before forecast heatPre-arranged anticipatory financeCan money be released before people are harmed?
Lost income or disrupted servicesCash assistance, contingency reserves or suitable insuranceWill money arrive before people borrow or go back to unsafe work?
Households that stay vulnerableSocial protection and affordable essential servicesWhat keeps supporting them between heatwaves?

A way to compare routes, not a claim that every route exists in every country.

Three triggers, not one

Warning trigger. Prompts an official heat advisory or warning.

Action trigger. Switches on agreed measures: outreach, rest breaks, cooling spaces open.

Payout trigger. Decides whether a specific insurance contract pays.

The three need not match. Protective action should never wait for an insurance payout.

Why can someone lose income to heat and get no payout?

Wrong place. The contract measures heat at a weather station or grid point, but the workplace or home is hotter.

Wrong measure. The index counts temperature, but humidity, heavy work or hot nights caused the harm.

Wrong amount. It pays, but a fixed amount that covers only part of what was lost.

If the contract does not pay, what support is still there?

From trigger to protection

1. Trigger confirmed  →  2. Fund holder paid  →  3. People receive support they can use  →  4. Protective action becomes possible

When money goes first to a government, cooperative or microfinance institution, someone still has to decide who gets it. Count the date at every step, not only the day the insurer pays.

Write down, for any money that passes through someone else

  • Who receives the money first
  • Who it is meant to reach
  • How it is shared out
  • When it must arrive
  • How it is paid, and the fallback
  • Where people can complain
  • What is still not covered

Will it survive the next season?

  • Who pays next year’s premium and running costs?
  • What happens when donor money ends?
  • Who keeps the knowledge when staff change?
  • How will you review seasons with a payout, and without one?
  • What evidence would make you change it, or stop it?

What has been tried: heat income cover for women informal workers (Gujarat and north-western India); government cover that switched to its own disaster data (Lao PDR); pay for days too dangerous to work (Philippines, 24 coastal municipalities). Who paid, who received it and what each left out are on the live page.

These reports describe how insurance is regulated, designed and delivered in Asia. The parametric report did not assess consumer outcomes, pricing, contracts or how triggers performed, so it supports design questions, not proof that a heat product works.
