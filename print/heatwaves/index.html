Lessons from Asia’s heatwaves, 1998–2026

Ten heatwaves across South and Southeast Asia: what they cost, what changed afterwards, and what they teach.

Four lessons

Plans save lives. After 2,042 recorded heatstroke deaths in 1998, Odisha wrote a heat plan, and recorded deaths fell to 91 the next year. Ahmedabad’s plan was later estimated to avoid 1,190 deaths a year.
Counts are a floor. In 2024 India recorded 110 heat deaths against more than 40,000 suspected heatstroke cases by mid-June. Ahmedabad’s 2010 toll was only found by counting excess deaths.
Heat arrives earlier, and wetter. In 2022 it came in March, before the wheat was ready. Humid heat that once came less than once a century in Bangladesh and India can now be expected about every five years.
Climate change raises the odds. Scientists found the 2022 heatwave about 30 times more likely because of climate change, a 30-day South Asian heatwave like April 2024’s about 45 times more likely, and April 2026’s heat about three times more likely.

Recorded deaths are a floor, not a total: many heat deaths are never counted as heat deaths. Where no solid count exists, the timeline says so.
