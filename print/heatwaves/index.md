# Lessons from Asia’s heatwaves, 1998–2026

Ten heatwaves across South and Southeast Asia: what they cost, what changed afterwards, and what they teach.

When | Where | Peak | Recorded toll |

**May 1998** | Odisha, India | Coastal Odisha, not known for extreme heat | 2,042 deaths from heatstroke recorded by hospitals |
**May–June 2003** | Andhra Pradesh, India | Weeks of heat across the state | 1,317 deaths in Andhra Pradesh in three weeks, as reported then |
**May 2010** | Ahmedabad, India | 46.8 °C | An estimated 1,344 excess deaths, 43% more than in the same weeks of 2009 and 2011 |
**May–June 2015** | India and Pakistan | 40–45 °C for weeks in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana | More than 2,500 deaths in India, mostly in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, and more than 1,100 in Pakistan, centred on Karachi |
**April 2016** | Thailand and mainland Southeast Asia | 44.6 °C at Mae Hong Son, Thailand’s highest ever at the time | No regional death count |
**March–April 2022** | India and Pakistan | 49 °C in Pakistan; India’s hottest March in 122 years | At least 90 deaths reported |
**April 2023** | Bangladesh, India, Laos and Thailand | 45.4 °C at Tak, Thailand’s highest ever | No regional death count |
**April 2024** | South and Southeast Asia | 48.2 °C at Chauk, Myanmar’s highest April temperature on record | At least 28 heat deaths reported in Bangladesh in April; likely an undercount |
**May–June 2024** | Northern India | Nearly 50 °C in parts of the north; Delhi’s hottest night in more than five decades | 110 heat deaths and more than 40,000 suspected heatstroke cases officially recorded from 1 March to 18 June |
**April 2026** | India and Pakistan | Above 46 °C in many cities | At least 37 heat deaths reported in India and 10 in Karachi; likely undercounted |

## Four lessons

**Plans save lives.** After 2,042 recorded heatstroke deaths in 1998, Odisha wrote a heat plan, and recorded deaths fell to 91 the next year. Ahmedabad’s plan was later estimated to avoid 1,190 deaths a year.

**Counts are a floor.** In 2024 India recorded 110 heat deaths against more than 40,000 suspected heatstroke cases by mid-June. Ahmedabad’s 2010 toll was only found by counting excess deaths.

**Heat arrives earlier, and wetter.** In 2022 it came in March, before the wheat was ready. Humid heat that once came less than once a century in Bangladesh and India can now be expected about every five years.

**Climate change raises the odds.** Scientists found the 2022 heatwave about 30 times more likely because of climate change, a 30-day South Asian heatwave like April 2024’s about 45 times more likely, and April 2026’s heat about three times more likely.

Recorded deaths are a floor, not a total: many heat deaths are never counted as heat deaths. Where no solid count exists, the timeline says so.
