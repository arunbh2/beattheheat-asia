# Extreme heat in Singapore

A city-state with hot nights, dense towers and an early national heat stress advisory.

**Heat type.** Humid heat, all year round.

**Heat action plan** Heat plan in forceSingapore launched a national Heat Stress Advisory in July 2023. It rates heat stress as low, moderate or high from the wet-bulb globe temperature, and tells people what to do at each level. Since March 2025 a heatwave is declared when the daily maximum reaches at least 35 °C and the daily mean at least 29 °C for three days in a row; a taskforce of 37 public agencies then opens air-conditioned cooling spaces in community centres. In March 2026 a Heat Resilience Policy Office was set up to coordinate action across agencies, oversee research and draft a heat resilience action plan.

## Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Singapore

Cycle | Why here | One action that breaks it |

**The sleepless-nights loop** | Singapore has averaged 76 warm nights a year over four decades; by 2100 they could come on most nights. | Cool nights and support |
**The cooling trap** | The commercial centre has measured up to about 7 °C warmer than the outskirts on calm, clear nights. | Cool by design |
**The dying-green loop** | Among the cities where the most urban tree species already face temperatures beyond their range. | Keep city trees alive |
**The invisible-deaths loop** | Workplace monitors found more heat stress than nearby weather stations reported. | Count every heat death |

## Who heat hits hardest here

- Workers in heat-exposed jobs
- Older people

## Four questions for Singapore’s heat plan

- Is it funded, with a named owner?
- Does it assess who is most vulnerable, and where they live?
- Does it have legal backing, so departments are required to act?
- Does it count indoor and night-time heat, not only daytime highs?
