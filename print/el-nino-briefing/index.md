# El Niño and heat in Asia

How a change in the Pacific affects rainfall, heat and the choices available to people.

**El Niño Advisory: A strong-to-very strong El Niño is likely through January to March 2027 (a greater than 79% chance), before it begins to weaken in early 2027.**
NOAA Climate Prediction Center, ENSO Diagnostic Discussion, issued 12 November 2026; next scheduled 10 December 2026. A Pacific probability is not any town's chance of a heatwave, water shortage or flood.

## ENSO phase odds (NOAA Climate Prediction Center, November 2026)

Season | El Niño | Neutral | La Niña |
January to March 2027 | 100% | 0% | 0% |
February to April 2027 | 98% | 2% | 0% |
March to May 2027 | 80% | 20% | 0% |
April to June 2027 | 38% | 61% | 1% |

Strength, March to May 2027: weak 53%, moderate 25%, strong 2%, very strong 0%. RONI, 1991–2020 base. Strength in the Pacific does not necessarily match the strength of local impacts.

## Your national or regional service

A regional pattern does not give one district's outlook.

**India and the South Asian monsoon:** India Meteorological Department. **Bangladesh and Nepal:** Bangladesh Meteorological Department; Nepal Department of Hydrology and Meteorology. **Mainland Southeast Asia:** ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre. **Indonesia and the maritime region:** BMKG, Indonesia; Meteorological Service Singapore. **The Philippines:** PAGASA.

## Questions for the first meeting

- Which public service will we protect first, and where do people wait or work in the heat?
- Who receives the official local warning, and what does it change?
- Who authorises, pays for, runs and repairs each measure?
- How will we know who could not get water, shade or care?
