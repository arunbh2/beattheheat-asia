A practical guide for municipal teams and community organisations in India's Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities. Choose where protection is needed, agree who will keep it working, and decide what changes when a local warning arrives.

## What a seasonal outlook is for

A seasonal El Niño outlook gives cities a reason to review preparations early. The local heat season, water supply and places where people spend the hottest hours decide what a city puts in place. District forecasts and official warnings decide when to step up the response.

**The first decision**Which public service will we protect first, who relies on it during the hottest hours, and who has the authority to keep it running?

One service, chosen with the people who use it, is enough to start. The rest of this guide helps a municipal team and a local organisation answer the question in one meeting, with information they already hold.

IllustrativeA vegetable seller loses sales if she leaves her stall to find shade. So the shade needs to reach her occupied stall in the afternoon, not a room across town. Drinking water needs to be there when she needs it, at 2 p.m., not only when the market opens.

### What a seasonal signal can and cannot tell a city

### It can

Give a reason to start ordinary protection early. In India's records for 1961-2013, most years with above-normal heatwave activity (April to June) followed El Niño events: an India-wide pattern, not a city forecast.

### It cannot

Give your city's chance of a heatwave, a water shortage or a flood. An ENSO probability describes the Pacific, never a municipality.

Long-term warming raises the baseline; the local heat island and failing infrastructure (a pump, a feeder, a drain) decide much of the harm, and those are within a city's reach.

### Two compound scenarios

- Scenario

### Heat with the taps off

An afternoon of heat, the ward's supply cut for a repair, the market water point running low by noon. What helps: storage sized to a hot day's demand, a refill arranged with enough lead time for the tanker to arrive, and someone who calls when it runs low.

- Scenario

### A downpour in a dry year

A below-normal monsoon, then one night of very heavy rain before the season turns. Covers tear, a lane floods, and a cover that blocks a drain turns one hazard into two. What helps: covers fixed against wind, drains cleared around each site, and an agreed alternative site.

## Your warning route

This guide sets no warning thresholds of its own. Find who issues your warning and who acts on it:
IMD district warnings](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#energywater)The official warning for your district, issued by the India Meteorological Department.
- **[Your regional or state meteorological centre](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/event/evolution.php)**IMD works through Regional Meteorological Centres, assisted by Meteorological Centres at the state level that disseminate advisories and warnings for their region.
- **[Your state or district disaster authority](https://beattheheat.asia/resources/heat-action-plans/india/)**The state and district heat action plans say which department acts at each alert level. Find yours in the India heat action plan archive.
- **The municipal officer or service operator named locally**The person who extends water hours or moves a queue indoors. If no one is named for your chosen service, name them before the season.

For health departments, NCDC's advisory for summer 2026 asked states to start daily heat illness reporting from 1 March 2026 and to set local early warning thresholds. ## Before, during and after: a local calendar

Read each window against your city's own season, not a national date.

Three decision windows
Window | What happens | Who decides |

Before the locally expected heat season | Select the service and sites, confirm permissions and operators, agree costs, put the measures in place and test them. Work backwards from when heat usually arrives in your city, how long procurement takes, and official guidance. | The body that manages each site (municipal body, market committee, health department or transport authority), the operator and the local organisation. |
When an official local warning is issued | The core protection is already working. Agree in advance who receives the warning and what is added under the applicable plan: an operator extends water hours, brings a refill forward, or moves a clinic queue indoors. | IMD issues district warnings; departments act under the state and district heat plans. |
During and just after the event | Check that each site works at the hours people rely on it, fix failures, and record who was left without access. After heavy rain, check covers and drains before reopening. | Each site's operator and the named repair officer, with the local organisation reporting what users see. |

Before opening a site, check how people will get drinking water during a power cut. If the supply depends on a pump, agree who can release stored water or order a refill, how long it will take, and where people can go in the meantime. If a site floods, the agreed alternative site takes over. Clinical care follows official guidance such as NCDC's.

Worked example

## Worked example: a market at 2 p.m.

Illustrative design
A vegetable seller would lose sales if she walked to a distant cooling room. So protection goes to her stall, in the hours she trades.

### The local organisation's design role

**Vendors, through their association or a local organisation** walk the market at 2 p.m. and mark which occupied stalls lose shade in the afternoon. Their map can move the shade: from the entrance, where it looks visible, to the aisle where sellers actually sit after lunch.

### Four roles, which can sit with different bodies

- **Authorises the site**Whoever manages the market: a municipal body, a market committee or another authority. Gives permission for shade and a water point.
- **Pays for installation**May be the municipal budget, a state scheme or a district allocation; agreed before procurement starts.
- **Covers summer operations**Water, cleaning and small repairs through the season; may be a different body from the one that paid to install.
- **Authorises a repair or emergency refill**One named person who can order a tanker or fix a torn cover the same day.

### On a warning day

Water hours extend and the refill comes forward, timed to the storage on site and the tanker's delivery time; the association checks the water point at noon and mid-afternoon.

### One trade-off, and the check

A badly sited cover can trap heat, pour rain onto the walkway or block a wheelchair or handcart. The check: stand under it at 2 p.m., pour a bucket on it, walk the aisle with a handcart.

Every service has an owner and an operating requirement
Service | Owner | Operating requirement |

Shade over occupied stalls | The site manager authorises; the installer repairs within an agreed time | Fixed against wind, drains clear, aisle passable |
Drinking water point | The operator refills; one named person can authorise an emergency refill | Water available during peak trading hours; storage sized to a hot day |
Failure reports | The association or local organisation | One number that answers; the operator records when each failure was fixed |

### Or: a shaded clinic queue

A shaded, seated waiting line with water, and a route indoors when it rains. Managed by: Whoever manages the clinic, often the health department rather than the municipality.

Patients and disability groups walk the route from the gate and say whether the shaded line is reachable with a wheelchair or a walking stick; their answer can move the queue indoors or change where the seating goes.

## Preparedness that has happened in India

A published plan shows a planning decision, an installed facility shows installation, and measured benefit needs outcome evidence; each example says which it is. Each comes from NRDC's 2026 review of heat policy in India.
Installed, reported in a review

### Jodhpur, Rajasthan Hot, dry

The decision: where to put protection. Under Jodhpur's heat action plan, a net zero cooling station designed for the informal sector was installed in a high-risk ward identified through ward-level risk assessment, and IMD heat alerts were passed on in locally accessible formats through community channels.An allocation, reported in a review

### Kerala Humid, coastal

The decision: who can pay at short notice. The state allocated ₹1 crore to each District Collector for heat protection such as drinking water kiosks and cooling centres, and directed local bodies to develop decentralised heat action plans.A state rule, reported in a review

### Uttar Pradesh Northern plains

The decision: what triggers a step-up. District-specific heatwave thresholds classify alerts as yellow, orange or red according to regional climatic conditions, so an alert and the response it sets moving reflect local conditions.
A large city, with outcome evidence Ahmedabad. A 2018 evaluation found Ahmedabad's heat action plan may have contributed to about 1,190 fewer deaths a year on hot days after full implementation.
