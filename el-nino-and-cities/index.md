## What a seasonal outlook is for

A seasonal El Niño outlook gives cities a reason to review preparations early. The local heat season, water supply and places where people spend the hottest hours decide what a city puts in place. District forecasts and official warnings decide when to step up the response.

**The first decision**Where do people already have to be in the hottest hours, which one place will we protect first, and who has the authority to keep it working?

One setting, chosen with the people who use it, is enough to start. Getting one change working across several schools or markets may protect more people than starting all four settings at once. The rest of this guide helps a municipal team and a local organisation decide in one meeting, with information they already hold.

### Where people already have to be

The best place to offer heat respite may be the place someone already has to be.

A child at the midday meal, a vendor at a stall, a patient in a clinic queue, a passenger at a bus stand: none of them can walk away without losing something. NIUA's Learning Note 1 describes public spaces becoming safe zones during heatwaves (p. 85). Here are four settings where a town can act before the hot season.

### What a seasonal signal can and cannot tell a city

### It can

Give a reason to start ordinary protection early. In India's records for 1961-2013, most years with above-normal heatwave activity (April to June) followed El Niño events: an India-wide pattern, not a city forecast.

### It cannot

Give your city's chance of a heatwave, a water shortage or a flood. An ENSO probability describes the Pacific, never a municipality.

Long-term warming raises the baseline; the local heat island and failing infrastructure (a pump, a feeder, a drain) decide much of the harm, and those are within a city's reach.

### Two compound scenarios

- Scenario

### Heat with the taps off

An afternoon of heat, the ward's supply cut for a repair, the market water point running low by noon. What helps: storage sized to a hot day's demand, a refill arranged with enough lead time for the tanker to arrive, and someone who calls when it runs low.

- Scenario

### A downpour in a dry year

A below-normal monsoon, then one night of very heavy rain before the season turns. Covers tear, a lane floods, and a cover that blocks a drain turns one hazard into two. What helps: covers fixed against wind, drains cleared around each site, and an agreed alternative site.

Worked example

## A school at lunchtime

Illustrative scene
Children queue for the midday meal in an open courtyard, in full sun. Along one side, a veranda is in shade at the time the meal is served. Could the school move the serving point to the veranda, keeping the route step-free and leaving kitchen staff room to serve the children quickly?

Nothing had to be built to see this. Watching one ordinary part of the school day, at the hour it happens, changed the proposal: from new shade over the whole courtyard to moving where the meal is served.

How a municipal team turns that observation into something it can deliver:

- Choose the right exposure

The meal queue is one exposure. Where a classroom is the main problem, there are alternatives: move the hottest lessons to a cooler room that is free at that time, shade the sun-facing windows, or treat a suitable roof. Match the choice to the building, the hours the room is used and the permissions available.

[Cool the hottest classrooms first](https://beattheheat.asia/practical-actions/#schools-classroofs) · [Shaded play space](https://beattheheat.asia/practical-actions/#schools-play)

- Give people a real design decision

- **Children**can point out where they stand in the sun.
- **Meal staff**know where they can serve safely and quickly.
- **Teachers**know which rooms are occupied during the hottest lessons.
- **The headteacher and the School Management Committee**can help choose a change that works in this school.
- **The education authority or building owner**approves changes to the building and maintains them. Who that is varies by town, so confirm it before the plan is agreed.

- Use the school calendar

Ask which schools are open, on which dates and at what hours during the hot weeks. Count reach only for the days children are present. Opening a school as neighbourhood respite during the holidays is a separate decision, needing permission, staff, drinking water, a step-free entrance and safeguarding.

- What changes on a heat-warning day

Something within the school's own authority: hold the morning assembly in the shaded area instead of the open yard, and check that the drinking-water point works before pupils arrive. Changes to school hours or closures follow the education department and the state or district heat action plan. A shorter school day also moves care to families and can cost a parent paid work, so changes are easier to carry when agreed with families early.

[Find your state's and district's heat action plan](https://beattheheat.asia/resources/heat-action-plans/india/) · [Water, and time to drink it](https://beattheheat.asia/practical-actions/#schools-water) · [Heat-ready timetables](https://beattheheat.asia/practical-actions/#schools-timetable)

- Check the result

For the meal queue, time how long pupils wait in direct sun during comparable lunch periods, before and after the change. For a roof or a classroom change, compare temperatures in occupied rooms at similar times of day and in comparable weather; NIUA's Table 2 pairs roofs treated with lower indoor temperatures (Learning Note 2, p. 54). Claims about attendance, learning or illness need evidence of their own.

**The question for the education team**Which schools share this problem, are open during the relevant weeks, and could have the change in place before the first hot spell?

### Worked example: a market at 2 p.m.

Illustrative design
A vegetable seller would lose sales if she walked to a distant cooling room. So protection goes to her stall, in the hours she trades.

### The local organisation's design role

**Vendors, through their association or a local organisation** walk the market at 2 p.m. and mark which occupied stalls lose shade in the afternoon. Their map can move the shade: from the entrance, where it looks visible, to the aisle where sellers actually sit after lunch.

### Four roles, which can sit with different bodies

- **Authorises the site**Whoever manages the market: a municipal body, a market committee or another authority. Gives permission for shade and a water point.
- **Pays for installation**May be the municipal budget, a state scheme or a district allocation; agreed before procurement starts.
- **Covers summer operations**Water, cleaning and small repairs through the season; may be a different body from the one that paid to install.
- **Authorises a repair or emergency refill**One named person who can order a tanker or fix a torn cover the same day.

### On a warning day

Water hours extend and the refill comes forward, timed to the storage on site and the tanker's delivery time; the association checks the water point at noon and mid-afternoon.

### One trade-off, and the check

A badly sited cover can trap heat, pour rain onto the walkway or block a wheelchair or handcart. The check: stand under it at 2 p.m., pour a bucket on it, walk the aisle with a handcart.

In practice

## Preparedness that has happened in India

A published plan shows a planning decision, an installed facility shows installation, and measured benefit needs outcome evidence; each example says which it is, and cites its own source.

- Installed, reported by a partner in the plan

### Jodhpur, Rajasthan Hot, dry

**The decision: where to put protection.** In April 2024 the city corporation and its partners opened a net-zero cooling station in a heat-vulnerable ward of Jodhpur North, chosen through the vulnerability assessment in the city's heat action plan. The 2023 plan also sets out passing heat warnings by SMS and WhatsApp.

- An allocation, reported in the news

### Kerala Humid, coastal

**The decision: who can pay at short notice.** In March 2026 the state decided to give each district collector ₹1 crore for summer measures, and asked officials to set up drinking water kiosks where people gather: markets, junctions and bus stands. The government order itself was not found online.

- A state method, with district thresholds reported

### Uttar Pradesh Northern plains

**The decision: what triggers a step-up.** The state disaster management authority set yellow, orange and red alert temperatures for each of its 75 districts, from percentiles of each district's own temperature record, so an alert reflects local conditions. Its 2024 heat plan sets out the method, first drafted for Agra.

A large city, with outcome evidence **Ahmedabad.** A 2018 evaluation estimated about 1,190 deaths a year avoided after Ahmedabad's heat action plan was fully implemented (95% confidence interval 162 to 2,218): an association, not proof of cause. The scale differs; the practice a smaller city can adapt is a named plan in which a warning sets agreed actions moving across departments. [Heat action plans, with Ahmedabad's](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#hap).
