# El Niño and the city: what to prepare before summer 2027

A decision guide for smaller Indian cities, starting in Maharashtra: what El Niño's lead time allows before summer 2027, who needs to agree, what changes when an official warning arrives, and how to tell whether people got relief.

## What a city can do with the lead time El Niño offers

El Niño gives a city months of notice. It cannot say which ward will overheat or which street will flood. It can say this is a season to start ordinary protection early: choose one public service, find its most exposed sites, and have shade, water and a named owner working by March.

**The next decision**By the end of January 2027: which service comes first (markets, clinic queues, bus stops or worksites), which sites, and who owns shade and water at each. Working by March, April at the latest.

Picture a vegetable seller at 2 p.m. under a sheet roof. She is a composite illustration, not a real person.

### What a seasonal signal can and cannot tell a city

### It can

Shift the odds early enough to prepare. In 1961-2013, most years with above-normal Indian heatwave activity (April to June) followed El Niño events: an India-wide pattern, not a city forecast.

### It cannot

Give a heatwave or flood probability for your city. A Pacific probability is never a municipal one.

El Niño gives notice; long-term warming raises the baseline; the local heat island and failing infrastructure (a pump, a feeder, a drain) decide much of the harm, and those are within a city's reach.

## Six linked risks

Rain and flooding are kept apart: flooding also depends on drains and land use. Health and livelihoods show a pathway, not a probability.

Six risks: what the evidence supports, what turns it into harm, one response
Risk | What the evidence supports here | What turns it into harm locally | One municipal response |

Extreme heat
India-wide pattern | Most above-normal Indian heatwave years (1961-2013) followed El Niño events. | Sheet roofs, unshaded stalls, queues and worksites. | Shade and drinking water at priority sites by March. |
Water scarcity
Observed, national | India's 2026 monsoon: 87% of normal. Check district reservoirs and groundwater. | Supply cuts, tanker dependence, water points dry at peak hours. | A water point per site, with a refill schedule and tanker back-up. |
Intense rainfall
Central India, 1950-2015 | Widespread extreme rain events over central India tripled as total rain declined. Not attributed to El Niño. | Very heavy rain within hours, often with wind. | Covers fixed against wind and rain; a named rain fallback per site. |
Urban flooding
Depends on the city | Depends on drains, land use and settlement, not rain alone; isolated fixes leave the system exposed (NIUA). | Blocked drains, building over water paths, low-lying homes. | Clear drains around priority sites before the monsoon. |
Public health
A pathway, not a probability | Heat with cut water or power raises heat illness. NCDC asked for daily heatstroke reports from 1 March 2026. | Long outdoor waits, unshaded clinics, delayed treatment. | Shade and water at clinic queues; watch where reports cluster. |
Livelihoods
A pathway, not a probability | Lost hours, fewer customers, spoiled goods. Informal settlements face higher exposure (NIUA). | Leaving a stall means losing sales. | Protection at the workplace, not a walk away from it. |

### Two compound scenarios

- Scenario

### Heat with the taps off

An afternoon of heat, the ward's supply cut for a repair, the market water point dry by noon. What helps: a refill schedule, a tanker contact, and someone who calls when it runs low.

- Scenario, with an observed precedent

### A downpour in a dry year

Below-normal rain, then one night of very heavy rain. IMD recorded very heavy rainfall over Marathwada on 30 September 2026, after a monsoon below normal for India. A cover that blocks a drain turns one hazard into two.

## From preparation to response

Three decision windows
Window | Already running | Added when it arrives | Who decides |

Before the hot season (December to March) | One service and its priority sites; shade, water and an owner at each; refill and repair times; a rain and drainage check; who pays to install and to run. | Nothing waits for a warning. | Municipal body, market associations or community organisations, and the operator. |
When an official local warning is issued | Everything above keeps working. | Extra refills and longer water hours, a noon check at each site, warnings passed on through associations, clinic queues moved under cover. | IMD issues district warnings; departments act under state and district heat plans. This page sets no thresholds. |
During and just after an event | A daily check: shade intact, water at 2 p.m.; log each failure and fix time. | After heavy rain, check covers and drains before reopening; use the fallback site; note what failed. | Each site owner and the named repair officer. |

If the power goes, shade and water still work; fans do not. If supply stops, the tanker contact takes over. If a site floods, people go to the fallback site. Clinical care follows official guidance such as NCDC's.

## Worked example: a municipal market at 2 p.m.

Illustrative design
A vegetable seller would lose sales if she walked to a distant cooling room. So protection goes to her stall.

### What each party could agree

- **Municipal body**Installs shade over afternoon stalls and one accessible water point, pays for installation, names a repair officer.
- **Market association or community organisation**Lists occupied afternoon stalls, keeps one contact for failures, passes on warnings.
- **Operator (water supplier or contractor)**Refills on schedule; fixes a tap or torn cover within an agreed time.

### On a warning day

Refill earlier and twice; the association checks the water at noon and 3 p.m.

### One trade-off, and the check

A badly sited cover can trap heat, pour rain onto the walkway or block a wheelchair or handcart. The check: stand under it at 2 p.m., pour a bucket on it, walk the aisle with a handcart.

Every service has an owner and an operating requirement
Service | Owner | Operating requirement |

Shade over occupied stalls | Municipal body (installs and repairs) | Fixed against wind, drains clear, aisle passable |
Water point | Operator (refills) | Water available at 2 p.m., refill schedule kept |
Failure reports | Market association or community organisation | One named contact, a number that answers |
Or: a shaded, seated waiting line with water, and a route indoors when it rains. | The clinic's medical officer | Someone checks the water and seating every morning, and the indoor route stays open. |

## What city scale means this season

No new vulnerability assessment is needed: use the market list, clinic attendance, ward knowledge and the summer plan. NIUA describes city-scale planning as moving beyond isolated projects (Learning Note 2, section 2.6, p. 40).

- One service across the city, not one showcase site.
- Count honestly: priority sites working out of all relevant sites (say 18 of 42 markets, an illustrative figure). Every market is the aim; the share working is this season's claim.
- Keep apart regular users, visits per day and catchment population. Footfall is not protection.
- Claim no avoided deaths or illness without health data and a fair comparison.

Pair each output with an observable result (after NIUA's Table 2, p. 54):

- **Output:** Shade installed**Observable result:** Occupied stalls shaded during trading hours
- **Output:** Water point installed**Observable result:** Water available at 2 p.m.
- **Output:** Cooling space opened**Observable result:** People using it at peak hours, with staff on duty

## Preparedness that has happened

Three documented examples: Nepal, Peru, Maharashtra

- Activities reported by the organisation

### Nepal

Practical Action Nepal used seasonal forecasts to guide early action: awareness with media and local partners, and support for heat action planning (June 2026).

- A stated aim

### Peru

With the Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation, the national civil defence institute (INDECI) and local government, Practical Action set out early action to protect 1,500 families from floods and mudslides.

- A plan

The State Heat Action Plan 2024 names Vidarbha, Marathwada and Khandesh as most vulnerable and 15 districts as high-risk, with cooling centres and reserved hospital beds.

## Akola, Nanded, Jalgaon and other smaller cities

Akola (Vidarbha), Nanded (Marathwada) and Jalgaon (Khandesh) sit in the three regions the state plan calls most vulnerable. Common Shade's archive has 2026 district plans for Nashik, Palghar and Wardha but none for these three; that gap does not mean no plan exists. The collector's office, district disaster authority and municipal corporation know what is current. Smaller towns often have limited resources and capacities (NIUA, Learning Note 1, p. 35), another reason to start with one service.

The method travels across South Asia; the warnings do not. Use your own country's weather service and disaster authority.
