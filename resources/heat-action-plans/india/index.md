# India heat action plan archive

146 verified documents from across India: plans written by cities, districts and states, the heat-health plans inside wider climate-and-health plans, and the national plan and guidance behind them. A growing archive, not a complete collection.

- **37**heat-health plans inside wider climate-and-health plans
- **1**national heat-illness action plan
- **23**guidance, advisory or manual documents

That is 123 plan documents and 23 guidance documents, not 146 standalone plans. Every one of the 36 states and union territories in the archive has a verified heat-health plan inside a climate-and-health plan; 16 have at least one verified standalone heat action plan.

## Search the archive

Years are as printed on each document. A period such as 2022-27 is a planning period, not a publication date, and “undated” means no printed year was found.

## Beyond the plans: heat resilience

India’s Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs has a national urban heat resilience initiative under the AMRUT 2.0 sector reforms. It is led by the Climate Centre for Cities (C-Cube) at the National Institute of Urban Affairs (NIUA) and supports 12 cities. Its [Heat Resilient Cities Framework](https://niua.in/publication-details/Heat_Resilient_Cities_Framework) (September 2026) describes two parts that work together:

- **A city intervention plan, for one or two years.** It starts with a needs assessment: heat hotspots, checked on the ground against air temperature and humidity, and layers showing who is most exposed. A city cooling perspective and a heat resilience plan follow, then delivery in steps, with monitoring and an impact assessment.

- **An urban heat resilience perspective, for the long term.** It covers five areas: buildings and infrastructure; working conditions and occupational heat protection; fair access to cooling and affordable energy; strategies co-created with citizens; and heat-health protection, social protection and services that keep running.

The framework treats this perspective as an extension of a city’s heat action plan or climate action plan, not a separate document. It notes that the Disaster Management (Amendment) Act, 2025 lets cities with municipal corporations set up urban disaster management authorities, and that Chennai, Bengaluru and Visakhapatnam are moving to establish them. One line from it sums up the spirit: Early warning systems are meaningful only when they are communicated to the ones most in need (p. 6).

Source: National Institute of Urban Affairs, Climate Centre for Cities (C-Cube), 2026, *Heat Resilient Cities Framework*, pp. 1-10, on [the publisher’s page](https://niua.in/publication-details/Heat_Resilient_Cities_Framework). Shared under CC BY-SA 4.0; [an unchanged copy (PDF, 5.8 MB)](https://beattheheat.asia/resources/guidance/niua-heat-resilient-cities-framework-2026.pdf) is also kept here, with credit to its publishers, in case the original moves. More of their work is at [niua.in/c-cube](https://niua.in/c-cube). The framework is guidance, not a heat action plan, so it is not counted in the archive above.

## How it was checked

- **Source verification was conducted on 1 November 2026.** For each document, the source content was retrieved and read to confirm that it is a relevant plan or guidance document, and the edition printed on it was recorded.

- **Verified means found, not endorsed.** Verification establishes that a relevant document was retrieved. It does not establish official adoption, the quality of implementation, or that the PDF can still be downloaded today.

- **A gap is a gap in this research.** Where a place has no document here, it means none was obtained in the searches so far, not that no plan exists.

- **A growing archive.** This is not a complete collection of every Indian plan or edition. Each release adds what new searches find and check, and documents first listed as gaps move into the archive once verified.

- **Each edition is its own record.** Translations in the same PDF count once. Where a hosting site labels a year differently from the document’s cover, the cover is used and the note says so.

- **Embedded plans are counted honestly.** For a heat-health plan inside a wider plan, the page count is for the whole document, and the note says where the heat chapter sits when that is known.

- **Drafts and caveats stay visible.** Drafts, proposals, presentation-format plans and uncertain editions are kept with the researchers' notes, rather than smoothed over.

Links open the source PDFs where they were found, on government and partner websites. Websites change; if a link no longer works, the catalogue ID and title will help find the document again, and we would be grateful to [hear about it](https://beattheheat.asia/corrections/).
