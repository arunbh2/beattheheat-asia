# A night in the heat

For many people, heat is not about a day’s pay. It is a room that will not cool, a body that cannot shed the heat, and no one who notices. Choose whose night to follow, decide what is in place, then live through it, hour by hour.

## Whose night will you follow?

- **A cooler roof**Reflective paint or shade over a thin metal roof, so the room cools after dark.
- **A fan she can afford to run**Bills or a subsidy that let the fan stay on all night.
- **The power stays on**No cut tonight, so the fan keeps turning.
- **Someone checks in**A neighbour or community health worker who calls or knocks.
- **A cool place, open at night**A cooling centre or cool community room within reach after dark.
- **A clinic warned and ready**The clinic heard the heat warning, with cool beds, fluids and staff ready, and open in the evening.

## The evidence behind the night

- Older people in a Kolkata informal settlement were 4.3 times more likely than older people in a nearby village to face dangerous heat, with a heat index of 45 °C or more, because their homes stayed hotter than the outdoors, especially at night.Source: [Weitz, Mukhopadhyay and Das, International Journal of Biometeorology, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35359160/)
- Each 1 °C rise raises the odds of preterm birth by about 5%, and a heatwave by about 16%.Source: [Chersich et al., BMJ, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33148618/)
- In 2014–2023, each infant in India faced an average of 7.7 heatwave days a year, 47% more than in the 1990s.Source: [Lancet Countdown, India data sheet, 2024](https://lancetcountdown.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Lancet-Countdown-2024_INDIA.pdf)
- Tin roofs, poor ventilation and crowded rooms can make homes hotter inside than out, leaving nowhere safe to rest. Thin roofs cool slowly, so the worst comes at night: in low-income homes measured across South Asia, tin-roofed houses stayed warmer indoors than the outdoor air.Source: [Frontiers in Built Environment, 2022: Ahmedabad informal settlements up to 7.6 °C hotter](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2022.874751/full)
- Older people living alone are among the most likely to die in a heatwave. Many victims of Chicago’s 1995 heatwave died alone at home.Source: [Semenza et al., New England Journal of Medicine, 1996: heat-related deaths in the July 1995 Chicago heatwave](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8649494/)
- When nights stay hot, the body has less chance to recover from the day, so the next day can start already depleted. Migrant workers in Singapore linked hot, crowded dormitories with poor sleep and tiredness on the next shift. A review of 11 studies finds heat linked to poorer sleep among people who work in it.Source: [Minor et al., One Earth, 2022: rising temperatures erode human sleep](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/rising-temperatures-erode-human-sleep-globally/)
