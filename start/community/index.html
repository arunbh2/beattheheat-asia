Why heat needs a shared response

Heat is a wicked problem. Every link runs through someone’s health, income or home, and many loop back to make things worse. The way out is shared.

No single owner, no single cause and no finish line. That is why protection works only when the people who hold each piece agree how it will work, before the hot weeks arrive.

05 / 05

Questions people ask first.

Is it free?

Yes. Everything is free to use, with no sign up. Text and data are shared under CC BY 4.0 (opens in a new tab), so you can copy, adapt and translate them with credit.

What happens to what I type?

It stays in your own browser. Plans, simulations and exercises are saved on your device, not sent to us. Visits are counted with GoatCounter, which uses no cookies and collects no personal data.

Where does the evidence come from?

From published studies, official guidance and reports, each linked where it is used. Where evidence is still emerging, the map says so. The method is explained on how this map works.

Who is behind it?

BeatTheHeat.Asia is being built by a group of organisations working on extreme heat. It is an independent work in progress, not an official publication of any one organisation. Listing an organisation does not imply its partnership, affiliation or endorsement.

Is this for campaigning?

No. It is a tool for cooperation: it helps the people and bodies who already hold responsibility agree how protection will work. It does not assign blame, and it never scores places or predicts illness.

Can I use it in a workshop or a community meeting?

Yes. There is a workshop guide for a 90 minute session for officials, funders and NGOs, and a community version for community groups and workers’ organisations.

Does it cover my city?

The map covers South and Southeast Asia, with pages for twelve cities, worker exercises for ten countries, and a planner that works for any ward, city or district you name.
