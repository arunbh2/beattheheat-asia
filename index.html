01 The reality

Heat does not stop at the thermometer.

Extreme heat affects income, health, mobility, productivity and the ability to work safely.

For millions of informal and outdoor workers, the problem is not simply whether a warning exists. It is whether they can afford to stop working, whether protection is available, and whether institutions respond in time.

Heat becomes economically dangerous not simply when temperatures rise, but when people lack the information, agency, institutions and financial protection required to act.

02 The question

What would it actually take to protect an informal worker before, during and after a dangerous heat event?

A warning only matters if people can act on it.

03 The protection chain

A heat warning is not protection.

Protection exists only when information triggers an action that a worker can actually use — and when institutions and finance make that action possible.

  1. 01

    Sense

  2. 02

    Decide

  3. 03

    Act

  4. 04
