# Corrections

Common Shade is built together, and it will sometimes be wrong. When it is, we say sorry, correct it in the open, and keep the record here.

- 1

### Tell us

A wrong figure, an unfair description, a broken link, a missing voice or a better source. Anyone can raise it, and no correction is too small.

- 2

### We check it with you

We look at the evidence together, and where we got it wrong, we say so plainly.

- 3

### We correct it in the open

The page changes, the change is listed below, and older figures are kept as superseded, so anyone can trace what changed and why.

[Suggest a correction](https://beattheheat.asia/join/#join-form)

## What we have corrected

- 26 November 2026 · Signals: El Niño set to bring more heat into 2027, Watch Common Shade grow

**What was wrong.** The El Niño record said, without qualification, that El Niño was expected to be the strongest on record by December and that WHO had, for the first time, classified its anticipated health impacts as a health emergency. Both phrases are WMO's, from its State of the Climate release.

**What changed.** The record now attributes both to WMO and qualifies them. The first is a forecast: NOAA's November discussion put the chance of a historic event at about 60%. On the second, WHO's own plan describes a Grade 3 response, its internal grading of its operational response, and no declaration of a public health emergency under the International Health Regulations was found. The growth entry that repeated the phrase was reworded the same way.

We are sorry. A forecast possibility read as a settled record, and another agency's description of WHO's response read as WHO's own declaration.

- 16 November 2026 · Privacy and terms

**What was wrong.** The privacy page listed the outside services the site asks, and said nothing else was sent anywhere. It left out one: Heat in the news asks Europe PMC, an open archive of research papers, for recent papers on heat in Asia.

**What changed.** The page now describes that request. The search is set by the page, so nothing a visitor types is sent; Europe PMC sees the request, as any website does, and the answer is kept in the browser for six hours.

We are sorry. A privacy page has to be complete to be trusted, and this one was not.

- 16 November 2026 · State of the field, October 2026

**What was wrong.** The October 2026 edition listed Bangladesh's first urban heatwave action plan, for Narayanganj, among the month's new records without its date, so it read as an October development.

**What changed.** The plan records its approval on 22 September 2025; October 2026 is when Bangladesh's heat and health assessment reported it. The edition now gives both dates, and keeps its first text below the revision log. The Atlas now dates the plan to 22 September 2025, reported October 2026.

We are sorry. A reader planning around this could have taken an older plan for a new one.

- 15 November 2026 · Bangkok city page (Heat over time)

**What was wrong.** For part of a day, the Bangkok page said its weather station shows a clear rise in hot days and in the hottest day of each year.

**What changed.** A new check compares each station with the regional model. Bangkok's daytime record shifts by about 0.7 °C against the model from 2006, which points to a change at the station rather than in the weather, so hot days and the hottest day now show their chart with no trend. The station's readings are not in doubt, and the rise in warm nights still stands.

We are sorry. A trend that cannot be told apart from a change at the station was shown as a clear rise before that check existed.

- 7 November 2026 · Cooling centres and check-ins (an action on the map), Affordable cooling for the poorest (an action on the map)

**What was wrong.** Two actions said more than the evidence shows: that public cooling spaces and check-ins save lives in every heatwave, and that efficient fans put safe cooling within reach of every household.

**What changed.** Both now say what the evidence supports and what it depends on. Cooling spaces can offer relief; how much they protect health depends on who can reach them, how long people stay, and the heat after they leave. Fans help in some conditions and not others, so cooling is matched to the local heat.

We are sorry. Readers plan real protection on these words, and they deserve the limits as well as the promise.

- November 2026 · Bangladesh’s first urban heatwave action plan, for Narayanganj

**What was wrong.** The Atlas placed Bangladesh’s first urban heatwave action plan, for Narayanganj, in October 2026, when it was reported. The plan records its approval on 22 September 2025.

**What changed.** Narayanganj City Corporation approved Bangladesh’s first urban heatwave action plan on 22 September 2025, as the plan itself records. Bangladesh’s first heat and health assessment, released in October 2026, reported it, alongside national guidelines for heat-related illness and a public campaign, “Beat the Heat”.

We are sorry. A plan approved in 2025 appeared on the Atlas as a 2026 step.

- 31 October 2026 · Across the site

**What was wrong.** Many pages spoke in an instructing voice, telling cities, employers, funders and others what to do.

**What changed.** We rewrote about 330 phrases to describe what helps, what it depends on and who could act, and added a check so the instructing voice cannot return. Laws, standards and quoted headlines keep their own words.

We are sorry. The people closest to the heat know their places best, and the site is here to learn alongside them, not to direct them.
