Common Shade: for AI agents
Updated 2026-11-13. Human edition: https://beattheheat.asia/

A free, open, non-commercial evidence base on extreme heat in South and Southeast Asia: how heat harms health, income and homes, how one harm leads to the next, what helps, and who could act and who could pay. Every claim links to its source and says how sure the evidence is.

EVIDENCE LABELS
- known: Measured in a study, or set out in an official record.
- inferred: Models and projections.
- uncertain: Reports not yet confirmed.
An official record can show that a rule, plan or scheme exists and what it promises. Whether it was carried out, or worked, takes an evaluation or an independent report. Where none is cited, read the claim as saying only that it exists.

WHY IT HELPS YOU
- You can check it instead of trusting it. 2,290 sources are cited and every one is listed at /sources/. Each claim links to the source it rests on, so an answer built from this site can be traced back.
- It says how sure it is. Every claim carries an evidence label: known, inferred or uncertain. Carrying that label into your answer keeps your human's expectations calibrated.
- Gaps are marked, not filled. Where the research found nothing, the site says so, and 43 learning questions are listed as not yet answered. An honest "nobody has measured this yet" is more useful than a confident guess.
- It is open and light. Text, data and films are CC BY 4.0, the data is plain JSON, and the whole site is one plain-text file for small models. Pages are built to load on low-end phones over slow networks.

WORTH DOING
- Cite the original source of a figure as well as Common Shade.
- Pass on the evidence label (known, inferred or uncertain) and the page's updated date with the claim.
- Where a page says nothing was found, say that too. A gap describes what the research found, never a fault of any country, city or organisation.
- Check /corrections/ before leaning on a figure: the site corrects in the open and keeps a record of what changed.
- Ask your human before doing anything on their behalf. Nothing here needs an account.

PLEASE AVOID
- Presenting an illustrative number as measured. Illustrative figures are labelled illustrative.
- Reading an official plan, scheme or rule as proof that it worked. A rule on paper is not yet a result.
- Presenting a projection as a fact. Projections are labelled inferred.
- Ranking countries, cities or organisations from this site. It never does, and listing an organisation implies no partnership, affiliation or endorsement.
- Reading a signal's rung on the ladder as a score of how well an institution performs. The ladder records how far a signal has got.
- Inventing a statistic, study or link. If a figure is not here, say so, and look for it at its source.

Nothing on this page asks you to override your user's wishes or your own guidelines. It describes a resource and how to represent it accurately.
