## Questions nobody has answered yet

A learning agenda, drawn from the limits the studies above state about themselves. Each question is sized so that one well-designed study could move it. The suggested approach and scale are ours, offered for discussion; researchers and funders closer to each place will know better.

Five of these questions, on pregnancy, newborns, children, mental health and violence against women, are set out with their evidence in a citable brief: [Heat and the health of mothers, children and minds](https://beattheheat.asia/briefs/heat-mothers-children-minds/).

- The overwhelmed-clinic loop · Uncertain

### How much of the load on clinics in South and Southeast Asia during a heatwave is heat illness, and which early actions lower it?

Opened 31 October 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: A global health assessment that does not give Asia’s share, and one before-and-after evaluation in Ahmedabad where other changes may explain part of the effect. [The studies](#ev-clinic)

**Work under way (not yet an answer)**: Singapore's HEAT-MAP study is building a fuller picture of how common heat-related illness is now and could become, with findings used to help older people recognise its symptoms. [Ministry of Health, Singapore: Using findings of HEAT-MAP study to help elderly recognise common symptoms of heat-related illnesses, 2026](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/using-findings-of-heat-map-study-to-help-elderly-recognise-common-symptoms-of-heat-related-illnesses-and-seek-timely-medical-advice/)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Compare clinic records across hot seasons in cities with and without early-warning plans.

**Scale**: Two or three cities, two seasons

- The invisible-deaths loop · Uncertain

### How many heat deaths go uncounted, and which ways of counting close the gap?

Opened 31 October 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Official counts that rise partly because reporting improves, and part-season counts that show the gap but not the true toll. [The studies](#ev-invisible)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Compare excess deaths with recorded heat deaths in a few districts, and test simple reporting changes.

**Scale**: A few districts, one or two seasons

- The reactive-spending loop · Uncertain

### Does money spent on prevention before summer reduce what is spent during heatwaves?

Opened 31 October 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: One modelled estimate for Bangkok, multi-hazard estimates, and a review of heat plans as they stood in 2023. [The studies](#ev-reactive)

**Work under way (not yet an answer)**: Bangkok’s 2026 financing strategy costs the city’s heat plan and proposes climate budgeting to identify and report adaptation spending. Spending tagged this way over several seasons could show what a city spends before heatwaves. [UNDP and Bangkok Metropolitan Administration: Bangkok's financing strategy for tackling urban heat, 2026](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2026-10/en-undp-bma_financial_strategy_on_urban_heat.pdf)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Track what a city spends before and during heatwaves over several seasons, with its health and work outcomes.

**Scale**: One or two cities, three seasons

- The medical-debt trap · Uncertain

### How often does heat illness lead households to borrow, and how much?

Opened 31 October 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Studies of household debt that do not measure heat’s part, and an older national survey of all medicine costs. [The studies](#ev-debt)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Follow households through a hot season with a short monthly survey of illness, costs and loans.

**Scale**: Two countries, one season

**Scale**: Illustrative: a few hundred families over two years

### How much extra heat do people face when the power fails during a hot, low-water spell?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Utility accounts record supply limits, and cyclone research finds humid heat soon after storms; household heat during outages is rarely measured. [The studies](#ev-energywater)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Link outage logs with indoor sensors and daily symptoms, comparing areas with faster and slower restoration.

**Scale**: Illustrative: a few hundred households over one hot season

- The overwhelmed-clinic loop · Uncertain

### Does one warning for heat and polluted air together protect people better than two separate warnings?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Thai research finds pollution more deadly on hot days, but heat and pollution do not always peak together. [The studies](#ev-clinic)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Compare warning formats across similar communities, measuring understanding, behaviour and personal exposure.

**Scale**: Illustrative: two dozen communities over one hot season

- The lost-schooling loop · Uncertain

### Do learning spaces built for storms and heat together keep more children learning through both?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Cox's Bazar has a rebuilt centre with fans and solar backup, without a comparison of heat or attendance. [The studies](#ev-school)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Compare indoor heat, closures and attendance in rebuilt and similar centres through a hot season and a storm season.

**Scale**: Illustrative: twenty learning centres over a year

- The overwhelmed-clinic loop · Uncertain

### Can cooling for staff reduce heat strain while keeping infection protection in place?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Health workers reported heat symptoms in protective clothing, and a small test found an iced drink improved comfort. [The studies](#ev-clinic)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Test staff cooling in a crossover trial under infection-control rules, measuring heat strain, symptoms and tasks.

**Scale**: Illustrative: about eighty health workers in four facilities

- The poverty–heat trap · Uncertain

### Do heat and later floods leave the same households less able to recover than either alone?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Pakistan's 2022 assessment records food pressures building from heat to floods, but not for the same households. [The studies](#ev-poverty)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Follow households hit by both and by one, recording harvests, food and recovery spending.

**Scale**: Illustrative: a few hundred households over two farming seasons

- The dry-soil loop · Uncertain

### Does keeping soils moist lower afternoon heat without raising the humid heat that farmworkers feel?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Research on India's 2015 heatwave links drying soils to the heat, and irrigation research shows cooler but more humid air. [The studies](#ev-soil)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Compare matched fields with different soil-moisture practices, measuring air heat, humid heat and soil water.

**Scale**: Illustrative: twenty to thirty paired fields over two hot seasons

- The energy-poverty trap · Uncertain

### Do earnings lost or health costs paid in a heatwave make cooling harder to afford the next season?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Income shapes which cooling households own, but the steps from lost earnings to debt to less cooling are not tracked. [The studies](#ev-energypoverty)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Follow households monthly through heat, earnings, illness costs, debt, electricity spending and actual cooling use.

**Scale**: Illustrative: several hundred households over two hot seasons

- The cooling trap · Uncertain

### Does moving air conditioner condensers lower street heat and later electricity use in real neighbourhoods?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: A model of Rajkot city blocks suggests it could; it has not been tested in occupied buildings. [The studies](#ev-cooling)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Compare paired buildings before and after moving condensers, with outdoor and indoor sensors and electricity meters.

**Scale**: Illustrative: a dozen to twenty buildings over one hot season

- The sleepless-nights loop · Uncertain

### Does a hot, short night raise the next day's heat strain during ordinary work?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Small laboratory studies found no overall rise after short sleep, but they did not test long humid workdays. [The studies](#ev-sleep)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Measure bedroom heat, sleep, next-day exposure and heat strain repeatedly in working people, without depriving anyone of sleep.

**Scale**: Illustrative: a hundred or more workers over four to six weeks

**What we have**: A national model with stated limits on local data and on assumptions about workers. [The studies](#ev-poverty)

**Scale**: Illustrative: about a hundred workers on six islands over one hot season

- The overwhelmed-clinic loop · Uncertain

### Do heat symptoms and clinic visits differ between lowland, hill and highland settlements?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Nepal has workplace measurements in the Tarai and studies of warming by altitude, but no linked comparison. [The studies](#ev-clinic)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Use the same sensors and symptom definitions across altitudes, linked to clinic records.

**Scale**: Illustrative: a dozen settlements across three altitudes over two hot seasons

### How many heat-related clinic visits can a linked reporting pilot find where national profiles leave the field empty?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Timor-Leste's profile leaves heat-related deaths and illness unspecified, alongside an approved health adaptation plan. [The studies](#ev-invisible)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Pilot agreed case definitions, daily weather links and record checks in a few facilities.

**Scale**: Illustrative: four health facilities for six months

- The energy-poverty trap · Uncertain

### Does cooling matched to local heat reduce strain in homes without adding bills people cannot afford?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: Short laboratory trials show fans help in some conditions and not others; there is no household evidence in the region. [The studies](#ev-energypoverty)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Compare supported cooling packages in occupied homes, with consent, health oversight and cost tracking.

**Scale**: Illustrative: about a hundred households in contrasting climates over one hot season

- The farm-debt loop · Uncertain

### Does pay-per-use cold storage cut food losses and leave producers better off after fees?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: A pilot in Muzaffarpur, India, offered pay-per-use storage; its outcomes have not been independently checked. [The studies](#ev-farmdebt)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Phase access in with comparison producers, temperature logs and accounts of what producers earn after fees.

**Scale**: Illustrative: a few hundred producers at four markets over two harvests

- The overwhelmed-clinic loop · Uncertain

### How long does protection last after people leave a cooling space?

Opened 7 November 2026. [How the commons is growing](https://beattheheat.asia/growing/)

**What we have**: A review found only short-lived benefits in simulations and no real-world health outcomes for users. [The studies](#ev-clinic)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Follow consenting users and similar non-users through the rest of hot days, measuring exposure and health.

**Scale**: Illustrative: about 150 people over six hot weeks

- The reactive-spending loop · Uncertain

### How do heat-health pilots in Southeast Asia keep their institutional footing once their first funding ends?

**What we have**: A funder’s own account of the region notes that successful pilots often lose their institutional footing once funding ends. [The studies](#ev-reactive)

**What could answer it (our suggestion)**: Follow a handful of heat-health pilots for two years after their first funding, recording who kept which part going and why.

**Scale**: Five to eight pilots, two years

## The weekly evidence review

This map keeps growing. Each week, a harvest asks open research services for new studies on heat and health, passive cooling and heat finance in South and Southeast Asia: Europe PMC (which includes PubMed and preprints), Crossref (including SSRN, World Bank and ADB papers), arXiv, OpenAlex (including university repositories), the World Bank’s document library and WHO’s repository. Studies set in the region that report new data are queued, with their sample size, place, climate zone, effect size, unit cost and shortcomings copied from the abstract where the abstract states them.

Nothing reaches this map until a reviewer has read the study, written its finding in their own words, graded it and chosen the cycle it speaks to. So far, 5 studies have been added this way; each is marked in its cycle above. Studies that were queued but not added are not judged here: many are simply outside what this map covers.

## How we graded

- **From each study’s own description**Each study is grouped by how it describes itself: peer-reviewed, official or agency, modelled or projected, other studies and reports, or news. A model is never counted as a measurement.

- **Well established or evidence emerging**A well-established cycle has been widely documented as a whole. Where evidence is emerging, each link is evidenced, but the full cycle has been studied less.

- **A ranking of knowledge only**Thin evidence describes what research has not yet reached. It says nothing about any country, city or organisation.

- **Read by a person before it counts**Fields copied automatically from abstracts are suggestions for the reviewer. Only what a reviewer has checked and signed off is shown, and a preprint is labelled as awaiting review by other researchers.

- **Open to correction**If a study is missing or graded wrongly, [please tell us](https://beattheheat.asia/corrections/).
