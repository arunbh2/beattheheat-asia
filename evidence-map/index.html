    The energy-poverty trapEvidence emerging 1 study · 0 peer-reviewed · 0 from the region

    People who can’t afford to cool fall ill. Illness brings debt, and debt puts cooling even further out of reach.

    • Official and agency Chilling Prospects: Global Access to Cooling Gaps 2023 Sustainable Energy for All, 2023 · Global, with country estimates · Global model
      Its limit: Risk of lacking cooling, estimated from income, power and heat data, not a direct survey.

    Open question: Who cannot afford to keep cool, measured directly rather than estimated?

    The displacement loopEvidence emerging A 2 studies · 0 peer-reviewed · 1 from the region

    People forced to move often land in heat-trapping housing, fall ill, lose income, fall further behind, and are forced to move again.

    • Modelled or projected Groundswell Part II World Bank, 2021 · East Asia and the Pacific · Regional model
      Its limit: A worst-case scenario to 2050; covers all climate drivers, not heat alone.
    • Modelled or projected Groundswell Part 2: Acting on Internal Climate Migration World Bank, 2021 · Six world regions · Global model
      Its limit: A worst-case scenario to 2050; covers all climate drivers, not heat alone.

    Open question: Does heat, on its own, push people to move within Asian countries, and where do they land?

    The protection-gap loopEvidence emerging AA 2 studies · 0 peer-reviewed · 2 from the region

    As insurers retreat, public budgets absorb the losses, protection is cut, and the risk drives insurers further away.

    • News reports Protection gap laid bare as Asian cat losses hit $65bn Swiss Re Institute sigma, reported by Global Reinsurance · Asia · Region-wide, 2025
      Its limit: Covers all natural catastrophes, not heat alone; heat losses are rarely insured at all.
    • Other studies and reports Climate Change & Insurance: Physical risks on the insurance industry Global Asia Insurance Partnership, 2026 · Asia · Region-wide review
      Its limit: A qualitative assessment; it does not measure the whole cycle in any one place.

    Open question: How large are uninsured losses from heat, and could money arranged in advance narrow the gap?

    The overwhelmed-clinic loopEvidence emerging A 2 studies · 2 peer-reviewed · 1 from the region

    Heat illness floods clinics. Overwhelmed clinics treat people later, and more cases become severe.

    • Peer-reviewed The 2024 report of the Lancet Countdown on health and climate change Lancet Countdown, The Lancet, 2024 · Global · Global indicators
      Its limit: A global figure; it does not give Asia’s share.
    • Peer-reviewed Pilot evaluation of the impact of India’s first heat action plan on all-cause mortality Hess et al., Journal of Environmental and Public Health, 2018 · Ahmedabad, India · One city, before and after the plan
      Its limit: A pilot evaluation; other changes over the same years could explain part of the drop.

    Open question: How much of the load on clinics in South and Southeast Asia during a heatwave is heat illness, and which early actions lower it?

    See this cycle on the map →

    The lost-schooling loopEvidence emerging AA 4 studies · 1 peer-reviewed · 2 from the region

    Children pulled out of school earn less as adults, and poorer families pull more children out of school.

    • Peer-reviewed Heat and Learning Park, Goodman, Hurwitz and Smith, American Economic Journal: Economic Policy, 2020 · United States · 10 million students
      Its limit: US schools and tests; the size of the effect in Asian classrooms is not known.
    • Official and agency Learning interrupted: global snapshot of climate-related school disruptions in 2024 UNICEF, 2025 · Global, by region · Global count from news and official reports
      Its limit: Counts students affected by closures or shorter days, not learning lost.
    • News reports Thousands of Philippine schools suspend in-person classes due to heat AFP via Malay Mail, April 2024 · Philippines · 5,288 schools, April 2024
      Its limit: A snapshot of one week, from official figures quoted in the press.
    • News reports Extreme heat forces school closures across Asia, affecting millions of students NBC News, 2024 · Bangladesh and Cambodia · National, April 2024
      Its limit: News figures from official announcements.

    Open question: How much learning do children lose in hot classrooms and heat closures in Asia?

    The reactive-spending loopEvidence emerging AAA 5 studies · 0 peer-reviewed · 3 from the region

    Stretched budgets skimp on prevention. Emergencies multiply, and costly emergency response stretches budgets further.

    • Other studies and reports Urban heat, mortality, and economic costs: evidence from Bangkok, Thailand Park and Rubinyi, World Bank working paper, 2026 · Bangkok, Thailand · One city, 2016–2018
      Its limit: An estimate from a statistical model; the value depends on how a life is priced.
    • Official and agency Adapt Now: A Global Call for Leadership on Climate Resilience Global Commission on Adaptation, 2019 · Developing countries · Global estimate
      Its limit: A broad benefit estimate covering many hazards, not heat alone.
    • Official and agency Spreading like Wildfire UNEP and GRID-Arendal, 2022 · Global · Global assessment
      Its limit: About wildfire budgets; we use it as a pattern for heat.
    • Other studies and reports How is India adapting to heatwaves? An assessment of heat action plans Centre for Policy Research, 2023 · India · 37 heat action plans
      Its limit: Plans as of 2023; several have been updated since.
    • News reports India updates disaster list to include heatwaves and lightning after Finance Commission push Ministry of Home Affairs, reply in India’s Lok Sabha, reported by Down To Earth, 2026 · India · National
      Its limit: A rule change; how money flows in practice is still to be seen.

    Open question: Does money spent on prevention before summer reduce what is spent during heatwaves?

    The dying-green loopEvidence emerging A 3 studies · 3 peer-reviewed · 1 from the region

    Heat and drought kill city trees. Without their shade the streets get hotter, and hotter streets kill more trees.

    • Peer-reviewed Climate change increases global risk to urban forests Esperon-Rodriguez et al., Nature Climate Change, 2022 · 164 cities in 78 countries · 3,129 species
      Its limit: Compares climate to each species’ natural range; trees can survive outside it with care.
    • Peer-reviewed Planning and maintaining nature-based solutions: Berlin, Jakarta, Melbourne and Santiago Hansen et al., chapter 10 in Nature-Based Solutions for Cities, 2023 · Berlin, Jakarta, Melbourne and Santiago · Four city cases
      Its limit: The tree figures are from Berlin; Asian evidence on tree loss in heat is thinner.
    • Peer-reviewed 50 Grades of Shade Middel et al., Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society · Tempe, United States · Street-level measurements
      Its limit: A desert city; humid cities gain less from shade alone, though shade still cuts radiant heat.

    Open question: Which street trees in humid Asian cities survive repeated heatwaves, and what care keeps them alive?

    The medical-debt trapEvidence emerging AAA 3 studies · 1 peer-reviewed · 3 from the region

    Treatment costs push families into debt. Debt removes any option to rest, and working through the heat brings more illness.

    • Other studies and reports Microfinance debt in Cambodia Human Rights Watch, 2025 · Cambodia · National loan data with interviews
      Its limit: Shows how heavy debt is; it does not measure the part heat plays.
    • Peer-reviewed Quantifying the financial burden of households’ out-of-pocket payments on medicines in India Selvaraj, Farooqui and Karan, BMJ Open, 2018 · India · National household survey, 2011–12
      Its limit: Older data on all medicine costs, not heat illness alone.
    • Other studies and reports The lived experiences of vulnerable populations in the context of extreme heat: five cities in Nepal and Bangladesh American Red Cross, Red Cross Red Crescent Climate Centre, Nepal Red Cross Society and Bangladesh Red Crescent Society, 2026 · Five cities in Nepal and Bangladesh · More than 2,000 residents
      Its limit: Reported experiences; it does not measure how often loans followed heat.

    Open question: How often does heat illness lead households to borrow, and how much?

    The green-displacement loopEvidence emerging A 4 studies · 4 peer-reviewed · 1 from the region

    Green space follows wealth. When greening reaches poorer districts without protection, rents rise, residents are pushed into hotter housing, and the gap grows.

    • Peer-reviewed Green gentrification in European and North American cities Anguelovski et al., Nature Communications, 2022 · 28 cities in Europe and North America · 28 cities, 2000–2016
      Its limit: No Asian cities in the sample.
    • Peer-reviewed Urban green space, public health and environmental justice: making cities “just green enough” Wolch, Byrne and Newell, Landscape and Urban Planning, 2014 · Mostly the United States · Literature review
      Its limit: Mostly US evidence.
    • Peer-reviewed Gentrification, urban informality and displacement in Bangkok Endo, The Developing Economies, 2026 · Bangkok, Thailand · One city, fieldwork
      Its limit: Qualitative; it does not measure how many people moved.
    • Peer-reviewed Towards just nature-based solutions for cities Chapter 3 in Nature-Based Solutions for Cities, 2023 · Global · Review
      Its limit: A framework for justice, not a measure of harm.

    Open question: When Asian cities add parks and trees, do rents rise and do residents move?

    The heat credit squeezeEvidence emerging AAAA 6 studies · 1 peer-reviewed · 4 from the region

    Heat empties shops and small firms miss repayments. Banks tighten credit, so firms can’t afford to adapt, and the next heatwave hits harder.

    • Other studies and reports The heat is on: Heat stress, productivity and adaptation among firms OECD Economics Department, Working Paper No. 1828, 2024 · 23 advanced economies · 2.7 million firms
      Its limit: Rich economies; small informal firms in Asia are not covered.
    • Peer-reviewed Heatwaves ripple into the financial system Aguilar-Gomez et al., summarised in VoxEU, 2024 · Mexico · National bank loan data
      Its limit: Outside Asia; how Asian banks respond is not yet measured.
    • News reports Micro-loan asset quality deteriorates in April–June quarter Sa-Dhan, reported by Business Standard, 2024 · India · National microfinance sector, one quarter
      Its limit: Heat is one named cause among several, not measured on its own.
    • Other studies and reports New study finds that microfinance is exacerbating climate precarity and harm in Cambodia King’s College London, 2023 · Cambodia · Fieldwork with borrowers
      Its limit: About climate shocks in general, not heat alone.
    • Other studies and reports Heatwave Havoc: the toll on Delhi’s street vendors Greenpeace India and National Hawkers Federation, 2024 · Delhi, India · 721 street vendors
      Its limit: Self-reported, from one city in one season.
    • News reports Passenger vehicle retail sales dip 7% in June as heatwave curtails showroom visits Federation of Automobile Dealers Associations, reported by Orissa Post, 2024 · India · National car retail, one month
      Its limit: Dealers’ own account; the link to their loans is not measured.

    Open question: How do small firms and their lenders in Asia respond when a heatwave empties streets and shops?

    The invisible-deaths loopEvidence emerging AAAAA 5 studies · 0 peer-reviewed · 5 from the region

    Heat deaths go uncounted, so heat protection goes unfunded, so more people die uncounted.

    • Official and agency Heat-related illness surveillance, 2021 to July 2024 (Unstarred Question 1693) Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, reply in India’s Rajya Sabha, 6 August 2024 · India · National surveillance
      Its limit: Counts what gets reported; reporting improves over time, so part of the rise is better counting.
    • News reports India likely undercounts heat-related deaths, tempering its response, officials say Associated Press via PBS NewsHour, 2024 · India · National, two agencies’ records
      Its limit: Compares two official counts in one year.
    • News reports Lost in the heat: the critical miscalculation in India’s heatwave mortality data Down To Earth, 2024 · India · National
      Its limit: Describes how deaths are recorded; it does not estimate the missing number.
    • News reports India updates disaster list to include heatwaves and lightning after Finance Commission push Ministry of Home Affairs, reply in India’s Lok Sabha, reported by Down To Earth, 2026 · India · More than 51,000 reporting units
      Its limit: A part-season count; it shows the gap, not the true toll.
    • Other studies and reports Heat stress reduces productivity among workers in Singapore NUS Project HeatSafe, 2024 · Singapore · 24 workplaces
      Its limit: One city; site conditions vary.

    Open question: How many heat deaths go uncounted, and which ways of counting close the gap?

    The farm-debt loopEvidence emerging AAA 3 studies · 3 peer-reviewed · 3 from the region

    A failed harvest pushes farmers into debt. Debt leaves less for seed, water and fertiliser, so the next harvest fails more easily.

    • Peer-reviewed Crop-damaging temperatures increase suicide rates in India Carleton, PNAS, 2017 · India · National, 1967–2013
      Its limit: An association that has been debated; it does not prove debt is the cause.
    • Peer-reviewed Extreme heat effects on wheat senescence in India Lobell, Sibley and Ortiz-Monasterio, Nature Climate Change, 2012 · Northern India · Wheat region, satellite records
      Its limit: About wheat only; it shows how models can miss losses.
    • Peer-reviewed Rice yields decline with higher night temperature from global warming Peng et al., PNAS, 2004 · Los Baños, Philippines · One research farm, 1992–2003
      Its limit: One site; later studies find varied effects across regions.

    Open question: When heat cuts a harvest, how often does debt follow, and what helps farmers recover?

    The poverty–heat trapWell established AAA 6 studies · 1 peer-reviewed · 3 from the region

    People who can’t afford to stop working fall ill. Illness costs income, and lost income makes stopping even less affordable.

      Its limit: State-level averages; neighbourhoods differ.

    Questions nobody has answered yet

    A learning agenda, drawn from the limits the studies above state about themselves. Each question is sized so that one well-designed study could move it. The suggested approach and scale are ours, offered for discussion; researchers and funders closer to each place will know better.

    Five of these questions, on pregnancy, newborns, children, mental health and violence against women, are set out with their evidence in a citable brief: Heat and the health of mothers, children and minds.

    1. The overwhelmed-clinic loop · Uncertain

      How much of the load on clinics in South and Southeast Asia during a heatwave is heat illness, and which early actions lower it?

      What we have
      A global health assessment that does not give Asia’s share, and one before-and-after evaluation in Ahmedabad where other changes may explain part of the effect. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Compare clinic records across hot seasons in cities with and without early-warning plans.
      Scale
      Two or three cities, two seasons

    2. The invisible-deaths loop · Uncertain

      How many heat deaths go uncounted, and which ways of counting close the gap?

      What we have
      Official counts that rise partly because reporting improves, and part-season counts that show the gap but not the true toll. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Compare excess deaths with recorded heat deaths in a few districts, and test simple reporting changes.
      Scale
      A few districts, one or two seasons

    3. The reactive-spending loop · Uncertain

      Does money spent on prevention before summer reduce what is spent during heatwaves?

      What we have
      One modelled estimate for Bangkok, multi-hazard estimates, and a review of heat plans as they stood in 2023. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Track what a city spends before and during heatwaves over several seasons, with its health and work outcomes.
      Scale
      One or two cities, three seasons

    4. The medical-debt trap · Uncertain

      How often does heat illness lead households to borrow, and how much?

      What we have
      Studies of household debt that do not measure heat’s part, and an older national survey of all medicine costs. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Follow households through a hot season with a short monthly survey of illness, costs and loans.
      Scale
      Two countries, one season

    5. The heat credit squeeze · Uncertain

      How do small firms and their lenders in Asia respond when a heatwave empties streets and shops?

      What we have
      Evidence from rich economies and from Mexico, an industry statement naming heat among several causes, and one self-reported survey of vendors in Delhi. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      With willing lenders, compare microfinance repayments against daily heat records.
      Scale
      Two or three lenders, two seasons

    6. The energy-poverty trap · Uncertain

      Who cannot afford to keep cool, measured directly rather than estimated?

      What we have
      A global model that estimates the risk of lacking cooling from income, power and heat data. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Add a few cooling questions to an existing national household survey.
      Scale
      One country, one survey round

    7. The lost-schooling loop · Uncertain

      How much learning do children lose in hot classrooms and heat closures in Asia?

      What we have
      Strong evidence from schools in the United States, and counts of closures in Asia that measure days lost rather than learning lost. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Compare learning results with classroom temperatures over a school year.
      Scale
      One country, one school year

    8. The farm-debt loop · Uncertain

      When heat cuts a harvest, how often does debt follow, and what helps farmers recover?

      What we have
      A debated national association, and studies from single sites or single crops. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Follow farm households across seasons, linking heat to yields, loans and recovery.
      Scale
      Two regions, three seasons

    9. The green-displacement loop · Uncertain

      When Asian cities add parks and trees, do rents rise and do residents move?

      What we have
      A multi-city study with no Asian cities, mostly US reviews, and one qualitative study in Bangkok. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Track rents and who lives where around new green spaces, before and after.
      Scale
      Two cities, three years

    10. The dying-green loop · Uncertain

      Which street trees in humid Asian cities survive repeated heatwaves, and what care keeps them alive?

      What we have
      Tree-loss figures from Berlin, and a global comparison of species’ natural climate ranges. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Survey street-tree survival after each hot season, with watering and care records.
      Scale
      Two cities, three seasons

    11. The displacement loop · Uncertain

      Does heat, on its own, push people to move within Asian countries, and where do they land?

      What we have
      Worst-case scenarios to 2050 that cover every climate driver together, not heat alone. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Link existing migration surveys with local heat records.
      Scale
      One or two countries

    12. The protection-gap loop · Uncertain

      How large are uninsured losses from heat, and could money arranged in advance narrow the gap?

      What we have
      Figures for all natural catastrophes together, and a qualitative regional review. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Estimate uninsured heat losses for one sector in one country, and test a pre-arranged payout design.
      Scale
      One sector, one country

    13. The poverty–heat trap · Uncertain

      Which protections lower heat risks in pregnancy in South and Southeast Asia: rest and lighter work, cooler homes, antenatal advice, or cash that lets women stop work?

      What we have
      A review of 70 studies in 27 countries, only seven of them low- or middle-income, and one cohort of 800 working women in Tamil Nadu; none tests what reduces the risk. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Add heat exposure and simple protections to antenatal records in a few districts, and compare outcomes across hot seasons.
      Scale
      Two or three districts, two to three years

    14. The overwhelmed-clinic loop · Uncertain

      How hot do labour wards and newborn units get in a heatwave, and what keeps them safe when the power fails?

      What we have
      A WHO, UNICEF and UNFPA call to action that names heat among the risks to mothers and newborns, and no measurements from facilities in the region that we have found. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Log indoor temperatures and power cuts in a sample of facilities through one hot season, alongside any cooling and back-up power they have.
      Scale
      Twenty to thirty facilities, one season

    15. The farm-debt loop · Uncertain

      Beyond farm households in India, how does heat affect self-harm and suicide across South and Southeast Asia, and does income support in bad seasons reduce it?

      What we have
      One national study of India over 47 years linking heat in the growing season to suicides through crop losses; little from cities or other countries. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Link daily temperatures with suicide and self-harm records where they exist, and study districts where crop insurance or cash reached households in bad years.
      Scale
      Two or three countries, existing records

    16. The poverty–heat trap · Uncertain

      Why is heat linked to more intimate partner violence in South Asia, and do income support, cooler homes or women’s groups weaken the link?

      What we have
      A cross-sectional study of 194,871 women in India, Nepal and Pakistan, using yearly temperatures; it shows an association, not the reasons for it. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Add heat and household strain questions to existing violence surveys, and compare places with and without seasonal income support.
      Scale
      Existing surveys, with a qualitative study in a few districts

    17. The poverty–heat trap · Uncertain

      How much does heat add to diarrhoea and undernutrition in young children in the region, and how much do safe water and oral rehydration offset it?

      What we have
      A review of 26 studies finding about 7% more diarrhoea per 1 °C, uneven across regions and with signs of publication bias. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Combine clinic records of child diarrhoea with temperature and water-supply data in a few towns over several seasons.
      Scale
      Three to five towns, three seasons

    18. The reactive-spending loop · Uncertain

      How do heat-health pilots in Southeast Asia keep their institutional footing once their first funding ends?

      What we have
      A funder’s own account of the region notes that successful pilots often lose their institutional footing once funding ends. The studies
      What could answer it (our suggestion)
      Follow a handful of heat-health pilots for two years after their first funding, recording who kept which part going and why.
      Scale
      Five to eight pilots, two years

    Know of a study that answers one of these, or a better question? Tell us, and we will add it with credit.

    The weekly evidence review

    This map keeps growing. Each week, a harvest asks open research services for new studies on heat and health, passive cooling and heat finance in South and Southeast Asia: Europe PMC (which includes PubMed and preprints), Crossref (including SSRN, World Bank and ADB papers), arXiv, OpenAlex (including university repositories), the World Bank’s document library and WHO’s repository. Studies set in the region that report new data are queued, with their sample size, place, climate zone, effect size, unit cost and shortcomings copied from the abstract where the abstract states them.

    Nothing reaches this map until a reviewer has read the study, written its finding in their own words, graded it and chosen the cycle it speaks to. The first reviewed studies will appear in their cycles above, marked as added in the weekly review. Studies that were queued but not added are not judged here: many are simply outside what this map covers.

      How we graded

      • From each study’s own descriptionEach study is grouped by how it describes itself: peer-reviewed, official or agency, modelled or projected, other studies and reports, or news. A model is never counted as a measurement.
      • Well established or evidence emergingA well-established cycle has been widely documented as a whole. Where evidence is emerging, each link is evidenced, but the full cycle has been studied less.
      • A ranking of knowledge onlyThin evidence describes what research has not yet reached. It says nothing about any country, city or organisation.
      • Read by a person before it countsFields copied automatically from abstracts are suggestions for the reviewer. Only what a reviewer has checked and signed off is shown, and a preprint is labelled as awaiting review by other researchers.
      • Open to correctionIf a study is missing or graded wrongly, please tell us.
