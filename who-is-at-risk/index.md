# Who extreme heat hits hardest

The same heatwave lands differently. Who is harmed depends on how exposed people are and what stands between them and safety.

[Open the interactive version](https://beattheheat.asia/#at-risk)

## Five questions behind every barrier

- **Who receives the warning** Alerts by app, radio or loudspeaker miss people without phones, signal, literacy or the right language.
- **Who can move to safety** Age, disability, care duties and distance decide who can reach shade, water or a cooling centre.
- **Who can keep earning** Without land, credit, contracts or savings, resting in the heat means losing the day’s income.
- **Who has a say** Women, migrants and people from excluded communities are often missing from the rooms where heat plans are made.
- **Who can reach care in time** A clinic that is far away, has no way to cool patients, or is open only while people are at work turns treatable heat illness into a death.

These barriers rarely come alone. [Intersections](https://beattheheat.asia/intersections/) is a short note and a game about where they meet, and who protection reaches.

## Smallholder and rainfed farming households

**Exposed to:** Work in open fields through the hottest hours, with crops and animals that depend on rain heat makes less reliable.

**Held back by:** Little irrigation, credit, insurance or savings, so one bad season can mean debt, sold animals and lost seed.

At the International Rice Research Institute’s farm in the Philippines, rice yields fell by about 10% for each 1 °C rise in growing-season night temperature. [Peng et al., PNAS, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0403720101)

In India, crop-damaging heat in the growing season is linked to more suicides; warming over 30 years is estimated to account for 59,300. [Carleton, PNAS, 2017: crop-damaging temperatures increase suicide rates in India](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28760983/)

On 25 April 2024, the UN reported drought in 41 Philippine provinces during a hot, dry spell, as FAO and partners activated anticipatory action in Isabela and Bangsamoro. [United Nations in the Philippines, 25 April 2024](https://philippines.un.org/en/267032-un-calls-increased-support-climate-resilience-philippines-drought-wreaks-havoc)

**What helps:** [Crop insurance and fair credit](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#farmcredit), [Keep water in the soil](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#soilwater), [Smart irrigation and groundwater rules](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#irrigate), [Social protection floors](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#socialprotection), [Mental health in heat plans](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#mindheat)

## Coastal and riverine communities

**Exposed to:** Humid heat that stops sweat from cooling the body, warming seas that drive fish away, and warm, low water that spoils drinking supplies.

**Held back by:** Livelihoods tied to one place and one resource, homes on fragile land with insecure tenure, and islands and river banks far from services.

Humidity makes heat far more costly: across 2.7 million firms, ten extra days above 35 °C cut productivity by 0.75% in very humid places, more than double the average, and barely at all in dry ones. [OECD Economics Department, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1787/19d94638-en)

In Viet Nam’s Mekong Delta, a long heatwave in February 2024 lowered canals and let sea water push inland; farmers who pumped the salty water onto their rice lost up to half their crop. [Viet Nam News, 2024](https://vietnamnews.vn/environment/1656592/mekong-delta-faces-severe-drought-salinity-concerns.html)

**What helps:** [Heat action plans](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#hap), [Social protection floors](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#socialprotection), [Anticipatory action](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#anticipatory), [Heat-ready health services](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#readyclinics)

## People in remote or poorly served settlements

**Exposed to:** Far from clinics, cool shelter, reliable power and phone signal, often at the end of the line for water and electricity.

**Held back by:** Warnings, ambulances and relief arrive late or not at all, and deaths here are the least likely to be counted.

**What helps:** [Anticipatory action](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#anticipatory), [Heat-ready health services](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#readyclinics), [Count every heat death](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#countdeaths), [Heat action plans](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#hap)

**Watch:** [Clinics in a heatwave (0:33)](https://beattheheat.asia/media/glossary/clinicheat.mp4), [When phones go quiet (0:34)](https://beattheheat.asia/media/glossary/telecomheat.mp4).

## People in informal settlements

**Exposed to:** Dense rows of homes with tin, asbestos or plastic roofs, little green space, and no way to cool down at night.

**Held back by:** Insecure tenure means families can’t invest in cooler homes and landlords won’t, and settlements are often missing from city plans and services.

In a survey of 860 households in Ahmedabad, people in informal settlements faced temperatures up to 7.6 °C higher than people in formal housing. [Mahadevia et al., 2020, reported in Frontiers in Built Environment, 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2022.874751/full)

In Jakarta, dense low-income neighbourhoods act as heat islands; informal workers rarely have fans or air conditioning and cool off on terraces or in parks, while green space is concentrated in wealthier areas. [Ufaira and others, Frontiers in Human Dynamics, 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-dynamics/articles/10.3389/fhumd.2023.1237515/full)

In a model of 16 buildings in Rajkot, air conditioner condensers on outside walls could warm the street air by more than 1 °C. [Scientific Reports, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-62477-4)

In consultations for Bangkok's financing strategy, participants said residents who use local services but are not registered in Bangkok mean some project budgets have to stretch to cover them. [UNDP and Bangkok Metropolitan Administration: Bangkok's financing strategy for tackling urban heat, 2026](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2026-10/en-undp-bma_financial_strategy_on_urban_heat.pdf)

**What helps:** [Cool, affordable housing](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#coolhousing), [Cool nights and support](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#coolnights), [Cooling centres and check-ins](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#coolingcentres), [Affordable cooling for the poorest](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#affordablecool)

## Workers in heat-exposed jobs

**Exposed to:** Street vendors, builders, salt-pan workers, waste pickers, rickshaw and delivery workers, traffic police and sanitation staff in the hottest parts of the city; factory workers beside furnaces in unventilated sheds; then hot, crowded homes at night.

**Held back by:** No paid leave, contract or savings: stopping work in dangerous heat means losing the day’s income. Fixed shifts leave little room to avoid the hottest hours.

Doctors at two Indian hospitals with heatstroke units said more than 90% of the patients they treated for heat illness in 2024 were outdoor workers: security guards, migrant labourers and street vendors. [Context (Thomson Reuters Foundation), 2024](https://www.context.news/climate-risks/ice-baths-and-ventilators-indias-hospitals-adapt-to-killer-heat)

In a survey of 721 Delhi street vendors in 2024, 80% saw fewer customers during heatwaves and 49% lost income. [Greenpeace India and National Hawkers Federation, 2024](https://www.greenpeace.org/india/en/press/16737/new-report-by-greenpeace-india-and-national-hawkers-federation-reveals-harsh-heatwave-toll-on-street-vendors/)

In Singapore, a study of 355 workers found each hot day cost a median S$21 in lost income, about 24% of a day’s median pay, and heat at workplaces was often higher than nearby weather stations showed. [NUS Project HeatSafe, 2024](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/PRESS-RELEASE_Project-HeatSafe-18MAR2024-FOR-IMMEDIATE-RELEASE.pdf)

Few delivery platforms in Asia make concessions to riders in hot weather, and riders may not be allowed into restaurants to pick up orders, get a drink of water or use the washroom, according to a source quoted by Eco-Business. [Eco-Business: Heatwave fee for Asia's gig workers does little to cool climate stress](https://www.eco-business.com/news/heatwave-fee-for-asias-gig-workers-does-little-to-cool-climate-stress/)

In Dhaka, Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Phnom Penh and Karachi, garment hubs where factories trap heat, days with wet-bulb temperatures above 30.5 °C rose by 42% in 2020–24 compared with 2005–09. [Cornell Global Labor Institute and Schroders, Higher Ground?](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/global-labor-institute/higher-ground-fashions-climate-breakdown)

In Tamil Nadu, 68% of people face 8–9 hours a day of strong heat stress from March to June, measured by an index that counts humidity, wind and sun as well as temperature. [Tamil Nadu Heat Mitigation Strategy, 2024](https://spc.tn.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/Heat_Mitigation_Strategy.pdf)

In East Kalimantan, Indonesia, 361 workers doing 90-minute tasks on cleared land faced heat stress readings 2.84 °C higher than in forest. [Masuda et al., 2021 (PMC7952402)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7952402/)

## Women whose livelihoods are held back by unequal access

**Exposed to:** Field, home-based and informal work, fetching water, and cooking in homes that trap heat.

**Held back by:** Less say over land, credit, technology and household decisions, and most of the unpaid care that grows in a heatwave. Without toilets at work or on transport, many drink less water. Violence at home rises with the heat.

Rural households headed by women lose about 8% more of their income to heat stress than those headed by men. [FAO, The Unjust Climate, 2024](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/fao-report--heatwaves-and-floods-affect-rural-women-and-men-differently--widen-income-gap)

In a study of Indian women workers, 87% of those with poor or no toilets at work reported genitourinary problems, and about 1 in 10 showed heat strain and moderate dehydration. [Venugopal et al., Global Health Action, 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5025522/)

In Nepal’s Madhesh Province, women cooking in hot, smoky kitchens carry a daily ‘thermal labour’ that falls hardest on women in the poorest single-room homes with little access to clean energy or credit. [Ghimire and others, Frontiers in Public Health, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1657267/full)

Personal sensors worn by women aged 40 to 79 in rural Tamil Nadu sometimes read more than 5 °C apart within the same hour, possibly from cooking on solid fuels. [Deshpande et al., 2023 (PMC10785747)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10785747/)

In April 2024, about 73,900 households in the Mekong Delta were short of water, and women and girls carried most of the extra fetching. [UNICEF Viet Nam, 27 April 2024](https://www.unicef.org/media/156076/file/Viet_Nam_Humanitarian_Situation_Report_No._01_%28drought%29_reporting_period__-27_April_2024.pdf)

Among women garment workers in Phnom Penh, reported heat symptoms rose from 68% in cool months to 88% in hot months. [Chea et al., BMC Public Health, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-025-22787-0)

## Pregnant women, babies and young children

**Exposed to:** Pregnancy strains the body’s cooling, and babies overheat and dehydrate faster than adults.

**Held back by:** Many women keep working and fetching water late into pregnancy, and maternity and child services are rarely ready for heat.

Each 1 °C rise raises the odds of preterm birth by about 5%, and a heatwave by about 16%. [Chersich et al., BMJ, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33148618/)

In 2014–2023, each infant in India faced an average of 7.7 heatwave days a year, 47% more than in the 1990s. [Lancet Countdown, India data sheet, 2024](https://lancetcountdown.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Lancet-Countdown-2024_INDIA.pdf)

In Tamil Nadu, among 800 pregnant women doing moderate to heavy physical work, those with high workplace heat had about twice the risk of miscarriage. [Rekha et al., BJOG, 2023: occupational heat and pregnancy outcomes in 800 women workers, Tamil Nadu](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37814395/)

Across 26 studies, each 1 °C rise in temperature was linked to about 7% more diarrhoea, a leading cause of death in young children. [Carlton et al., International Journal of Epidemiology, 2016: temperature and diarrhoeal disease, 26 studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26567313/)

In Dhaka, diarrhoea admissions among children under 5 rose by 13.9% on heatwave days, in hospital records from 1981 to 2010. [PLOS Global Public Health, 2024](https://journals.plos.org/globalpublichealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgph.0003629)

Among 800 pregnant women doing physical work in Tamil Nadu, 47.3% had high heat exposure at work. [Rekha et al., BJOG, 2023 (PubMed 37814395)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37814395/)

**What helps:** [Heat-ready health services](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#readyclinics), [Heat action plans](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#hap), [Free care for heat illness](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#freecare), [Social protection floors](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#socialprotection), [Heat-safe pregnancy and newborn care](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#maternalheat), [Safe water and rehydration for young children](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#childwater)

## Older people

**Exposed to:** Bodies that sense and shed heat less well, often with heart, kidney or lung conditions.

**Held back by:** Living alone, limited mobility, and warnings and help that rely on phones, apps or someone knocking on the door.

Older people in a Kolkata informal settlement were 4.3 times more likely than older people in a nearby village to face dangerous heat, with a heat index of 45 °C or more, because their homes stayed hotter than the outdoors, especially at night. [Weitz, Mukhopadhyay and Das, International Journal of Biometeorology, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35359160/)

In Chiang Rai, older people and their families described changing daily routines to cope with heat, and the help families give to keep them going. [Chiang Rai study, 2023 (PubMed 37372754)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37372754/)

Bhutan's 2015 WHO profile projects about 49 heat-related deaths per 100,000 people aged 65 or over by 2080 if emissions stay high. It is a projection, not a count. [WHO, 2015: climate and health country profile, Bhutan](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/searo/wsh-och-searo/bhu-c-h-profile.pdf?sfvrsn=9b974adb_2)

In consultations for Bangkok's financing strategy, participants said heat response may not reach older and bedridden people living in metal-roofed homes, who are highly affected by heat. [UNDP and Bangkok Metropolitan Administration: Bangkok's financing strategy for tackling urban heat, 2026](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2026-10/en-undp-bma_financial_strategy_on_urban_heat.pdf)

## People with disabilities or long-term illness

**Exposed to:** Some conditions and common medicines, such as diuretics, interfere with the body’s cooling. Heart, kidney and lung disease and diabetes raise the danger, and heat worsens many mental health conditions.

**Held back by:** Warnings in formats they can’t use, cooling centres they can’t reach, and heat plans that rarely name their needs.

Heat can worsen underlying illness, including heart disease, diabetes, asthma and mental health conditions. [WHO, 2026: Heat and health, fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/climate-change-heat-and-health)

In interviews across three Bangladesh locations in 2025, people with disabilities described heat making it harder to move about and to get care, and leaning on family for help. [International Journal of Disaster Risk Science, 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13753-026-00778-y)

Among 272 people with chronic kidney disease at a Sri Lankan hospital in 2019, the median monthly cost of haemodialysis care was LKR 38,540, including income lost by patients and carers. The study did not look at heat. [Rathnayake et al., BMC Health Services Research, 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-026-15146-1)

## Migrants, prisoners and other excluded groups

**Exposed to:** Hot, crowded rented rooms, cells and the hardest outdoor jobs, often in a place where they have few connections.

**Held back by:** No local ID, address or language can mean no warnings, clinics, rations or relief. Many migrant workers are not registered under welfare laws, such as construction workers’ boards, so they miss health and safety benefits meant for them. Who is registered, and who is known to local officials, can decide who is reached first.

Tamil Nadu’s heat strategy names prisoners, held in concrete buildings with little green space, among the groups heat plans are meant to reach, and proposes cool roofs and trees for its jails. [Tamil Nadu Heat Mitigation Strategy, 2024](https://spc.tn.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/Heat_Mitigation_Strategy.pdf)

**What helps:** [Cool, affordable housing](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#coolhousing), [Social protection floors](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#socialprotection), [Free care for heat illness](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#freecare), [Heat-safe work](https://beattheheat.asia/actions/#heatrules)

## Weather, work and the person

The same reading on a thermometer can be safe for one person and dangerous for another. How much heat the body has to carry depends on three things together: the weather, the work, and the person. Change any one and the danger changes.

### The weather

Air temperature is only the start. Humidity stops sweat from cooling the body, sun and hot surfaces add radiant heat, still air removes less of it, and a hot night leaves no time to recover. Conditions at a workplace or under a roof can be worse than the nearest weather station shows.

- Monitors at 24 indoor and outdoor workplaces in Singapore found workers could face more heat stress than nearby national weather stations reported. [NUS Project HeatSafe, 2024](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/PRESS-RELEASE_Project-HeatSafe-18MAR2024-FOR-IMMEDIATE-RELEASE.pdf)
- Ten extra days above 35 °C cut firms’ productivity by 0.75% in very humid places, more than double the average. [OECD Economics Department, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1787/19d94638-en)

### The work

Heavy work makes heat inside the body. Protective clothing, gowns and masks hold it in. Long shifts, fixed hours and no shade or water break let it build. The same weather is a different risk for a desk worker, a builder and a nurse in protective gear.

- In a laboratory trial of 18 men doing treadmill work and virtual-reality welding and plank-walking at height, humid heat raised core temperature and unsteadied balance, though task speed and accuracy held up. [Tan et al., Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise, 2025](https://heathealth.info/resources/physical-work-in-humid-heat-impairs-postural-balance-during-simulated-construction-tasks-at-height/)
- Across 3,082 health workers in India, Indonesia, Singapore, Taiwan, Thailand and Vietnam, poor practices with protective equipment were linked to heat-related symptoms. [Yang et al., BMC Nursing, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12912-024-01770-x)

### The person

Older bodies sweat less and feel thirst later. Heart, kidney and lung disease, diabetes and pregnancy add strain, and some medicines reduce the body’s ability to lose heat. A body that did not cool down overnight starts the next day already behind.

- Thinking held up at modest heat strain: 28 adults whose core temperature reached about 38.5 °C showed no loss of executive function or risk-taking. At 39.5 °C, in a separate study of 11 adults, executive function did fall. [Modest strain study of 28 adults, PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12804366/) [Tan et al., Journal of Sport and Health Science, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2023.09.001)
- Hot nights shorten sleep, with bigger losses for older people and in lower-income countries, across 47,628 people in 68 countries. [Minor et al., One Earth, 2022](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/rising-temperatures-erode-human-sleep-globally)

Effects depend on exposure, task and the person. That is why a single heat alert cannot protect everyone in the same way, and why plans can ask who is doing what, in what, and after what kind of night.

### Three groups heat plans often miss

**People on long-term medicines.** Some medicines used for long-term conditions, including some for blood pressure, heart disease, Parkinson’s and Alzheimer’s disease, blood thinners and some cancer drugs, can reduce sweating or the blood flow to the skin that carries heat away. Ageing and illness can add to the effect. Never stop or change a medicine because of the heat. Ask a doctor, nurse or pharmacist what to watch for, and plan water, shade and check-in calls for people on these medicines. [Wee and Lee, Pharmacological Reviews (summary by NUS Medicine, 2023)](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/news/medications-for-chronic-diseases-affect-the-bodys-ability-to-regulate-body-temperature-keep-cool)

**Health workers in protective gear.** Clinics and wards fill up in a heatwave, and staff in gowns, gloves and masks carry heat they cannot shed. A survey of 3,082 health workers across six Asian places linked poor practices with protective equipment to heat-related symptoms. Its symptom scale proved unreliable, so the authors still advise staff to check their own symptoms early. Plan cool rest rooms, water, shorter rotations in full protective gear, and cooling for the clinic itself, so the people treating heat illness are not its next patients. [Yang et al., BMC Nursing, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12912-024-01770-x)

**Workers in hot factories and sheds.** Heat is not only an outdoor problem. In a survey of 753 women at three garment factories in Phnom Penh, the share reporting heat symptoms rose from 68% in the cooler months to 88% in the hot months, as the factory heat index rose from about 25 °C to 29 °C. More said heat slowed their work and cut their motivation. These are workers’ own reports, not measured output. Plans can count indoor workplaces: measure heat where people work, cool the roof and walls, add air flow, water and breaks. [Chea et al., BMC Public Health, 2025](https://bmcpublichealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12889-025-22787-0)

**For heat plans:** Ask three questions for every group in a heat plan: how hot is it where they are, what are they doing and wearing, and what do they bring to it, such as age, illness, medicines and a night without sleep.
