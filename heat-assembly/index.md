# The Heat Assembly

A shaded hall is open beside the market, and almost nobody who needs it can use it. Take a seat at the table, make agreements over four rounds of heat, and see who could take a break, whose earnings changed and why.

FictionalThe market town, the people and the events here are made up. The direction of each relationship comes from the sources listed below; every number in the game is a game mechanic, not a measurement.

11:40 a.m. · A made-up market town on a river plain Fictional

The public hall beside the market is open, shaded and paid for. It is nearly empty.

Out in the loading lane, porters wait in a queue for the next truck. Leaving the queue means going to the back of it, and maybe losing the next paid load.

Across the square, a caretaker is due at the childcare centre this afternoon. The same caretaker is the only person who can keep the hall open.

A forecast has just come in: a hot day tomorrow, a hot night after it, and a second heat alert to follow.

Loading the game

By the end, you could explain from your own game how a decision about pay, care, staffing or authority changed who could use heat protection. About 10 minutes on your own, or 15 to 20 minutes around a table.

## The people

- Tara, queue porter, paid per load

Paid for each load carried. A place in the queue is the only security in the job. No one at home depends on Tara during the day.

**Needs:** A midday break in the hall, without losing a place in the queue.

- Jeevan, porter on a day rate

Hired by one trader for the whole day at a fixed rate, so a break costs Jeevan no earnings. Free to rest whenever the hall is open.

**Needs:** Any open hour in the hall.

- Noor, home-based stitcher, cares for a child of three

Paid for each piece finished at home. The hours the childcare centre is open are the hours Noor can work. Looks after a child of three.

**Needs:** Childcare at midday and in the afternoon; nothing on the table cools Noor's home in the day.

- Kiran, stall holder with a fixed pitch

Sells from a pitch Kiran holds. A neighbour can mind the stall in the afternoon lull, not at the midday peak. Lives in a solid house that cools at night.

**Needs:** An afternoon break in the hall.

- Hira, older tea seller at the loading bay

Earns from daily sales at the bay. Cannot walk to the hall in the heat; a rickshaw costs a day's money.

**Needs:** Water and shade at the bay itself.

## The seats at the table

- Workers' representative

**Resource:** Two rota days for the whole game: a day when a porter on a fixed rate covers the queue so others can rest.

**Constraint:** On a rota day, Jeevan covers the loads at midday, so Jeevan can rest only in the afternoon.

**What the others need to hear:** Leaving the queue costs a porter paid per load about two loads. A porter on a day rate loses nothing.

- Market association

**Resource:** The loading order.

**Constraint:** Changing the queue rule takes one round's notice, and the loads still have to move: every round the rule runs, someone has to cover them.

**What the others need to hear:** Trucks run late on hot days, so midday is the heaviest unloading.

- Municipal officer

**Resource:** An operating budget of three units for the whole game, and authority over the public hall.

**Constraint:** Staff money approved now buys cover next round. Hiring on the day costs twice as much and gives less.

**What the others need to hear:** The hall's funding covers the building, not extra staff hours.

- Neighbourhood services coordinator

**Resource:** A caretaker who can work two blocks a day (one at night) and an attendant who can work one.

**Constraint:** One person, one service, one block at a time. Whoever works the evening is not there next morning.

**What the others need to hear:** Noor's paid hours are the childcare hours. Hira cannot walk to the hall.

## Four rounds

- **The forecast** 11:40 a.m., the day before the heat
- **The first hot market day** Midday and afternoon
- **The hot night and the morning after** Evening and next morning
- **The second heat alert** Midday and afternoon

Funded, open and usable are different things here. The hall can be paid for and staffed, and still be out of reach for someone who would lose a paid turn by leaving the queue.

In your own market, workplace or neighbourhood, who could change the equivalent of the queue rule?

## Assumptions

- Budget, rota days and staff blocks are counted in simple game units, not money or hours from any real place.
- Simplifying assumption: each household has a reserve of 0 to 3 days of money. Losing a day's earnings lowers it by one. Someone pays their own way to a break only if at least one day remains afterwards, so a loss now makes the next break harder, which can mean a further loss. A loss when the reserve is already empty is counted as a day the household could not cover.
- A cool evening adds one day to the reserve of someone who spends it in the hall, standing in for recovering ground before the next day; a solid house does the same for Kiran.
- Money approved ahead buys staff one round later; hiring on the day costs twice as much and gives less. A queue rule agreed now runs from the next round.
- The game counts breaks, earnings and reserves. It does not predict illness or death, and a break in the hall is not a measured health benefit.

## Sources

The direction of each relationship comes from evidence already on Common Shade. The sizes are the game's own.

- **Why a break costs a porter paid per load more than one on a day rate.** Piece-rate pay and fixed deadlines that make stopping cost money, and no inspection. [NUS Project HeatSafe, 2024](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/PRESS-RELEASE_Project-HeatSafe-18MAR2024-FOR-IMMEDIATE-RELEASE.pdf)
- **Why an open hall can stay unusable for someone who cannot leave work.** A cooled building nobody can reach, open at the wrong hours, or a worker who cannot leave work to use it. [US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, in the GHHIN resource library](https://heathealth.info/wp-content/uploads/cdc_47657_DS1-compressed-1.pdf)
- **The same limit for worker cooling stations.** A station nobody can leave work to use, or one that closes when the day is hottest. [People’s Courage International, 2025: emergency response to the heatwave crisis in South and Southeast Asia](https://www.peoplescourageinternational.org/pdfs/Emergency%20response%20to%20heat%20wave%20crisis%20in%20South%20and%20Southeast%20Asia.pdf)
- **Why a hot day without protection lowers earnings.** In Singapore, a study of 355 workers found each hot day cost a median S$21, about 24% of a day’s pay. [NUS Project HeatSafe, 2024](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/PRESS-RELEASE_Project-HeatSafe-18MAR2024-FOR-IMMEDIATE-RELEASE.pdf)
- **Why traders lose income in a heatwave.** Of 721 Delhi street vendors surveyed in 2024, 80% saw fewer customers during heatwaves and 49% lost income. [Greenpeace India and National Hawkers Federation, 2024](https://www.greenpeace.org/india/en/press/16737/new-report-by-greenpeace-india-and-national-hawkers-federation-reveals-harsh-heatwave-toll-on-street-vendors/)
- **Why informal workers cut hours in the heat.** In Bangkok, nearly 80% of informal workers surveyed had cut their hours because of heat. [WIEGO and HomeNet Thailand, 2025](https://www.wiego.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/WIEGO-Policy-Brief-33-Climate-Change-and-Heat-Stress.pdf)
- **Why spending that starts when the heat does arrives late.** Procurement that starts when the heat does. [The Hitavada, 11 May 2026](https://www.thehitavada.com//Encyc/2026/5/11/green-nets-at-city-squares-relief-measure-or-another-half-planned-half-planned-civic-experiment.html)
- **Why money helps most when it arrives in time.** Social protection can cushion the income workers lose to extreme heat, if it is designed to arrive in time. [ILO: addressing the impacts of extreme heat through social protection](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/addressing-impacts-extreme-heat-through-social-protection)
- **Why the game makes no claim about health outcomes.** A 2025 review found no study that measured health outcomes for people using cooling centres; in simulated heatwaves, the benefits were short-lived. That is not evidence of no benefit. [Dearman et al., UKHSA, 2025: public health effectiveness of cooling centres, a systematic review](https://researchportal.ukhsa.gov.uk/en/publications/public-health-effectiveness-of-cooling-centres-during-periods-of-/)
