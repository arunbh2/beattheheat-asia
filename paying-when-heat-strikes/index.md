# Paying when heat strikes

Most money set aside for disasters was designed for floods, storms and earthquakes, which break things that can be counted. Heat mostly takes income, health and working days, quietly. This section gathers what exists to pay when heat arrives, and asks, gently, one question.

Which layer could carry heat?

- **98**schemes and arrangements we documented
- **54**name heat in their cover
- **18**with a payment we could trace, amount and date

Counts are of what we documented, not of everything that exists. Money is never added up across kinds.

The [protection-gap loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#protection)

A small business owner seeking insurance

After several bad years, insurers no longer cover heat losses for businesses like hers. When a heatwave hits, she turns to public relief, which strains the budget meant for protection.

The [reactive-spending loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#reactive)

A municipal budget officer

With money stretched, the cooling shelters and water points she planned are cut back. When the heatwave arrives, emergency spending takes even more of the budget, and prevention is cut again.

A composite, drawn from the evidence for this cycle. Not a real person. Money arranged before heat arrives is one way these two cycles could be eased.

Layers of shade

## The layers of protection

Money for a bad season can be held in layers, like canopies one above another. The ones closest to people carry the heat of most years; the higher ones open only for rarer, harder years. Each strip below is one scheme we documented: filled where heat is in its cover, open where it is not. Where the shade has holes, heat reaches people as lost pay and illness.

A picture of how layered protection is meant to work, drawn from the schemes we documented. It is not a measurement of the protection gap, which we could not put a reliable number on.

Kept apart, on purpose

## Three triggers, and two clocks

A warning, a decision to act and a contract that pays are different things, set by different people for different reasons. After a payout trigger, two more clocks run: until money reaches an institution, and until it reaches a person. A fast payment to a treasury can still be a slow one for a family.

- 1

### A warning

A weather service says dangerous heat is coming. It tells people to take care, and starts nothing on its own.

In Dhaka, readiness begins on a forecast 5–7 days ahead. [Source](https://bdrcs.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Heat-Wave-Feasibility-Study-Final-publish-version-07-September-2021.pdf) [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20250109112012/https://bdrcs.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Heat-Wave-Feasibility-Study-Final-publish-version-07-September-2021.pdf)

- 2

### An action trigger

A threshold agreed in advance gives permission, and money, to act: cash, water, cooling, outreach.

Dhaka’s Early Action Protocol: Temperature at least 38°C and heat index at least 38 for at least two consecutive days. First activated in April 2024. [Source](https://adore.ifrc.org/Download.aspx?FileId=724054) [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20260902010857/https://adore.ifrc.org/Download.aspx?FileId=724054)

- 3

### A payout trigger

A contract pays when a measured index crosses its line, whether or not a person was harmed.

A worker cover in Noida, India: Noida: 42°C for five consecutive days; additional ten-day tier reported. [Source](https://www.theinsurer.com/parametric-insurer/news/heatwave-triggers-insurance-payouts-to-indian-workers-2025-06-18/)

- 4

### Money reaches an institution

A ministry, agency or organisation receives the funds. This clock is the one most often reported.

A regional pool paid Lao PDR US$2 million 6 business days after receipt of latest government impact data, for floods rather than heat. [Source](https://seadrif.org/news/seadrif-insurance-company-makes-payout-of-us2-million-to-support-lao-pdr-government/) [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20260617043448/https://seadrif.org/news/seadrif-insurance-company-makes-payout-of-us2-million-to-support-lao-pdr-government/)

- 5

### Money reaches a person

The clock that matters most to someone losing a day’s pay, and the one we could least often see.

In Dhaka in 2024, households received BDT 5,000 each; the dates they received it are not in our sources. [Source](https://adore.ifrc.org/Download.aspx?FileId=724054) [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20260902010857/https://adore.ifrc.org/Download.aspx?FileId=724054)

### Ways to measure heat for a trigger

No single measure sees all of heat. Each one catches some harm and can miss another.

- **Air temperature**Humid heat: Poorly captured when used alone.

**What it does well** Simple, observable and supported by long weather histories.

**What it can miss** Misses humidity, radiation, wind, indoor heat and workload; station/grid may differ from exposure.

**Data in Asia** IMD daily temperature grids and national station services; local usable hourly coverage would ideally be audited.

**Used in** bd-dhaka-eap

- **WBGT - measured**Humid heat: Captures humid heat and radiant conditions; not a universal personal safety threshold.

**What it does well** Local natural wet-bulb, globe and dry-bulb sensors integrate humidity, wind and radiation.

**What it can miss** Sensor siting, calibration, maintenance and averaging matter; workload/clothing/individual response remain outside index.

**Data in Asia** Singapore publishes instrument-based WBGT and an API; comparable public coverage across all South/SE Asian locations not verified.

- **WBGT - modelled**Humid heat: Potentially captures humid/radiant heat if full method/inputs support it.

**What it does well** Enables spatial/historical estimates without a globe sensor at every location.

**What it can miss** Dependent on meteorological inputs, radiation/wind assumptions and validation; a simple temperature–humidity proxy is not direct measurement.

**Data in Asia** Reanalysis/forecast inputs available regionally; Asian worksite validation of any selected model would ideally be checked.

- **Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI)**Humid heat: Includes humidity but does not remove exposure and vulnerability mismatch.

**What it does well** Combines temperature, humidity, wind and radiation in a physiological reference model.

**What it can miss** Reference person/activity/clothing assumptions; grid and outdoor setting do not reproduce every person or indoor workplace.

**Data in Asia** ERA5-HEAT provides hourly 0.25° gridded estimates across South/SE Asia; modelled exposure, not a dense instrument network.

- **Satellite land-surface temperature (LST)**Humid heat: Poor direct capture of humidity; LST alone does not measure humid heat.

**What it does well** Spatially detailed view of surface heating and urban land-cover patterns.

**What it can miss** Skin temperature differs from shaded air or human heat stress; clouds, overpass time, emissivity and missingness matter.

**Data in Asia** MODIS daily 1 km day/night products cover region subject to quality/clear-sky availability; HotPay explores LST-enabled indicators.

- **Duration and night-time indicators**Humid heat: Persistence does not itself include humidity unless combined with a humid-heat metric.

**What it does well** Captures persistence and reduced recovery opportunities.

**What it can miss** Choice of window and baseline changes eligibility; day/night and time-zone definitions would ideally be fixed.

**Data in Asia** Daily/hourly weather histories and ERA5-HEAT night statistics available; local indoor recovery exposure still unknown.

**Used in** in-sewa-2024; bd-dhaka-eap

- **Excess mortality / heat-attributable mortality**Humid heat: Can model humidity jointly, but mortality is not a direct humidity metric.

**What it does well** Reveals harm beyond certified heatstroke; supports burden and fiscal-needs estimates.

**What it can miss** Lagged/incomplete registration, baseline and attribution uncertainty; post-event outcome cannot by itself fund pre-impact action.

**Data in Asia** India facility surveillance and city all-cause studies exist; timely complete national daily death data are not assumed.

- **Forecast probability**Humid heat: Depends on forecast variable; temperature-only probability misses humidity.

**What it does well** Can release funds before harm; expresses forecast uncertainty.

**What it can miss** Requires local calibration, reliability and decision-cost testing; a probability of above-normal seasonal heat is not a probability of the contracted event.

**Data in Asia** ECMWF/GFS inputs used by Dhaka; NOAA GEFS publishes heat-exceedance probabilities. No financial probability threshold inferred.

### How long the data takes

- **National stations and WIGOS quality monitoring**Operational, subject to reporting and transmissionNo uniform adequate station density assumed.
- **IMD gridded daily maximum temperature**Historical archive; not demonstrated as the real-time payout feedDocumentation says post-2008 grids use around 180 operationally received stations. This is a product-input count, not the total current Indian network.
- **ERA5 / ERA5T**ERA5T about five days; consolidated ERA5 about two–three monthsReanalysis is observation–model synthesis, not satellite LST or a local station reading. Settlement would ideally define preliminary/final version.
- **ERA5-HEAT**Intermediate daily updates; final monthly updates with two–three month lagSpatially complete grid does not establish complete worksite exposure measurement.
- **MODIS MOD11A1 v6.1 and proposed EO trigger services**Product-specific; distinguish archive, near-real-time and compositesDo not substitute LST degrees for air, WBGT or heat-index degrees.
- **Singapore WBGT instruments and API**Automatic publication; gaps and subsequent corrections possibleOctober 2026 ministry reply says around 30 stations, expanded from 15 in 2025; count is time-specific.
- **Operational forecast and ensemble products**Forecast release schedule; distinguish forecast lead from payment-processing timeGeneral product availability is not proof of local reliability at contracted threshold.
- **Health-facility surveillance and civil-registration mortality**Suspected reports early; confirmation and death registration laterFacility deaths miss deaths outside facilities; all-cause excess is not automatically attributable to heat.

Why heat is different

## Seven things that make heat hard to finance

- 1

### Health and income harm without damaged assets

**What has been tried.** SEWA income-oriented parametric cover; separate public/social benefits and heatstroke clinical benefits address other losses.

**Still open.** These mechanisms cover different needs. A clinical-event payment is not wages and a fixed benefit is not full expense indemnity.

- 2

### Uncounted or unattributed deaths

**What has been tried.** Reviewed operational heat examples use weather or forecasts rather than requiring a counted heat death.

**Still open.** This bypasses death-certification delay but does not make payouts track mortality. Mortality surveillance and excess-mortality analysis remain relevant for calibration; no operational excess-mortality heat payout trigger verified.

- 3

### Accumulation across consecutive days and nights

**What has been tried.** Multi-day triggers are used in SEWA and AXA designs; historical Pakistan DRF learning considered maximum and minimum temperatures.

**Still open.** Long consecutive-day requirements may miss intermittent severe exposure; hot-night-specific payment wording not verified for current examples.

- 4

### Humidity and occupational exposure

**What has been tried.** Start Network's Pakistan design describes a GFS-based heat-index model; Bangladesh activation references heat index alongside temperature.

**Still open.** Heat index is not WBGT. Neither automatically captures workload, radiant heat, clothing or indoor conditions. Exact current algorithms require review.

- 5

### Sparse stations and coarse exposure data

**What has been tried.** SEWA's first pilot used NASA POWER; provider examples also use official weather observations.

**Still open.** Gridded/reanalysis data are not local ground measurement or satellite land-surface temperature. Independent local validation, missing-data rules and a backup feed are proposed safeguards, not verified scheme features.

- 6

### Warning, action and payout are separate

**What has been tried.** Humanitarian EAPs pre-agree actions and finance; lower-threshold assistance can sit beside insurance.

**Still open.** Record forecast issue, trigger confirmation, release approval, institutional receipt and final receipt separately; an action lead time is not cash-in-hand speed.

- 7

### Forecast false alarms and missed harm

**What has been tried.** Start Network reports a historical Karachi activation where the predicted threshold event did not materialise; preventative activities continued.

**Still open.** False positives have costs; misses can leave harm unfunded. No universal acceptable error rate or independently measured basis-risk rate is established.

What changed for people

## What the evidence says about effect

Few of these arrangements have been studied for what they changed in people’s lives. Here is what has, each with how it was studied. Some come from outside Asia, or from other hazards, and are shown because they teach something about timing.

- Evaluated

### SEWA / HERA HERInsura insurance and cash-assistance bundle

Ten districts in Gujarat and Rajasthan, India · 2025 heat season; report available October 2026

Programme participants reported better outcomes on some measures. Self-rated health means were 3.44 versus 2.91 on a five-point scale (table headers n=577 and 434). This is an association, not a causal insurance effect.

**Limits.** No random allocation or individual baseline; geographic, occupational and other differences; self-report; commissioned evaluation; insurance cannot be separated from cash assistance. Inconsistent table denominators require clarification.

[Source](https://heranow.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/HERInsura-Pilot-Evaluation-Study.pdf)

- Measured

### MHT heat-risk-insurance pilot

Ahmedabad, Vadodara and Surat, India · 2024 pilot; report posted September 2026

Study documents awareness and payment experiences, with identification/bank matching and understanding of ERA5 versus local weather as practical issues.

**Limits.** No causal estimate of health, income, debt or work-rest effects. Paid subgroup differs from whole survey. Results cannot be extrapolated to all low-income workers.

[Source](https://www.mahilahousingtrust.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Impact-Study-CMSR-for-CRI-2024.pdf) [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20261008075129/https://www.mahilahousingtrust.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Impact-Study-CMSR-for-CRI-2024.pdf)

- Reported

### VimoSEWA heat and rainfall pilot documentation

India · December 2025 report on 2024 pilot

## What we know, what we infer, and what we are unsure of

### The instrument map

Heat finance is a portfolio of funding sources, contracts, authorisations and delivery channels. The 19 categories below distinguish actual heat applications from non-heat models and unverified heat concepts. Risk transfer covers a contracted liability; reserves and borrowing leave the underlying loss with the holder. Anticipatory action describes timing and pre-agreed action. Social protection describes a benefit system and delivery route. Premium support changes who pays for insurance. Results-based finance rewards verified performance and is usually not emergency liquidity. These dimensions overlap, so their money would ideally not be added as if each were an additional protection benefit.

**Known**: Verified heat applications exist at household, employer and humanitarian-programme levels.

**Inferred**: A useful Common Shade map would ideally let users distinguish funding source, recipient, release condition and delivery channel.

**Uncertain**: No issued sovereign heat policy, heat catastrophe bond or heat-health outcome-payment contract was verified in this review. This is a search finding, not evidence that none exists.

### Risk layering for heat

Start with a costed response plan, not an insurance product. Retain the cost of frequent needs through budgets and replenishable reserves. Use contingent credit for liquidity needs that justify borrowing and meet drawdown conditions. Consider insurance/reinsurance or capital-market transfer for financially severe, less frequent residual losses. These are tendencies, not fixed return-period boundaries. Timing overlays every layer: forecast releases can fund early action, while ready benefit systems, payroll and public services can deliver support. World Bank guidance describes this layered approach. The Philippines Cat DDO example demonstrates declaration-linked liquidity; SEADRIF demonstrates an impact-linked sovereign receipt. Neither reviewed example proves heat eligibility. For cities, borrowing authority, transfers from national government and procurement rules would ideally be checked separately. Heat's recurring income and clinical costs need routine funding even when no rare-event policy triggers. Prevention remains a recurrent public and employer responsibility. A warming climate also means pricing and attachment assumptions need review, rather than automatic extrapolation from historical loss frequencies.

**Known**: Different instruments can finance different severities and phases; source availability alone does not ensure delivery.

**Inferred**: For heat, the recurring budget and last-mile capability may be more foundational than an upper risk-transfer layer.

**Uncertain**: Optimal attachment points, reserve size, premium affordability and borrowing capacity require local data and an agreed response liability.

### Why heat needs different financing designs

Heat can create serious health and livelihood losses without destroying an asset. Harm can develop quickly within a prolonged hot period, and cumulative days and nights matter. Ambient temperature alone does not represent humidity, radiant load, work intensity or indoor exposure. Mortality under-reporting complicates both loss assessment and trigger calibration. These facts support a separation between public-health warnings, permission and resources to act, and contractual payout triggers. They do not establish that any one index is universally superior.

**Known**: Heat-health risk depends on exposure, duration and vulnerability; under-reporting and delayed delivery can obscure need.

**Inferred**: Different response costs may warrant different trigger families and a lower-threshold retained fallback.

**Uncertain**: The best local index, station/grid resolution, verification method and health-income calibration cannot be specified from a global map.

### What can be said about the heat protection gap

No verified Asia-wide estimate matching heat-specific economic losses to heat-specific insured compensation was found. The nearest auditable regional comparison here is Swiss Re sigma 1/2025, table 3: Asia's 2024 disaster-event economic losses were USD 66.5 billion and insured losses USD 11.1 billion. Subtracting the rounded figures gives approximately USD 55.4 billion uninsured, or 83.3%. This table includes natural and man-made disasters and is not a heat estimate. Its economic-loss definition emphasises direct damage and property-related business interruption; it excludes wider indirect earnings/GDP and non-economic losses, and its insured-loss definition excludes life and liability. The dataset covers 104 qualifying Asian disaster events, not a population sample. Source currency is USD, price year 2024, conversion in this report 1 USD = 1 USD. Underlying foreign-currency losses were converted by sigma using occurrence-year end exchange rates and inflation-adjusted as described in its methods. Event-level original currencies and rates were not reproduced in the regional aggregate. Kind: economic-loss estimate, insured-loss estimate and calculated uninsured-loss estimate-not premiums, limits or bank-confirmed payouts. No counterfactual baseline is applicable. These scope limits make the statistic an especially poor proxy for heat mortality, daily wages or informal work.

**Known**: A broad regional uninsured-loss comparison is available; its scope is explicitly wider in hazards and narrower in welfare losses than the question about heat.

**Inferred**: A heat-finance gap assessment would ideally separately measure unfunded response costs, uncovered income/health harm and delays in delivery.

**Uncertain**: Asia's heat-specific insured share and the amount reaching affected people before harmful coping remain unquantified here.

**Our own check** We have not yet been able to open the cited table and confirm these regional figures ourselves, so they are shown as the research reported them, with its caveats. They cover all disasters, not heat.

### Evidence and publication boundary

This is a targeted, source-linked landscape map checked on 31 October 2026, not an exhaustive systematic review or a contract-level due-diligence certification. Searches prioritised South and Southeast Asia, then Africa, Middle East and Latin America, with Hong Kong and Japan retained for specific transferable design lessons. English searches were supplemented by Japanese primary product documentation and French administrator leads. Missing primary access is recorded, not silently replaced by a claim of verification. Historical policy-year records are separated from current programme availability. Loop/lever mappings and possible causal pathways are analytical interpretations; they are not evidence that any scheme has changed a feedback loop. No comparative ranking was undertaken.

**Known**: Reported payment activity is not equivalent to an evaluated health or income effect. The J-PAL page describes a randomized encouragement evaluation design; reviewed material does not report its results.

**Inferred**: Publication would ideally distinguish existence, contractual design, actual cash receipt and measured effect as separate filters.

**Uncertain**: Current renewals, full trigger schedules, customer complaints, rejection rates and subgroup access remain incompletely documented.
