# Ledger: Oxfam America, "The unequal burden of extreme heat at work" (2 November 2026)

Source: https://politicsofpoverty.oxfamamerica.org/the-unequal-burden-of-extreme-heat-at-work/ (supplied by the owner with its text). An advocacy organisation's blog that reports its own programme work and others' research; the site attributes what it uses to Oxfam America. Batch: `data/inbox/2026-11-oxfam-heat-work/`.

## Used
A "Watch for" on the Heat-safe work lever (`heatrules`): protections work only when workers can use them.
- `c-vests`: Hong Kong, outdoor cleaners issued cooling vests and then prevented from wearing them because the vests obscured a company logo (Oxfam's own programme with local partners).
- `c-unions`: Cambodia, workers whose unions had bargained over heat exposure were better protected than those without (research the blog reports; the study itself is not named in the post).

## Held back, and why
- **ILO: more than 2.4 billion workers, about 70 percent, exposed to excessive heat.** An ILO figure; it belongs to the ILO's own report, cited directly, not through a blog.
- **Hong Kong's WBGT sensor network with smartwatch alerts**, and workers taking breaks when prompted: an Oxfam programme with a health technology company; worth a later check against a programme or partner page before it becomes an example.
- **Garment brands: about a third ask suppliers to measure temperatures** (a study by "Siers"): the study is not linked, so it is not used.
- **United States farmworkers (up to 35 times more likely to die of heat), Malawi sugarcane, La Isla Network in Latin America:** outside the region; La Isla's rest, shade and water findings would be an "Elsewhere" example cited to La Isla's own work, not this post.
- **Purchasing practices and supply-chain due diligence:** a direction for companies, not a finding; noted for a possible later lever or guide.
