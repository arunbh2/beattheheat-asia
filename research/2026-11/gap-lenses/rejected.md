# Tested and rejected, or not verified (gap lenses, November 2026)

Kept here, never on the site. Each lens’s own section I, as it came, followed by what the reconciliation set aside.

## Lens 1

- **Full cycle closure.** The map's complete edge sequences were not supplied. The evidence supports selected relationships, including the housing-sleep part of sleep, rather than a demonstrated self-reinforcing cycle. No cycle rating is upgraded.
- **Younger adolescents from the Indonesian study: rejected.** Its methods specify ages 19-24. The title alone could invite a wider age interpretation. Its cross-sectional design also leaves the direction between symptoms and protective behaviour unresolved. [S5]
- **A universal refugee heat-gastroenteritis link: rejected.** The two Bangladesh camps had different high-temperature associations. Their clinic records also incompletely represented young children. The paper does not justify transferring the Dhaka under-5 estimate to all displaced populations. [S13]
- **One maternal heat threshold for everyone: rejected.** The global review describes heterogeneous metrics and methods. Its estimated thresholds are not adopted as local safety limits. Postnatal health was excluded, so its results do not establish postpartum outcomes. [S3]
- **A general Indigenous heat-tolerance claim: rejected.** The Wardha abstract documents local practices, not comparative physiological protection or participant Indigenous identity. Full text is restricted. A separate physiology review uses tropical residency terminology; that is not evidence about Indigenous identity or immunity to heat. [S14]
- **People without secure housing: retained as a distinct group, with thin outcome evidence.** The Nagpur conference record confirms relevant research, but its abstract supplies limited detail. The Delhi report Surviving Heat on the Streets was located through its publishers; its PDF exceeded the reading service's size limit, so its numerical findings were excluded. [S17]
- **Employment formality: not inferred from migration.** The southern India abstract does not establish participants' formal employment status. Two candidate links from informal work were removed on that basis. [S8]
- **Caregiver exhaustion and unpaid-work losses: not added as quantified harms.** The opened regional evidence describes family assistance and disruptions in care. It does not establish caregiver-specific heat illness rates, time losses or the effectiveness of respite. [S6, S8]
- **Detention mortality and programme effectiveness: unverified.** The ICRC case study supports inclusion of the setting and documents an initiative. It does not verify heat-specific deaths or a measured reduction in harm. The original regional programme blog returned a gone response; a preserved ICRC account on PreventionWeb remained readable. [S12]
- **Regional postpartum effects: unresolved.** India guidance includes breastfeeding and infant-care counselling, but that is different from a measured postpartum heat-outcome study. No postpartum effect size is supplied. [S11]
- **Detailed author limitations behind abstracts: unavailable.** S2, S6, S8 and S17 were read through abstracts or repository summaries. Their records identify this review depth; no unseen methods or limitations were reconstructed. The older-person study also combines heat with cold and air pollution.
- **Other inaccessible leads.** A Nepal study of household cooking heat returned a publisher access error. Some child-health review pages returned reading challenges. They contributed no claims or numbers.
- **Group coverage remains uneven.** No Indigenous-specific or remote-community heat effect size was verified for this run. Disability findings do not represent every impairment or support need. Neither gap is evidence that relevant knowledge or protective practice is absent.

The next agreed lens is 3, slow and hidden harms, followed by 2, 5, 4 and 6. It can retain these group distinctions while testing longer-term outcomes.

## Lens 2

- Interaction versus overlap: simultaneous or successive hazards do not automatically produce effects greater than their separate effects. No regional mortality multiplier was calculated.

- Pakistan's 2022 sequence: the rainfall assessment identifies monsoon rainfall as the direct flood cause. It does not establish a spring-heat-to-monsoon-flood link. [S11]

- Heat and pollution: a universal co-peak rule was rejected. ADB's country and metric differences support local analysis; exposure correlation is not a health interaction estimate. [S5]

- Power outages: heat does not invariably produce outages. EVN reported continuous supply during April 2024 heat; this is a utility account, not an independent evaluation of individual measures. [S3]

- Mekong Delta: the UNICEF report documents drought and salinity, without heat measurements. Its counts were retained as water-component evidence, not heat-attributable cases. [S7]

- Partial access: the Thai mortality paper, Indonesian fire paper and 2023 Shisper study were reviewed through accessible abstracts. Their unavailable detailed methods were not inferred. [S4,S12,S14]

- Additional leads: the 2025 eastern India humid-heat/cyclone paper encountered a publisher access barrier. The World Bank's August 2023 Viet Nam report exceeded the reading tool's file-size limit, and its repository was inaccessible. No outage-cost estimate was imported.

- Outbreak guidance: the 2020 GHHIN brief was used for historical interaction pathways. Its detailed infection-control advice was not carried forward as current operational guidance. [S8]

- Protection outcomes: the cooling test measured comfort. The learning-centre and pond accounts were not controlled impact evaluations, and no harmful side effects were established. Empty unintended fields mean none documented in these sources, not proof of none. [S9,S10,S15]

- Conflict and coverage: no sufficiently verified regional heat-conflict interaction was established in this run. This is not an assertion that such evidence or risks are absent.

- Cycle closure: the supplied map lists cycle names but not their edge sequences. Links assigned to cycles are partial contributions; none is presented as closing a verified vicious cycle.

## Lens 3

- Child growth in Bangladesh: a primary study was located, but the full text could not be verified. Publisher access returned an error, and a repository download was rate-limited. No growth effect, number or new node was added.

- CKDu: the heat-only explanation was rejected as an addition. The Sri Lankan review and occupational comparison do not establish it. A single reduced kidney-function measurement also does not establish chronic disease. [S1,S3,S4]

- Learning and mental health: lower test scores and concurrent distress were retained as bounded findings. Permanent cognitive injury and lasting psychiatric illness were not established by the opened evidence. [S5,S9,S15]

- Debt and care: household accounts were retained as limited evidence. Heat-specific borrowing rates, multi-year repayment effects and quantified losses of unpaid-care productivity were not established. The HomeNet report combines climate pressures and pandemic-era interviews. [S10,S11]

- Modelled losses: the Indian annual-output scenario was labelled INFERRED. The IIED report's annual calculations were not treated as observed annual accounts; they depend on assumptions about heat days and inefficient working hours. [S6,S10]

- Intervention transfer: the Indian education paper was accessible as an abstract, with full text behind an access barrier. No detailed programme design was inferred. The Ahmedabad roof comparison did not measure sleep or long-term health, so that benefit remains a testable proposition. [S5,S13]

- Cycle closure: the supplied map lists cycle names but not their edges. Section C assigns candidate contributions to existing cycles; no link is presented as closing a verified cycle. Section E supports one leg only.

- Coverage: no claim of an evidence absence is made for older people, people with disabilities, migrants, detained people or postpartum women. This run did not establish their longer-term effects across the region. The next agreed run is lens 2, compound and cascading events.

- Access stability: the rural Tamil Nadu exposure paper was read in full, but a later automated check encountered a browser challenge. Its content was retained from the successfully opened version. [S12]

## Lens 4

- This is a targeted place screen, not a systematic review of every country or an exhaustive audit of every site file.
- The coverage table counts unique URLs in EVIDENCE.json whose where field explicitly names a country or the documented Jakarta alias. It excludes global studies without country tags and all other site sections.
- The mountain-is-safe and island-is-only-sea-level-risk framings were not retained. Country projections and local observations are kept distinct, rather than converted into unverified local mortality counts.
- No complete cycle is added in E. The proposed links concern exposure, modifiers and selected pathways; their lags are analytical suggestions.
- Direct humid-versus-dry field comparisons, small-town studies and outer-island clinical studies remain unverified in this run.
- For Pakistan, Myanmar and Malaysia, this run did not open a new country-specific primary study. Existing and regional evidence is not treated as absent.
- The South Asia systematic-mapping record and the Southeast Asia attribution review could not be read beyond access barriers. Their search-result counts were excluded.
- The final Lao workplace standard and its implementation were not verified. The March 2025 draft announcement is not described as current law.
- Where only an institutional abstract was accessible, the authors' full limitations section remains unverified.

## Lens 5

- Scope of the audit: selected candidate returns were tested, rather than every harm outside a cycle. The supplied count of 54 unconnected harms is not treated as a refreshed count.
- Forest return: the Indonesian experiment tested cleared land. It does not verify that heat-driven forest dieback produces the same exposure change. No new forest cycle is declared.
- Household return: the reviewed evidence did not establish illness or earnings loss followed by debt and later loss of cooling. Energy poverty remains emerging.
- Sleep return: the small laboratory null results narrow the existing sleepless-to-heatstroke claim. They do not show that sleep is irrelevant, and do not test both directions of the separate sleepless-mental-health cycle.
- Groundwater projections: the USDA technical abstract and indexed journal abstract give different future depletion multipliers. Neither multiplier is reproduced; the qualitative mechanism is retained.
- Access limits: the soil study's full text and several publisher texts could not be read. Institutional abstracts are identified in H. The Rajkot abstract was read successfully once; later access failed.
- Irrigation: a modelled water saving is not a measured reduction in total withdrawals after farmers change practice. No universal net-saving claim or field health benefit is added.
- Other nodes: this run did not verify return links for marine harms, tourism or elections. This is a limit of the reviewed evidence, not evidence that such links do not exist.

## Lens 6

- Cooling centres: the map's statement that they save lives in every heatwave is broader than the reviewed evidence. Proposed wording: public cooling spaces can offer relief; sustained health benefits depend on use and local conditions. [S2]
- Education: the Kerala cool-roof working paper was located but its PDF and institutional result pages could not be opened. Search-result effect sizes and attendance findings were excluded. The J-PAL page describes a pilot, not its outcomes.
- Singapore coatings: a university announcement was read, but the primary paper and preprint could not be opened. Its temperature and comfort numbers were excluded.
- Forest shade: the regional experiment is not an evaluated conservation programme and does not establish year-round wage benefits.
- Cold storage: provider-reported benefits are not independently evaluated producer-income effects. [S3]
- Tenant displacement, rent changes and unequal access were not measured by the retained intervention studies. No claim of no side effects is made.
- No full cycle is added in E. Several response mechanisms touch existing cycles, but the retained studies do not evaluate complete feedback closure.
- Additional worked examples remain unverified here for blackcarbon, firesmart, drypower, pumpbackup, farmcredit, freecare, coolnights, heat-specific mindheat and regional cooling-centre health outcomes.
- Soilwater, irrigate, socialprotection, safeathome and childwater have bounded examples in earlier runs. They are not counted again as new results.
- Four of seven sources concern regional settings, plus a global report with an Indian pilot. This is a targeted appraisal, not a comprehensive adaptation review.

## Set aside in the reconciliation (before checking sources)

- **New dots not added.** `localheat` (lenses 4 and 5, under different labels) overlaps urban heat islands and the centre of the map; its evidence goes to the harms it touches (drying soils, forest dieback, farmwork, productivity). `heatsymptoms` (lens 4) joins heatstroke and heat exhaustion. `adaptationstrain` (lens 6) is a documented side effect of one lever, so it becomes a watch-out on "Affordable cooling for the poorest". `detentionheat` (lens 1) joins the existing group "Migrants, prisoners and other excluded groups", because the evidence documents a programme, not a heat-specific outcome.
- **Relabels not adopted.** Existing harms keep their names, so the map, films and pages stay stable. Lens proposals such as "Lower heat supports output" or "Cooling needs continuous service" describe a condition or a lever, not a harm; their evidence is added to the harm's description instead.
- **Lever edges not moved.** Lens levers that reuse an existing id with a different edge become worked examples on the existing lever where they fit. Two did not fit and were set aside: the detention ventilation example (proposed for "Violence prevention in heat planning") and the East Kalimantan forest experiment (proposed for "Keep city trees alive", which is about city trees).
- **New lever held.** `foodcool` (pay-per-use cold storage) rests on one provider-reported pilot with unverified outcomes. It is kept as a learning question.
- **New at-risk groups not created.** Children, caregivers, young adults, displaced people and people in detention fit existing groups (mothers and young children; older people; people with disabilities or long-term illness; informal settlements; excluded groups).
- **Duplicate links.** Links already on the map are not added again; where a lens brings new evidence for one, the evidence goes to the harm or to the cycle it belongs to.
- **The lens 4 country coverage table.** A tagging audit of one file, not a literature count; on the site it could read as a ranking of countries, so it stays here.
