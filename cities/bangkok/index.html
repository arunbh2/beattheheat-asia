Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Bangkok

The poverty–heat trap

Nearly 80% of informal workers surveyed cut their hours in the heat; 75% of home-based workers lost a day’s work a month to heat illness.

People who can’t afford to stop working fall ill. Illness costs income, and lost income makes stopping even less affordable. Follow it on the map.

The reactive-spending loop

Heat deaths, about 593 a year, cost up to 0.7% of the city’s economy: more than its 2020 public health budget.

Stretched budgets skimp on prevention. Emergencies multiply, and costly emergency response stretches budgets further. Follow it on the map.

The sleepless-nights loop

One of the tropical cities whose nights stay hot.

Hot nights wreck sleep. Poor sleep frays mental health, and stress makes sleep harder still. Follow it on the map.

The green-displacement loop

Fieldwork is tracing how green urban renewal affects the city’s lower-income residents.

Green space follows wealth. When greening reaches poorer districts without protection, rents rise, residents are pushed into hotter housing, and the gap grows. Follow it on the map.

Who heat hits hardest here

  • Workers in heat-exposed jobs
  • People in informal settlements

Already at work here

  • HomeNet Thailand: With WIEGO, surveyed more than 1,000 informal workers in Bangkok on heat: nearly 80% had cut their hours. A trusted link between workers and authorities.
  • Bank of Thailand: A 2023 policy statement asks banks to build environmental and climate risk into governance, strategy, risk management and disclosure. It has worked with the World Bank on assessing physical climate risk in Thai banks’ loan books.
  • United Nations Development Programme (UNDP): Helps governments connect heat resilience with health systems, urban planning and finance. Its work includes a financing strategy for tackling urban heat in Bangkok.
  • Asian Development Bank: Supports heat resilience in Asian cities, including technical assistance on heat stress among women and girls in Bangladesh, Cambodia, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Tajikistan. Its 2026 report on the heat crisis in Thailand and Southeast Asia calls for mobilising public and private finance, and proposes a heat action plan for Thailand.
