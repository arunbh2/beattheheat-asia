How the estimate is made

Wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) is the heat-stress index used in international and national standards for heat at work.

The figure here is our estimate for the city as a whole, calculated from forecast air temperature and humidity with the Australian Bureau of Meteorology's simplified formula. That formula assumes fairly strong sun and light wind, and leaves out changes in sun and wind. In cloudy or windy weather, and at night or early in the morning, it is likely to read higher than a measured value.

It is not a measurement and not an official warning. No heat-stress level is shown beside the estimate: published WBGT levels are for measured values.

Researchers comparing methods recommend a fuller calculation for outdoor work, which also uses sun and wind; this page does not use it yet.

Sources: [Australian Bureau of Meteorology: Thermal comfort observations, about the approximation to the WBGT](http://www.bom.gov.au/info/thermal_stress/) · [Lemke and Kjellstrom, 2012: Calculating workplace WBGT from meteorological data, Industrial Health](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/indhealth/50/4/50_MS1352/_pdf) · [Weather data by Open-Meteo.com](https://open-meteo.com/)

## Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Bengaluru

### [The dying-green loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#deadgreen)

Bengaluru’s green cover fell from 68% in 1973 to about 6%, and more than 79% of its waterbodies have vanished or been encroached upon. Source: [Down To Earth, 2025](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/beyond-the-thermometer-bengalurus-race-to-modernise-ignored-climate-now-its-residents-are-paying-the-price)

Heat and drought kill city trees. Without their shade the streets get hotter, and hotter streets kill more trees. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-deadgreen).

### [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#clinic)

160 urban primary health centres serve 198 municipal wards: fewer than one per ward. Source: [Citizen Matters, 2025: Bengaluru failed to beat the heat](https://citizenmatters.in/bengaluru-failed-to-beat-the-heat-how-localised-approach-could-help-in-the-future/)

Heat illness floods clinics. Overwhelmed clinics treat people later, and more cases become severe. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-clinic).

## Already at work here

- [Hasiru Dala](https://hasirudala.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/HW-HD-report_final.pdf): Works with waste workers in Bengaluru to document heat-related health and income losses and press for water, shade, safer housing and workplace protection.
- [Bengaluru Sustainability Forum](https://www.bengalurusustainabilityforum.org): Supports a domestic-worker heat research and training project.
- [Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike / Bengaluru city government](https://bbmp.gov.in): Participates in the C40 Cool Cities Accelerator for heat protection and urban adaptation.
- [C40 Cities](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/bengaluru-joins-global-coalition-to-beat-extreme-heat-3786724): Its Cool Cities Accelerator brings together cities including Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, Mumbai and Singapore on early warnings, building standards and tree cover.
- [WRI India](https://india.mongabay.com/2025/04/safeguarding-indian-cities-from-heat-in-conversation-with-sudeshna-chatterjee-interview/): Works on heat mitigation planning with Indian cities, including greening and cooling pilots in Kochi and Mumbai and the Bengaluru Heat Collective.
