    Bengaluru: who is doing what about heat?

    Who is working on heat in the city, what exactly they are doing, what anyone can use, and what is still only planned. Built from public sources, each line checked against its source.

    Start here · Who is doing what · The Heat Collective · Work by theme · Tools and resources · Places and projects · Commitments and delivery · Organisations · What is not known · How this was made

    In July 2026 the city recorded its hottest July day on record. The response so far is a mix: a new coalition of research, worker and civic groups, ward climate plans with first budgets, a national heat pilot run through one city corporation, and seasonal cooling centres and kits for outdoor workers. Most of it is planned or in its first season; little has been evaluated.

    13 activities14 organisations6 tools and resources10 public bodies

    Organisations and activities identified in public sources, read between 24 and 28 November 2026. It is not a complete list of everyone working on heat in Bengaluru, and no organisation was contacted. Common Shade is not a member, partner or representative of the Bengaluru Heat Collective.

    Start here

    A city or ward team

    The Begur plan and its first budget are the most concrete ward-level starting point; the cooling kit team's proposal for Indira canteens is a ready list for heat alerts. First budget for a ward climate plan: Begur · Cooling kits for outdoor public workers

    A worker organisation or NGO

    The waste-worker survey and the apartment workers' charter give evidence and a practical text to build on; the cooling centres show what a season of relief took. How heat hits waste workers' health and income · A heat safety charter for apartment workers · Cooling centres for outdoor workers

    A researcher

    Nothing here has been evaluated. Measuring what the centres, kits and roof coatings change for workers' health and income is an open question.

    A funder

    The announced allocations have no published spending record yet; following them from announcement to delivery is itself useful work.

    Ward climate action plans in five pilot wards

    Plans that list climate projects ward by ward, built with public consultations in Shantinagar, Jakkur, Begur, Vijinapura and Nalwadi Krishnaraja Wadiyar. Heat is not one of their sectors, but heat maps informed them and Begur's plan includes heat proofing of buildings.

    Lead
    Greater Bengaluru Authority
    With
    WRI India (anchored the drafting (Shantinagar) and steered the enabling work (Begur)); Jan Sahas (working group: community engagement); Socratus (working group: community engagement); Hasiru Dala (working group); Sensing Local (working group); CSTEP (working group: anchors the energy sector); WELL Labs (working group: water); Bengaluru Central City Corporation (received the Shantinagar plan)
    Where
    Five pilot wards, one in each zone (Wards)
    People
    Residents, Informal workers, Waste workers
    When
    December 2025
    Outputs
    Public consultations held in all five pilot wards; Shantinagar's plan completed and submitted to the Climate Action Cell and Bengaluru Central City Corporation; A pipeline of 302 projects submitted to the South and North corporations for 2026-27

    Bengaluru Heat Collective

    The Bengaluru Heat Collective was launched in Bengaluru on 15 July 2026 by 11 organisations, as a platform to advance coordinated action on rising urban heat. Common Shade is not a member, partner or representative of the Bengaluru Heat Collective, and this page does not speak for it or its members.

    Launch event
    15 July 2026
    Announcements published
    17 July 2026 by WRI India; 17 July 2026 by KHPT
    Founding members
    ARTPARK, Bengaluru Sustainability Forum, C40 Cities, CSTEP, Hasiru Dala, HeatWatch, Jan Sahas, KHPT, NIMHANS, Socratus, WRI India

    Four priority areas

    Named for the Collective's initial phase. These are priorities; the sources do not report them as delivered work.

    1. Heat mapping, data and monitoring. In the launch release's words: "Understanding heat and its spatial dimensions to inform policy and investment". Related work
    2. Cooling infrastructure and the built environment. In the launch release's words: "Enabling infrastructure interventions". Related work
    3. Worker and community protection and health. In the launch release's words: "Strengthening relief and resilience measures for vulnerable communities". Related work
    4. Ward-level planning. In the launch release's words: "Advancing ward-level microplanning". Related work

    Governance, as far as the sources show

    • A WRI India partner update after the launch reports that the members agreed a Charter document for the group to function as a collective body, and that a Strategy Note on heat resilience in Bengaluru was developed as a living document. Neither document was found published.
    • A later partner update reports that the Collective convened in August 2026 to begin developing a theory of change and a strategic workplan.
    • No secretariat, lead organisation, workstream leads, legal status or public contact address for the Collective was found in the sources reviewed. Its charter and strategy note were not found published.

    At the launch

    • The launch notice described a panel on lived experiences of heat and a charter signing at Science Gallery Bengaluru.
    • At the launch, an ASHA worker, a domestic worker, a construction worker and a waste picker spoke about heat in their work.
    • Officials of Bengaluru North City Corporation, Bengaluru South City Corporation and NIUA's Climate Centre for Cities spoke at the launch.

    Followed step by step in Signals: from announcement to delivery

    What happened, and when

    Dated events, with plans kept apart from delivery. The date a source was checked is not a date the work moved.

    1. 2025 Event HeatWatch and Hasiru Dala publish their waste-worker heat survey. The report page shows no date; the PDF on Hasiru Dala's site sits in a folder dated August 2025.
    2. September 2025 Authority The Greater Bengaluru Authority's five city corporations come into being, after the BBMP.
    3. September 2025 Plan A domestic-worker heat project starts, supported through Bengaluru Sustainability Forum.
    4. 19 October 2025 Plan Deadline for C40's request for technical assistance on two high impact heat actions; work planned for November 2025 to March 2026.
    5. March 2026 Plan Ward climate plan consultations completed in five pilot wards; zone-wise budgets not yet released.
    6. April 2026 Delivery Cooling kits go to 202 outdoor public workers, mostly in North City Corporation.
    7. 10 April 2026 Delivery Three cooling centres for outdoor workers open; they close in the first week of July.
    8. 27 June 2026 Money Four of five corporations reported to have put about ₹52 crore towards climate action in 2026-27 budgets (not heat-specific).
    9. July 2026 Plan After the launch, members reported to have agreed a charter and a strategy note (neither found published).
    10. 14 July 2026 Event The city records 33.6°C, its hottest July day on record.
    11. 15 July 2026 Event The Bengaluru Heat Collective is launched by 11 organisations. North City Corporation describes ₹5 crore under the NIUA pilot and plans roof coating demonstrations; South City Corporation announces ₹2 crore for the Begur ward plan.
    12. August 2026 Plan The Collective reported to have begun a theory of change and strategic workplan.
    13. October 2026 Plan Shantinagar's ward climate plan is completed and submitted to the Climate Action Cell and Bengaluru Central City Corporation.

    Who has public authority to act

    Public bodies with responsibilities that touch heat in the city, under their current names. Older names are kept for older records.

    • Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA), since September 2025: City-wide authority over five city corporations (Bengaluru Central, North, South, East and West) since September 2025; its Climate Action Cell leads the ward climate plans.
    • Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP), until September 2025: The city's former municipal corporation, before the Greater Bengaluru Authority and its five corporations (September 2025). Its name is kept for older records, such as the 2025-26 climate earmark.
    • Bengaluru North City Corporation (BNCC, North City Corporation): Submitted the city's proposal under the national heat resilience pilot; plans roof coating demonstrations; most cooling kits went to its workers.
    • Bengaluru South City Corporation (BSCC, South City Corporation): Announced ₹2 crore for phased implementation of the Begur ward climate plan.
    • Bengaluru Central City Corporation (BCCC): Received the completed Shantinagar ward climate plan.
    • National Institute of Urban Affairs (Climate Centre for Cities) (NIUA): Runs the Building Heat Resilient Cities pilot under AMRUT 2.0 of the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, in which Bengaluru takes part.
    • Karnataka State Natural Disaster Monitoring Centre (KSNDMC): Named by ARTPARK as a partner in its heat-risk forecasts.
    • Karnataka State Disaster Management Authority (KSDMA): Named by ARTPARK as a partner in its heat-risk forecasts.
    • Department of Health, Government of Karnataka: Named by ARTPARK as a partner in its heat-risk forecasts.
    • India Meteorological Department (IMD): Runs the city observatory whose records the July 2026 temperature figures come from.

    What is not known

    Open questions, stated as gaps in what the sources show, not as failures of any organisation.

    • No published evaluation of any heat measure in Bengaluru was found: cooling centres, kits and plans are described by organisers or reporters.
    • Whether the ₹5 crore and ₹2 crore have been sanctioned, released or spent. Only statements at the launch were found.
    • Whether the planned roof coating demonstrations have been installed.
    • The Collective's charter, strategy note, workstreams and any lead or coordinator.
    • How many workers used Bengaluru's cooling centres; published figures are national.
    • Whether the ARTPARK forecasts are used by any authority, and their accuracy.
    • Where women, older and disabled people, and migrant workers shape decisions, as distinct from being consulted or receiving support.
    • The Karnataka Heat Action Plan 2026 could not be read for this profile (its server returned an error), so its arrangements for Greater Bengaluru are not described here.
    • Kannada-language sources were not searched systematically for this first version.

    Where sources disagree

    • Pilot wards: Mongabay India names five (one per zone); Citizen Matters, three months earlier, reported plans for ten wards across five corporations.
    • The ₹28 crore for climate action: described as allocated by the former body in one report and as earmarked for proposed plans in another.
    • Member names vary across sources: Socratus appears as Socratus, Socratus Corporation, Socratus Collective Wisdom Corporation and Socratus Foundation; Jan Sahas also as Jan Sahas Foundation and Jansahas.
    • KHPT describes itself joining ten organisations; WRI India counts eleven founding members including KHPT. These agree on the same eleven.

    How this was made

    Organisations and activities identified in public sources, read between 24 and 28 November 2026. It is not a complete list of everyone working on heat in Bengaluru, and no organisation was contacted. Common Shade is not a member, partner or representative of the Bengaluru Heat Collective.

    52 of 54 claims passed the source check, last run 28 November 2026: each was kept only when every word it rests on was found on its source. A source that only opened does not count. The date of the check is not a date any project moved.

    CSTEP's organisational mapping of heat actors was used to find leads; every entry used here was checked separately. The research matrix and the claims are published as files: research matrix · claims · confirmed claims.
