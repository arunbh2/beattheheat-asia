# Ahmedabad: who is doing what about heat?

Who is working on heat in the city, what exactly they are doing, what anyone can use, and what is still only planned. Built from public sources, each line checked against its source.

Ahmedabad wrote South Asia's first city heat action plan in 2013, after the 2010 heat wave, and an evaluation cited by the WHO Foundation credits it with lives saved. Since then the work has spread from warnings to homes, streets and income: cool roofs, a misted bus stop and a heat insurance for women in informal work, much of it carried by one women's housing organisation working with the city.

### Heat Action Plan with early warnings

The city's plan, first launched in 2013: an early warning system, awareness campaigns, and since 2017 cool roofs. Scroll reported that parks stay open for shade, signboards explain heatstroke and all departments meet weekly on the plan.

**Lead**: [Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation](#auth-amc)

**Where**: Ahmedabad (City-wide)

**People**: Residents

**When**: 2013

**Outputs**: An early warning system; Parks open from 6 am to 11 pm for shade (as reported in March 2025); 120 heatstroke signboards (as reported)

**Evaluation**: An evaluation by researchers credits the plan with 1,190 lives saved a year in its first five years; the evaluation itself was not read for this profile.

**Evidence**: Independent reporting; latest dated evidence March 2025

### Cool roof pilot on 3,000 low-income homes (2017)

Roofs of over 3,000 low-income homes painted with white lime paint under the Heat Action Plan, by city staff, student volunteers and civil society groups. In 2018 real estate developers were asked to apply cool roofs to private buildings voluntarily.

**Lead**: [Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation](#auth-amc)

**With**: [Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC), India programme](#org-nrdc) (partner); [Indian Institute of Public Health Gandhinagar](#org-iiphg) (partner); [Mahila Housing Trust](#org-mht) (partner)

**Where**: Low-income neighbourhoods (not named) (Neighbourhoods)

**People**: Low-income households

**When**: 2017 to 2018

**Outputs**: Over 3,000 roofs painted (NRDC's figure); 20 to 25 developers engaged (voluntary)

**Evaluation**: Not evaluated in the sources read.

**Evidence**: The organisation's own account; latest dated evidence April 2019

### Heat insurance for women in informal work

A parametric insurance that pays women a fixed sum when temperatures cross set thresholds, so a lost day's earnings is partly covered without a claim. Launched in April 2024; a payout was triggered in May 2024.

**Lead**: [Mahila Housing Trust](#org-mht)

**Where**: Ahmedabad (City-wide)

**People**: Women in informal work

**When**: April 2024

**Outputs**: Payouts triggered in May 2024

## What is not known

Open questions, stated as gaps in what the sources show, not as failures of any organisation.

- How many roofs have been coated city-wide since the 2017 pilot, and how many were maintained.
- The current edition of the Heat Action Plan was not read for this profile; its partners and budget are not described here.
- Whether the cool roofs, the misted bus stop or the insurance have been independently evaluated.
- Gujarati-language sources were not searched systematically for this first version.

### Where sources disagree

- Heat insurance terms: Down To Earth (June 2025) describes two thresholds of 43.7°C and 44.1°C, paying Rs 750 and a further Rs 1,250; the WHO Foundation (September 2024) describes one trigger of 44.2 ºC and Rs 2,000 a season. They may describe different seasons of the scheme.
