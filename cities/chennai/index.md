How the estimate is made

Wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) is the heat-stress index used in international and national standards for heat at work.

The figure here is our estimate for the city as a whole, calculated from forecast air temperature and humidity with the Australian Bureau of Meteorology's simplified formula. That formula assumes fairly strong sun and light wind, and leaves out changes in sun and wind. In cloudy or windy weather, and at night or early in the morning, it is likely to read higher than a measured value.

It is not a measurement and not an official warning. No heat-stress level is shown beside the estimate: published WBGT levels are for measured values.

Researchers comparing methods recommend a fuller calculation for outdoor work, which also uses sun and wind; this page does not use it yet.

Sources: [Australian Bureau of Meteorology: Thermal comfort observations, about the approximation to the WBGT](http://www.bom.gov.au/info/thermal_stress/) · [Lemke and Kjellstrom, 2012: Calculating workplace WBGT from meteorological data, Industrial Health](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/indhealth/50/4/50_MS1352/_pdf) · [Weather data by Open-Meteo.com](https://open-meteo.com/)

Each count is measured against this station's own record, so it is not a comparison between cities, and the station may sit at an airport or in open ground, so streets nearby can be hotter. Warm nights at a station can also rise as the city grows around it, which is part of the city's own heat. Humidity is not included, so these figures show temperature alone; in humid cities the heat felt in the body can rise even where temperature changes little. Strong El Niño years, such as 2016 and 2023 to 2024, bring hotter years in much of the region, and warm years near the end of a record tilt a trend upward.

## Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Chennai

### [The sleepless-nights loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#sleep)

In 50 low- and middle-income homes tracked from October 2025 to April 2026, indoor temperatures rarely fell below 31 °C and often stayed above 34 °C for hours after sunset. Source: [Down To Earth, 2026](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/new-study-sheds-light-on-indoor-nighttime-heat-retention-in-indian-homes)

Hot nights wreck sleep. Poor sleep frays mental health, and stress makes sleep harder still. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-sleep).

### [The overwhelmed-clinic loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#clinic)

For the 2026 hot season, government hospitals set up dedicated heatstroke wards with ICU beds, hydration counters and rapid cooling. Source: [The South First, 2026](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/how-chennai-is-racing-to-build-heatstroke-wards-as-temperature-soars/)

Heat illness floods clinics. Overwhelmed clinics treat people later, and more cases become severe. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-clinic).

### [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#poverty)

Across 11 districts of Tamil Nadu, 90.3% of the outdoor workers in a large study reported at least one symptom of heat illness. Source: [The South First, 2026](https://thesouthfirst.com/tamilnadu/work-slows-but-survival-cannot-extreme-heat-drives-tamil-nadus-outdoor-labourers-to-brink/)

People who can’t afford to stop working fall ill. Illness costs income, and lost income makes stopping even less affordable. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-poverty).
