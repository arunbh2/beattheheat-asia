## Heat action plan: Parts in place, no full plan found

In June 2026 the city government set up a City Heat Action Team and designated chief heat action officers. Through the C40 Cool Cities Accelerator it has committed to clear heat action leadership, a stronger early warning system and wider access to cooling during heat emergencies within two years. 32 air-conditioned community libraries serve as cooling centres.

Sources: [Quezon City Government, 2026: QC beefs up action vs extreme heat](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qc-beefs-up-action-vs-extreme-heat-designates-chief-heat-action-officers/) · [Zurich Climate Resilience Alliance, 2026: heat early warning in the Philippines](https://preparecenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/ZCRA_Philippines-HEWS-case-study.pdf) · [Quezon City Government, 2026: QCPL libraries as cooling centers](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qcpl-libraries-as-cooling-centers/)

How the estimate is made

Wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) is the heat-stress index used in international and national standards for heat at work.

The figure here is our estimate for the city as a whole, calculated from forecast air temperature and humidity with the Australian Bureau of Meteorology's simplified formula. That formula assumes fairly strong sun and light wind, and leaves out changes in sun and wind. In cloudy or windy weather, and at night or early in the morning, it is likely to read higher than a measured value.

It is not a measurement and not an official warning. No heat-stress level is shown beside the estimate: published WBGT levels are for measured values.

Researchers comparing methods recommend a fuller calculation for outdoor work, which also uses sun and wind; this page does not use it yet.

Sources: [Australian Bureau of Meteorology: Thermal comfort observations, about the approximation to the WBGT](http://www.bom.gov.au/info/thermal_stress/) · [Lemke and Kjellstrom, 2012: Calculating workplace WBGT from meteorological data, Industrial Health](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/indhealth/50/4/50_MS1352/_pdf) · [Weather data by Open-Meteo.com](https://open-meteo.com/)

## Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Quezon City

### [The energy-poverty trap](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#energypoverty)

Heat indices averaged 42 to 44 °C in 2024 and 2025, and extreme heat drives up electricity use that strains family budgets. Source: [Quezon City Government, 2026: QC beefs up action vs extreme heat](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qc-beefs-up-action-vs-extreme-heat-designates-chief-heat-action-officers/)

People who can’t afford to cool fall ill. Illness brings debt, and debt puts cooling even further out of reach. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energypoverty).

### [The energy–water loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#energywater)

Prolonged dry periods strain water access and raise the cost of essential goods and services. Source: [Quezon City Government, 2026: QC beefs up action vs extreme heat](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qc-beefs-up-action-vs-extreme-heat-designates-chief-heat-action-officers/)

Water systems need power and power plants need water. In a heatwave, each failure deepens the other. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-energywater).
