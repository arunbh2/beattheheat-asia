Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Colombo

The cooling trap

A University of Colombo study found a pronounced urban heat island, as green space gives way to concrete and asphalt.

Air conditioning heats the street, and a hotter city needs even more air conditioning. Follow it on the map.

The poverty–heat trap

Heat advisories ask people to work in the shade; market and fish workers who sell in the open often cannot.

People who can’t afford to stop working fall ill. Illness costs income, and lost income makes stopping even less affordable. Follow it on the map.

Who heat hits hardest here

  • Workers in heat-exposed jobs
  • Coastal and riverine communities

Already at work here

  • Asia-Pacific Network for Global Change Research (APN): Funds regional research and capacity building, including a US$40,000 grant for gender-sensitive heat action policies in South Asia and newer work on urban heat in Southeast Asian cities.
  • World Meteorological Organization: With WHO, runs the South Asia Climate–Health Desk with India’s meteorological agencies, turning weather and climate information into health protection.
  • UN ESCAP: Helps fast-growing cities in South and Southeast Asia plan for extreme urban heat, working with Iloilo City and Cox’s Bazar.
