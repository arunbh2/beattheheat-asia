How the estimate is made

Wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) is the heat-stress index used in international and national standards for heat at work.

The figure here is our estimate for the city as a whole, calculated from forecast air temperature and humidity with the Australian Bureau of Meteorology's simplified formula. That formula assumes fairly strong sun and light wind, and leaves out changes in sun and wind. In cloudy or windy weather, and at night or early in the morning, it is likely to read higher than a measured value.

It is not a measurement and not an official warning. No heat-stress level is shown beside the estimate: published WBGT levels are for measured values.

Researchers comparing methods recommend a fuller calculation for outdoor work, which also uses sun and wind; this page does not use it yet.

Sources: [Australian Bureau of Meteorology: Thermal comfort observations, about the approximation to the WBGT](http://www.bom.gov.au/info/thermal_stress/) · [Lemke and Kjellstrom, 2012: Calculating workplace WBGT from meteorological data, Industrial Health](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/indhealth/50/4/50_MS1352/_pdf) · [Weather data by Open-Meteo.com](https://open-meteo.com/)

Each count is measured against this station's own record, so it is not a comparison between cities, and the station may sit at an airport or in open ground, so streets nearby can be hotter. Warm nights at a station can also rise as the city grows around it, which is part of the city's own heat. Humidity is not included, so these figures show temperature alone; in humid cities the heat felt in the body can rise even where temperature changes little. Strong El Niño years, such as 2016 and 2023 to 2024, bring hotter years in much of the region, and warm years near the end of a record tilt a trend upward.

Trend: Sen's slope with its 95% range, and a Mann-Kendall test: "a clear rise" where the test is under 5%, "leaning upward, not clear" between 5% and 20%, otherwise "no clear change". ## Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Delhi

### [The heat credit squeeze](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#squeeze)

Of 721 Delhi street vendors surveyed in 2024, 80% saw fewer customers in heatwaves and 49% lost income. Source: [Greenpeace India and National Hawkers Federation, 2024](https://www.greenpeace.org/india/en/press/16737/new-report-by-greenpeace-india-and-national-hawkers-federation-reveals-harsh-heatwave-toll-on-street-vendors/)

Heat empties shops and small firms miss repayments. Banks tighten credit, so firms can’t afford to adapt, and the next heatwave hits harder. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-squeeze).

### [The dying-green loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#deadgreen)

New Delhi is among the cities where every urban tree species studied is at risk from rising heat. Source: [Esperon-Rodriguez et al., Nature Climate Change, 2022](https://www.vibrantcitieslab.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/climate-change-increases-global-risk-to-urban-forests.pdf)

Heat and drought kill city trees. Without their shade the streets get hotter, and hotter streets kill more trees. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-deadgreen).

### [The groundwater loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#pumping)

North-west India (Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana) lost 109 km³ of groundwater in six years, mainly to irrigation. Source: [Rodell, Velicogna and Famiglietti, Nature, 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08238)

Dry fields need more water, so farmers pump harder, aquifers fall and the shortage deepens. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-pumping).

### [The invisible-deaths loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#invisible)

India confirmed 185 heatstroke deaths against 48,385 suspected cases in 2024, up to 28 July. Source: [Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, reply in India’s Rajya Sabha, 6 August 2024](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/750681/1/PQ_265_06082024_U1693_p358_p362.pdf)

Heat deaths go uncounted, so heat protection goes unfunded, so more people die uncounted. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-invisible).

## Already at work here

- [National Disaster Management Authority, India](https://ndma.gov.in): Issues national guidance for state and city heat action plans.
- [India Meteorological Department and IITM Pune](https://wmo.int/node/38332): Issue India’s heatwave forecasts and work with WHO and WMO on the South Asia Climate–Health Desk.
- [National Centre for Disease Control, India](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/includes/About/CentresAndDivision/CEOH.php): Leads heat-health guidance, training and surveillance through India’s National Programme on Climate Change and Human Health.
- [Council on Energy, Environment and Water (CEEW)](https://www.ceew.in/publications?field_focus_area_tid=All&field_pub_type_tid=All&field_authors_target_id_selective=All&title=heat): Assessed heat risk in all 734 districts of India in 2025, finding 57% of districts, home to three in four Indians, at high or very high risk. Its 2026 heat-health reports for the GHHIN South Asia hub cover India, Nepal, Bangladesh and Bhutan, and it helped Thane build its heat action plan.
- [SEEDS](https://www.seedsindia.org/portfolio/partnering-to-strengthen-climate-resilience/): Combines building-level heat risk mapping, community preparedness and affordable cool roofs for vulnerable urban settlements in India.
- [HeatWatch India](https://cooling-centres.heatwatch.in/about): Focuses on heat and worker protection through research, heat exposure mapping, practical education and a public directory of cooling centres.
- [Centre for Science and Environment (CSE)](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/no-time-and-money-to-cool-delhis-vulnerable-seek-priority-relief-from-rising-heat): Analyses urban heat stress ward by ward: in Delhi it found almost 76 per cent of the city facing heat stress in summer (2015 to 2024), and it has mapped heat zones in Kolkata.
- [Women in Informal Employment: Globalizing and Organizing (WIEGO)](https://www.wiego.org/research-library-publications/working-heat-street-vendors-delhi-india/): Documents how heat affects informal workers’ health and earnings, and develops worker education, policy recommendations and protection guidance with local organisations.
- [Greenpeace India](https://www.greenpeace.org/india/en/press/16737/new-report-by-greenpeace-india-and-national-hawkers-federation-reveals-harsh-heatwave-toll-on-street-vendors/): Surveyed over 700 Delhi street vendors on heatwaves with the National Hawkers Federation, and asked the Delhi Heat Action Plan's authority to consult outdoor workers.
- [National Hawkers Federation](https://www.greenpeace.org/india/en/press/16737/new-report-by-greenpeace-india-and-national-hawkers-federation-reveals-harsh-heatwave-toll-on-street-vendors/): A street vendors' federation that collected Delhi vendors' heatwave experiences with Greenpeace India.
- [Indo-Global Social Service Society (IGSSS)](https://india.mongabay.com/2025/01/when-heat-rises-incomes-fall-for-the-informal-workforce/): Fitted thirty Delhi homes with wall panels in a pilot from April 2022 to March 2023.
- [Mahila Housing Trust](https://citizenmatters.in/new-cool-roof-solutions-heat-in-informal-settlements/): Cools homes in informal settlements with reflective and modular roofs, and runs heat income insurance for women workers.
- [Self Employed Women’s Association (SEWA)](https://www.swissre.com/our-business/public-sector-solutions/insights/parametric-structure-for-sewa-in-south-east-asia.html): Protects women informal workers’ income in heatwaves. After the May 2024 heatwaves, more than 46,000 members received insurance payouts.

Listing does not imply partnership, affiliation or endorsement.
