Extreme heat in Singapore: plans, cycles and who is at work

A city-state with hot nights, dense towers and an early national heat stress advisory. Heat plan: heat plan in force.

Open the interactive map

Heat type: Humid heat, all year round. Dry heat and humid heat, explained.

Heat action plan: Heat plan in force

Singapore launched a national Heat Stress Advisory in July 2023. It rates heat stress as low, moderate or high from the wet-bulb globe temperature, and tells people what to do at each level.

Sources: Ministry of Sustainability and the Environment, 2023

Heat today

Live readings and the week’s forecast are on the interactive map. Official warnings: Meteorological Service Singapore. How much climate change shaped today’s heat: Climate Shift Index map, Climate Central.

Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Singapore

The sleepless-nights loop

Singapore has averaged 76 warm nights a year over four decades; by 2100 they could come on most nights.

Hot nights wreck sleep. Poor sleep frays mental health, and stress makes sleep harder still. Follow it on the map.

The cooling trap

The commercial centre has measured up to about 7 °C warmer than the outskirts on calm, clear nights.

Air conditioning heats the street, and a hotter city needs even more air conditioning. Follow it on the map.

The dying-green loop

Among the cities where the most urban tree species already face temperatures beyond their range.

Heat and drought kill city trees. Without their shade the streets get hotter, and hotter streets kill more trees. Follow it on the map.

The invisible-deaths loop

Workplace monitors found more heat stress than nearby weather stations reported.

Heat deaths go uncounted, so heat protection goes unfunded, so more people die uncounted. Follow it on the map.

A worked plan to learn from

Singapore’s heat rules for outdoor work (Singapore, 2023 onwards)

Already at work here

  • National Environment Agency, Singapore: Issues public heat stress advisories through myENV and weather services, so people can adjust outdoor activity as heat rises.
  • Ministry of Manpower, Singapore: Requires hourly wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) checks on outdoor worksites, shaded rest breaks when heat stress is high, and time for new workers to acclimatise, and enforces the rules.
  • Singapore Management University, Urban Institute: Studies urban heat vulnerability and cooling through the Cooling Singapore collaboration, linking neighbourhood conditions to climate-sensitive planning.
  • National Research Foundation, Singapore: Funds Project HeatSafe, which studies heat, health and productivity among workers in Singapore and partner countries including Vietnam and Cambodia, and Cooling Singapore.
  • Monetary Authority of Singapore: Its 2020 environmental risk guidelines expect banks to assess physical climate risk, including through scenario analysis and stress tests. Its Singapore-Asia Taxonomy (2023) covers emission cuts only, so there are no criteria yet for finance that helps people adapt to heat.
  • C40 Cities: Its Cool Cities Accelerator brings together cities including Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, Mumbai and Singapore on early warnings, building standards and tree cover.
