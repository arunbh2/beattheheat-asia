How the estimate is made

Wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) is the heat-stress index used in international and national standards for heat at work.

The figure here is our estimate for the city as a whole, calculated from forecast air temperature and humidity with the Australian Bureau of Meteorology's simplified formula. That formula assumes fairly strong sun and light wind, and leaves out changes in sun and wind. In cloudy or windy weather, and at night or early in the morning, it is likely to read higher than a measured value.

It is not a measurement and not an official warning. No heat-stress level is shown beside the estimate: published WBGT levels are for measured values.

Researchers comparing methods recommend a fuller calculation for outdoor work, which also uses sun and wind; this page does not use it yet.

Sources: [Australian Bureau of Meteorology: Thermal comfort observations, about the approximation to the WBGT](http://www.bom.gov.au/info/thermal_stress/) · [Lemke and Kjellstrom, 2012: Calculating workplace WBGT from meteorological data, Industrial Health](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/indhealth/50/4/50_MS1352/_pdf) · [Weather data by Open-Meteo.com](https://open-meteo.com/)

A single station shows how one site has changed against its own past. Each count is measured against this station's own record, so it is not a comparison between cities, and the station may sit at an airport or in open ground, so streets nearby can be hotter. Warm nights at a station can also rise as the city grows around it, which is part of the city's own heat. Humidity is not included, so these figures show temperature alone; in humid cities the heat felt in the body can rise even where temperature changes little. Strong El Niño years, such as 2016 and 2023 to 2024, bring hotter years in much of the region, and warm years near the end of a record tilt a trend upward.

Trend: Sen's slope with its 95% range, and a Mann-Kendall test: "a clear rise" where the test is under 5%, "leaning upward, not clear" between 5% and 20%, otherwise "no clear change". Index definitions follow the ETCCDI (TN90p, TX90p, TXx), counted against this city's whole record. A change at the station is checked by comparing its yearly mean maximum and minimum with the regional model: for daily maxima, a step of at least 0.5 °C (Pettitt's test under 1%) means hot days and the hottest day show no trend; for minima, the same step (test under 5%) withdraws warm nights only where it fits clearly better than a steady drift, because nights warm gradually as a city grows. ## Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Jakarta

### [The global cooling loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#global)

Half of Southeast Asia’s projected 300 million air conditioners in 2040 could be in Indonesia. Source: [International Energy Agency, 2019](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-cooling-in-southeast-asia)

Cooling powered by fossil fuels adds to the warming that makes us need more cooling. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-global).

### [The wildfire loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#fire)

Indonesia’s 2015 forest and peat fires cost an estimated US$16.1 billion. Source: [World Bank, 2016](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/776101467990969768/pdf/103668-BRI-Cost-of-Fires-Knowledge-Note-PUBLIC-ADD-NEW-SERIES-Indonesia-Sustainable-Landscapes-Knowledge-Note.pdf)

Heat dries forests into fuel. Fires release carbon and strip away the trees that cool the land. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-fire).

### [The dying-green loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#deadgreen)

Among the hot, fast-growing cities where urban trees are at risk. Source: [Esperon-Rodriguez et al., Nature Climate Change, 2022](https://www.vibrantcitieslab.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/climate-change-increases-global-risk-to-urban-forests.pdf)

Heat and drought kill city trees. Without their shade the streets get hotter, and hotter streets kill more trees. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-deadgreen).

## Already at work here

- [Otoritas Jasa Keuangan (Financial Services Authority), Indonesia](https://ojk.go.id/en/Publikasi/Roadmap-dan-Pedoman/Perbankan/Pages/The-2026-Banking-Climate-Risk-Management-and-Scenario-Analysis-CRMS-Guideline.aspx): Its climate risk guideline for banks (2024, refined in 2026) includes data on Indonesia’s physical climate hazards. A 2022 rule lets banks restructure loans for borrowers in areas it designates as disaster-hit: after the 2025 Sumatra floods, Rp12.58 trillion for 237,083 borrowers. Heat has not yet been designated.
- [KOMIDA, with Build Change (Indonesia)](https://www.globalinnovation.fund/investments/build-change-2): One of Indonesia’s largest microfinance institutions, piloting small home-improvement loans for climate adaptation with Build Change, aimed at women earning under US$5 a day and paired with digital advice.
- [Nusantara Fund (Dana Nusantara)](https://nusantarafund.org/en/program/mosintuwu-community/): Funds Indigenous peoples and local communities in Indonesia directly, such as protecting water sources in Central Sulawesi. Heat-specific funding is not confirmed.
