Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Jakarta

The global cooling loop

Half of Southeast Asia’s projected 300 million air conditioners in 2040 could be in Indonesia.

Cooling powered by fossil fuels adds to the warming that makes us need more cooling. Follow it on the map.

The wildfire loop

Indonesia’s 2015 forest and peat fires cost an estimated US$16.1 billion.

Heat dries forests into fuel. Fires release carbon and strip away the trees that cool the land. Follow it on the map.

The dying-green loop

Among the hot, fast-growing cities where urban trees are at risk.

Heat and drought kill city trees. Without their shade the streets get hotter, and hotter streets kill more trees. Follow it on the map.

Already at work here

  • Otoritas Jasa Keuangan (Financial Services Authority), Indonesia: Its climate risk guideline for banks (2024, refined in 2026) includes data on Indonesia’s physical climate hazards. A 2022 rule lets banks restructure loans for borrowers in areas it designates as disaster-hit: after the 2025 Sumatra floods, Rp12.58 trillion for 237,083 borrowers. Heat has not yet been designated.
  • KOMIDA, with Build Change (Indonesia): One of Indonesia’s largest microfinance institutions, piloting small home-improvement loans for climate adaptation with Build Change, aimed at women earning under US$5 a day and paired with digital advice.
  • Nusantara Fund (Dana Nusantara): Funds Indigenous peoples and local communities in Indonesia directly, such as protecting water sources in Central Sulawesi. Heat-specific funding is not confirmed.
