## Vicious cycles the evidence ties to Phnom Penh

### [The cooling trap](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#cooling)

UN ESCAP projects that the urban heat island could add 2 °C to 7 °C on top of global warming in Phnom Penh. Source: [UN ESCAP, Asia-Pacific Disaster Report 2025, reported by Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/?p=241501)

Air conditioning heats the street, and a hotter city needs even more air conditioning. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-cooling).

### [The poverty–heat trap](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#poverty)

At three garment factories, workers reporting heat symptoms rose from 68% in the cooler months to 88% in the hot months. Source: [Chea et al., BMC Public Health, 2025](https://bmcpublichealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12889-025-22787-0)

People who can’t afford to stop working fall ill. Illness costs income, and lost income makes stopping even less affordable. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-poverty).

### [The lost-schooling loop](https://beattheheat.asia/loops/#school)

In the April 2024 heatwave, Cambodia shortened public school days by two hours. Source: [NBC News, 2024](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/schools-close-extreme-heat-philippines-bangladesh-asia-rcna149485)

Children pulled out of school earn less as adults, and poorer families pull more children out of school. [Follow it on the map](https://beattheheat.asia/#loop-school).

## Already at work here

- [UN ESCAP](https://unescap.org/our-work/environment-development/cities-for-a-sustainable-future/addressing-urban-extreme-heat): Helps fast-growing cities in South and Southeast Asia plan for extreme urban heat, working with Iloilo City and Cox’s Bazar.
- [Asian Development Bank](https://www.preventionweb.net/publication/beating-heat-investing-pro-poor-solutions-urban-resilience): Supports heat resilience in Asian cities, including technical assistance on heat stress among women and girls in Bangladesh, Cambodia, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Tajikistan. Its 2026 report on the heat crisis in Thailand and Southeast Asia calls for mobilising public and private finance, and proposes a heat action plan for Thailand.
- [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/region/sar/publication/a-livable-future-protecting-jobs-and-growth-from-extreme-heat-in-south-asia-s-cities): Its 2026 report A Livable Future finds extreme heat already costs South Asia the equivalent of about 31 million full-time jobs a year, and sets out how cities can protect jobs and growth.
