# How Common Shade sees heat

A shared map for everyone working to protect people from extreme heat in South and Southeast Asia. These are the ideas it is built on, and the standards it holds itself to.

## Six ideas

- 1

### Heat is a system, not a single hazard

One hot night costs sleep; lost sleep costs a shift; a lost shift costs a repayment. The map follows these links, so protection can be aimed where a cycle breaks.

[The map](https://beattheheat.asia/#explore)

- 2

### Protection is held up together

A shaded rest point needs land, water, someone to open it and money to keep it going, and each belongs to a different body. Protection reaches people only when every part holds.

[Make it work together](https://beattheheat.asia/together/)

- 3

### The missing middle

The damage of heat is widely reported. The question asked less often is how protection is organised, paid for and kept going: who pays, who decides, who checks.

[How heat is reported](https://beattheheat.asia/how-heat-is-reported/)

- 4

### Known, inferred, uncertain

Every claim says what kind of evidence stands behind it. Known means measured in a study, or set out in an official record; models and projections are inferred; reports not yet confirmed are uncertain. An official record can show that a rule, plan or scheme exists and what it promises. Whether it was carried out, or worked, takes an evaluation or an independent report: where none is cited, read the claim as saying only that it exists.

[Sources and references](https://beattheheat.asia/sources/)

- 5

### Built together, open to correction

Common Shade is built with the people who use it. When something here is wrong, unfair or unclear, we say sorry, correct it in the open and keep a record of what changed.

[Corrections](https://beattheheat.asia/corrections/)

- 6

### A gap is a task to solve together

Where the research found nothing, the site says so plainly. A gap describes what we found, never a fault of any country, city or organisation.

[The heat network](https://beattheheat.asia/heat-network/)

## How we check facts

- **Every claim is sourced**Each figure links to the publication it comes from, and new sources keep an archived copy, so a claim can be checked even if a page moves.
- **Evidence is labelled honestly**Projections are never presented as facts. Where reports differ, the site shows both and explains why.
- **Four things kept apart**What kind of source a claim rests on (a study, an official record, a news report), how the work was done, what it found, and how sure we are of it. A strong source can carry a modest finding, and a rule on paper is not yet a result.
- **A careful, diplomatic voice**No blame, no political positions, no ranking of countries, cities or organisations, and no individuals named in the site’s own text.
- **A humble voice**The site describes what helps, what it depends on and who could act. It does not instruct anyone: the people closest to the heat know their places best.
- **Listing is not endorsement**Organisations, businesses and funders appear because of their published work, not as partners, and never in order of merit.
- **Checked before publishing**New research passes seven checks before it appears: structure, sourcing, evidence, tone, a reviewer’s note, consistency with what is already published, and a test of every page.
- **Kept current, with history**The knowledge base is updated monthly. Older figures are marked as superseded, not deleted, so changes can be traced.

## How AI is used on Common Shade

Common Shade is built with the help of AI. This is what it does here, what it is never allowed to do, and who checks its work.

- **Builds and maintains the site**An AI coding assistant (Claude Code, made by Anthropic) writes most of the scripts that build the pages, the page code and the checks, working in the site's open repository. Every change to pages and data goes through a pull request that has to pass the site's automated checks. The site owner decides what is worked on and approves what is published; the assistant merges on that approval.
- **Researches and drafts**It searches for sources, reads documents the site owner shares, and drafts page text and data records from them. Drafts are written in the site's own careful voice, and every factual line names its source.
- **Prepares the checks, but does not do the checking**Sources are checked by plain scripts on GitHub, not by AI: they open each page or PDF and look for the exact words a claim rests on. A claim whose words are not found is dropped and listed, never published. Each research set keeps its dropped list.
- **Gives the films a voice**Narration uses a synthetic voice (Piper, with a LibriTTS voice under CC BY 4.0). Films made with the site's own renderer draw every scene in code, with faceless figures. Earlier background footage generated with AI was replaced by drawings made in code.

### What AI is never allowed to do here

- Invent a statistic, study, quote, link or source.
- Present an illustrative number as measured, or a projection as a fact.
- Create a person: no invented names, quotes or faces, and no synthetic people standing in for real ones.
- Publish without the source check and the site owner's approval.
- Rank countries, cities or organisations.
- Run on the site for visitors: there is no chatbot, and nothing a visitor types or chooses is sent to an AI service.

AI can still be wrong. The source check confirms that a source contains the words a claim rests on, not that the claim is the best reading of it. That is why every claim links to its source, evidence is labelled known, inferred or uncertain, and corrections are made in the open.
