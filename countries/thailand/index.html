Heat context

Climate: Official guidance or data Monsoonal climate: hot-humid exposure especially Bangkok/southern lowlands, with seasonally drier intense heat inland; evaluate humidity by season and time rather than treating Thailand as one cooling climate. Plans

  • Official guidance or data WHO2026 profile lists Health National Adaptation Plan Phase1(2021–2030) as approved and under revision. It marks a heat-health plan present for2025 while its note describes further national plan development as forthcoming. Final standalone HHAP text, adoption date and legal instrument not verified. WHO Thailand climate-and-health country profile (opens in a new tab) ↗
  • Reported, not evaluated Bangkok2026 urban-heat response is reported, but February BMA account describes submitting a draft for approval; October UNDP strategy references the urban-heat plan. Signed adoption instrument and final annexes not retrieved. BMA urban-heat preparation and warning levels (opens in a new tab) ↗ · Bangkok heat-financing strategy (opens in a new tab) ↗

  • Official guidance or data ADB2026 suggests a national HAP roadmap; an external recommended roadmap is not an adopted government obligation. ADB Thailand heat policy and solutions report (opens in a new tab) ↗

Rules for heat at work

Legal basis: Official guidance or data Ministerial Regulation on occupational heat/light/noise B.E.2559(2016), under the Occupational Safety, Health and Environment Act B.E.2554(2011), published17October2016. Current labour authority provides text and measurement notifications; full later-amendment audit not completed. Thai heat/light/noise occupational regulation (opens in a new tab) ↗ · Department of Labour Protection and Welfare legal resource page (opens in a new tab) ↗ Limits:
  • Workload: light Value: 34 Unit: °C WBGT
  • Workload: moderate Value: 32 Unit: °C WBGT
  • Workload: heavy Value: 30 Unit: °C WBGT
 Measurement: Law defines average WBGT during the hottest two hours of normal work, with workload classes; obtain qualified assessment for the specific task. Legal controls: Warning signs, engineering correction and documentation; workload controls where engineering cannot meet standard. Limit sources: Thai heat/light/noise occupational regulation (opens in a new tab) ↗ Caveat: These are statutory WBGT limits, not a universal ambient-temperature stop-work rule, proof of universal physiological safety or automatic entitlement for all self-employed workers. Paid rest and enforcement must be verified locally. Research: Measured in a study Thai observational construction/foundry study found heat strain among some workers even under compliant conditions; do not interpret compliance as a guarantee. Occupational heat standard and worker strain study (opens in a new tab) ↗

Gaps in what we found

  • No consistent national city exposure ranking verified.
  • Signed final heat-plan adoption instruments and annexes not retrieved.
  • Comparable indoor/night heat, worker income, migrant/disability access, centre opening/access and recurrent budgets insufficient in collected evidence.
  • Nationwide heat-triggered cash/insurance benefits, current grant calls and city unit costs not verified.

Ways to help (proposed)

  • Worker-informed warning-to-action protocol with HomeNet/WIEGO and relevant city/employer counterparts.
  • Test cooling-centre accessibility, actual hours, power/water and night coverage with BMA.
  • Support costed municipal portfolios and public-service maintenance arrangements.
  • Seek qualified worker heat measurement and operating changes alongside compliance.

Known, inferred, uncertain

Known: Official warnings, employer regulation, Bangkok services and research partnerships documented. Inferred: Common Shade can connect them through practical accountability and operating finance. Uncertain: Final adoption, local reach, programme outcomes and current applicant eligibility.
