How to read this. Facts link to their sources; anything marked proposed or inferred is the researchers’ suggestion, not a commitment by anyone named. Gaps are gaps in what we found, not faults.

Heat context

Known: Western/southwestern daytime hotspots coexist with humid urban and night exposure. Inferred: Combine work, housing and overnight recovery with warning-to-action design. Uncertain: Comparable ward-level exposure, health burden and nationally representative indoor monitoring. Are hotspots and frequencies of heat waves changing over time? Dhaka

Heat context: Humid heat and night recovery concern; monitored factory/home exposures complement grid/station data.

Heat plan: No consolidated currently approved DNCC/DSCC heat plan retrieved. National guideline, health planning and project pilots are different instruments.

Warnings: Bangladesh Meteorological Department heat-wave warning portal (opens in a new tab) ↗ Note: Current city warning-to-action agreement and coverage not verified.

Local finance: Study estimates and reported government health-pilot channel, not verified city heat expenditure or household loan product.

Gaps in what we found: Separate Dhaka North/South responsibilities and peri-urban factory locations; current facilities, budgets and wage-protected rest not verified.

A place to start (proposed): Worker-led work-to-home protection package with pay, retrofit maintenance and night monitoring.

Selection basis: Large-city humid/night exposure and original worker/home evidence.

Geographic caution: Station, grid, district and municipal boundaries must not be treated as interchangeable.
